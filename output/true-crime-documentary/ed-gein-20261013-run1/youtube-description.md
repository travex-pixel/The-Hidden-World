Plainfield, Wisconsin, November 1957: a hardware-store owner, Bernice Worden, vanished, and the last receipt she wrote led police to a quiet local handyman, Ed Gein. What they found at his farm made national news and later inspired Psycho, The Texas Chain Saw Massacre and The Silence of the Lambs. Netflix's Monster: The Ed Gein Story brought it back in 2025, but fact-checks say several scenes were invented. This is what the record shows, and what is legend.

LEGAL RECORD: Ed Gein was charged with the first-degree murder of Bernice Worden. He was found incompetent to stand trial in 1958 and committed to a state hospital. At a 1968 trial without a jury, the judge found he committed first-degree murder and was legally insane, so he was committed, not imprisoned. Investigators said he also admitted killing Mary Hogan (1954); he was never charged or tried over her death. He was never charged over his brother Henry's 1944 death. He died at the Mendota Mental Health Institute in 1984.

CONTENT: this film does not describe what was found in the house beyond the fact that he had robbed graves. Every person shown is an illustration, not a likeness; Bernice Worden and Mary Hogan appear only as silhouette cards; the farm, store and street are generic illustrations.
