# The Widower: opening concepts (v2)

Test question: if a stranger met this in their recommendations, would the first 10 seconds stop the scroll, and would the next 50 make them stay?
Only claims that are CORROBORATED in `evidence/claims.json` may carry the hook. Single-source details, such as the clean ski mask, stay later in the film, attributed.

## A. The 911 call (chronological cold open)

> In May 2008, a man in Las Vegas called 911. His wife had been shot, he said, by a masked intruder. And then he told them something else. He'd got the gun away from the intruder, and shot him.

- **Clarity:** high. It is one concrete event.
- **Accuracy:** high. Randolph's account is attributed ("he said"); Fox and Dateline's archive audio carry it.
- **Impact:** high. A phone call, a dead wife, a dead intruder.
- **Visuals:** strong. A house at night, a man on the phone, police lights.
- **Curiosity:** medium on its own. Many true-crime films open on a 911 call. It needs a twist inside 15 seconds.

## B. The acquittal that came back (paradox first)

> Thomas Randolph has stood trial for murder three times. One jury set him free. Two juries found him guilty. And the first guilty verdict was thrown out, because of the case he had already won.

- **Clarity:** medium. A legal paradox takes a beat to land with no picture to hang it on.
- **Accuracy:** high. Court records plus several outlets support it.
- **Impact:** medium.
- **Visuals:** weaker. Courtrooms and stamps.
- **Curiosity:** very high. It is a genuine contradiction that the film resolves.

## C. The undercover cellmate (rejected)

> In a Utah jail, a man awaiting his murder trial offered a cellmate cash and a car title to kill the witness against him. The cellmate was an undercover police officer.

- **Impact:** high. **Visuals:** good. **Accuracy:** high (his guilty plea to witness tampering).
- **Rejected for fairness.** Opening on this prior act is exactly what Nevada's Supreme Court called unfairly prejudicial when the 2017 jury heard it. It would prime viewers before they hear the case. The detail stays in the film, in its place, beside the 1989 acquittal.

## Chosen: A, then B inside the first minute

The concrete event first: picture, sound and a human voice on the phone. Then the twist, then the paradox, then the reason to keep watching.

| Time | Job | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | Hook | The 911 call: his wife shot "by a masked intruder"; he says he shot the intruder |
| 10–25 s | Stakes | The dead man was a handyman he knew. The caller is Thomas Randolph, and Sharon was his sixth wife. Prosecutors told a different story: he hired the man, then killed him |
| 25–40 s | Escalate | He says he is innocent. He has stood trial for murder three times: one acquittal, two convictions |
| 40–60 s | Reason to stay | The first conviction and its two death sentences were thrown out, because the jury heard about the case he had already won. To see why, go back to Utah, 1986 |

After a minute the viewer knows:
1. **The mystery:** what happened in that house, and how the courts got to their answer.
2. **Why it matters:** a man is serving 60 years to life, and he says he is innocent.
3. **Why the obvious answer is not enough:** the story he gave police, and an acquittal that undid a conviction.
4. **What is coming:** the Utah case, the evidence, two trials, and where he is now.
