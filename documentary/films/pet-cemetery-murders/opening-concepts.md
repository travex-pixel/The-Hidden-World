# The Pet Cemetery Murders: opening concepts

Test question: if a stranger met this in their recommendations, would the first 10 seconds stop the scroll, and would the next 50 make them stay?
Only claims that are CORROBORATED in `evidence/claims.json` may carry the hook. The ghost is never presented as fact: it is always what a witness said.

## A. The chained body (chronological cold open)

> In April 1985, the woman who ran a pet cemetery in south Louisiana disappeared. She was due in court within days, fighting to get her land back. Less than a month later, fishermen found her in the Mississippi River, weighted down with steel chains.

- **Clarity:** high. It is one concrete event: a disappearance, a lawsuit and a body in the river.
- **Accuracy:** high. Supported by P-VANISH, P-RIVER and P-CEMETERY (Sheriff's Office, FOX 8, The Advocate, AOL).
- **Impact:** high. A pet cemetery, a court date and chains.
- **Visuals:** strong. Small stones under the oaks at dusk, then dark water and search torches.
- **Curiosity:** high. Who wanted her gone before that court date?

## B. The haunted witness (paranormal first)

> In 2009, a man went to the police in St. Bernard Parish with a story about a murder twenty-four years old. He said the killer had confessed to him. And he said the dead woman's ghost was haunting his dreams.

- **Clarity:** medium. Without the crime first, the viewer has nothing to hang it on.
- **Accuracy:** high if it is attributed: it is his claim, as Detective Doran described it (P-WITNESS1).
- **Impact:** very high. It is the HBO hook.
- **Risk:** opening on the ghost makes the film sound like it endorses it, and it is also exactly what every other video about this series will open with.

## C. The plea (outcome first)

> After twenty-nine years, the man police had suspected from day one finally pleaded guilty. Not to murder. To manslaughter. Ten years.

- **Clarity:** high. **Accuracy:** high (P-PLEA, P-SENTENCE, P-27).
- **Impact:** medium. It gives away the ending before the viewer cares about Dorothy.
- **Visuals:** weak (a courtroom).

## Chosen: A, then B and C inside the first minute

The concrete crime first, then the witness who says a ghost made him talk, then the unexpected outcome as the reason to stay.

| Time | Job | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | Hook | April 1985: the pet cemetery owner vanishes, days before her court date over her land |
| 10–25 s | Stakes | Found in the Mississippi, weighted with steel chains. Police suspected one man from the start: her groundskeeper. No physical evidence, and the case went cold |
| 25–40 s | Escalate | 2009: a man says the groundskeeper confessed to him, and that her ghost is haunting his dreams. 2012: a prison inmate says he was there |
| 40–60 s | Reason to stay | A case built almost entirely on one witness ends in a guilty plea, to manslaughter, not murder. This is the real case behind HBO's ghost story |

After a minute the viewer knows:
1. **The mystery:** who killed Dorothy Thompson, and why it took 29 years.
2. **Why it matters:** a woman fighting for her land was silenced days before court.
3. **Why the obvious answer is not enough:** the suspect was known from the start, but there was no evidence; the breakthrough came from witnesses with strange stories.
4. **What is coming:** the land, the river, the cold case, the two witnesses, the plea, and what the HBO series leaves open.

## Rejected details

- The victim's own past (the 1970 shooting ruled self-defence, and a later husband found shot with no charges): single-source at excerpt level and adverse to the victim. Not used.
- "Ten days before trial" (FOX 8 only): the film says "days before".
- The 200-pound chains figure, the named 2012 witness (police did not name him), the 1934 family history and anything about the HBO series' own reconstructions: not used.
