# Read-aloud scripts: Case Files 48–53

How to record: one audio file per video. Read each line, then pause about 2 seconds before the next. If you fluff a line, stop, pause, and read the whole line again (the last clean take is kept). Phone 20 cm from your mouth, a little to the side, quiet room (a wardrobe full of clothes is ideal). Name the file with the case number, e.g. `48.m4a`.

## 48 — The Monopoly cop
1. The prize guard was the thief.
2. Over twenty million dollars in prizes.
3. He was a former cop who guarded McDonald's Monopoly pieces.
4. But he stole the top winning pieces.
5. Then handed them out, for a cut.
6. Some paid him fifty thousand dollars, upfront.
7. For twelve years, nobody stopped him.
8. Until someone tipped off the FBI.
9. Agents posed as a McDonald's film crew.
10. In 2001, they arrested him and seven others.
11. Got a prize message? Save these three rules.
12. Americans reported losing three hundred and one million to fake prizes.
13. He then got thirty-seven months in prison.
14. This time, someone else was the guard.

## 49 — The Twitter teen
1. At seventeen, he hijacked Obama's Twitter.
2. Then Musk, Gates and Apple.
3. How? He phoned staff, posing as tech support.
4. They typed passwords into a fake site.
5. Inside, they targeted a hundred and thirty accounts.
6. The tweets: send Bitcoin, get double back.
7. In one day, people sent over a hundred thousand dollars.
8. So Twitter briefly muted every verified account.
9. Then, sixteen days later, police arrested him in Tampa.
10. SIM swaps cost Americans sixty-eight million dollars in 2021.
11. Want to stop this? Save these three.
12. His plea deal: three years.
13. No computers without police supervision.

## 50 — The lottery coder
1. The lottery's own security boss rigged it.
2. His secret code made three days a year predictable.
3. His brother and a friend claimed wins in five states.
4. The brother was a Texas judge.
5. Then one Iowa ticket hit a sixteen-and-a-half million dollar jackpot.
6. But nobody claimed it.
7. Lawyers tried claiming it for a trust in Belize.
8. The lottery refused. Who bought it?
9. A gas station camera filmed the buyer.
10. Co-workers knew that voice. It was Eddie Tipton.
11. Powerball jackpot odds? One in two hundred and ninety-two million.
12. A text says you won? Save this.
13. The judge cited greed, and gave him twenty-five years.

## 51 — The maple syrup heist
1. They stole eighteen million dollars of syrup.
2. And the barrels still looked full.
3. It was Quebec's strategic reserve of syrup.
4. Yes, that is real.
5. How do you steal that much?
6. Slowly, at night, for almost a year.
7. They emptied barrels at a sugar shack,
8. added water, and brought them back.
9. In 2012, an inspector climbed up the barrels.
10. And the barrel under him? Empty.
11. Canada makes over three quarters of the world's syrup.
12. Buying second-hand online? Save these three red flags.
13. Then the ringleader got eight years.
14. And a fine of over nine million dollars.

## 52 — The fake invoices
1. He sent Google fake invoices. They paid.
2. Then Facebook paid. Over a hundred million dollars.
3. The sender was a Lithuanian man.
4. So how did it work?
5. Both bought hardware from a real supplier based in Taiwan.
6. He emailed their staff fake invoices in its name.
7. But the money went to him.
8. In Latvia, he registered a company with the same name.
9. You pay bills by email? Save these three checks.
10. Last year, this scam cost victims three billion dollars.
11. He got five years in prison.
12. His weapon? A copied name and an email.

## 53 — The cocaine plane, part 2
1. The pilot got eighteen years. Who sent him?
2. A Melbourne crime syndicate.
3. Police were already watching.
4. Reportedly, for two years.
5. Their plane flew up to P-N-G for over five hundred kilos of cocaine.
6. But it crashed on take-off.
7. The same day, police arrested five men in Australia.
8. Not one kilo of it got through.
9. So why risk it?
10. Australians used six point eight tonnes of cocaine in a year.
11. It did reach Australia, eventually.
12. It went straight into a furnace.
13. Offered easy cash to carry a bag? Save this.
14. Syndicate members got fifty-four years between them.
15. All for a plane that never left the ground.
