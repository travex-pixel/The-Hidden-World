**Sources.** Everything here comes from the dossiers in `series/true-crime/research_next_slate/`, plus the earlier dossiers in `series/true-crime/research_other_candidates/`. The legal status of every case must be re-checked on the day it is produced.

## What the hits have in common
The BTK floppy disk, Chamberlain and the Unabomber episodes all have the same shape:
- **One verified, surprising clue**, revealed in the first line: a floppy disk, a brother recognising someone's writing, a jacket found years later.
- **A settled legal outcome**, or a status stated precisely.
- **A non-graphic, illustrated telling**, 80–90 s long, that ends on a real open question.

The next slate keeps that shape. We have no access to the account's analytics; "doing well" is the owner's own report.

## How each episode gets made (existing pipeline)
1. **Research ledger** (`episodes/<id>/research.md`): at least two sources per claim; facts, allegations and myths kept separate; status re-verified on the day.
2. **Script:** the clue goes in the first line. About 12 scenes and 90 s or less, using score sections A–E.
3. **Shots:** 11–12 shots across at least 4 sets, with characters acting on the spoken word. Closed cases use illustrated representations ("not a likeness"); ongoing cases use silhouettes.
4. **Production:** `python series/true-crime-cinematic/produce.py <n>` runs the 24-check QA gate, including the 10 audio checks.
5. **Review and delivery:** five review passes, then the final MP4, a share copy of 30 MiB or less, and an Instagram caption.

**Effort.** A render takes 12–19 minutes on this machine. Writing sets and scenes is the real work. One episode per session is comfortable, and two are feasible once the shared set kits (museum, vault, night street) exist.
