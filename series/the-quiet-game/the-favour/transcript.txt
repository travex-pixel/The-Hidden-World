== 4:15  THE QUIET FORCES ==

[4:16] NARRATOR: Let's pause. Daniel isn't a bad person.
[4:19] NARRATOR: So why does a decent person stay quiet?
[4:22] NARRATOR: Psychologists would point to at least three quiet forces.
[4:28] NARRATOR: The first is reciprocity.
[4:30] NARRATOR: Sociologist Alvin Gouldner described a near-universal norm:
[4:33] NARRATOR: when someone helps us, we feel we owe them.
[4:36] NARRATOR: When Graham finally asked, saying no felt like refusing to pay a debt.
[4:41] NARRATOR: In one classic 1971 study, people given a free soft drink by a stranger
[4:47] NARRATOR: later bought about twice as many of his raffle tickets as people given nothing.
[4:52] NARRATOR: The second is cognitive dissonance: the discomfort of acting against your own values.
[4:58] NARRATOR: We often ease it by changing the story, not the behaviour.
[5:01] NARRATOR: It's only point eight percent. Graham will fix it. It's not my place.
[5:06] NARRATOR: Recent large replications found weaker effects, so treat it as a tendency, not a law.
[5:13] NARRATOR: The third is pluralistic ignorance.
[5:15] NARRATOR: Many people privately doubt something, but each assumes the others are fine with it.
[5:20] NARRATOR: So nobody speaks.
[5:22] NARRATOR: In one Princeton study, students thought they were more uneasy about campus drinking
[5:26] NARRATOR: than their peers were. Many of their peers felt exactly the same.
[5:31] NARRATOR: Once you've agreed to something small, the next request feels smaller than it is.
[5:35] NARRATOR: Psychologists call it the foot in the door.
