## Recommendation: #1, "The Nicest Person in the Office Might Be Playing You"

- It is honest about ambiguity. "Might be" matches the episode, which never proves Graham's intent and says so ("Daniel never found out for certain").
- It speaks to the viewer ("you"). Most people have worked with a likeable person whose kindness came with strings, so it reaches beyond this one story.
- It pairs with the thumbnail, which carries the other half of the hook ("JUST A FAVOUR?" plus the struck-out figure). The title and thumbnail do not repeat each other.
- It works at mobile truncation. The first 40 characters ("The Nicest Person in the Office Might…") still carry the hook.

**A/B test partner, if you use YouTube's title test:** #2, "Why Office Favours Can Become Political Traps". It is stronger for search (office favours, office politics) and weaker on emotion.

**Avoid:** titles claiming the manager *is* a manipulator, or that favours are always traps. The episode explicitly says most favours are kindness.
