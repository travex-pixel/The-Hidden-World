[3:11] NARRATOR: In a 1989 study, people took turns naming ideas in small groups.
[3:16] NARRATOR: Later, some of them listed their partners' ideas as their own.
[3:20] NARRATOR: Memory tends to keep an idea, and quietly lose where it came from.
[3:25] NARRATOR: Our memories don't come with a label saying who said what.
[3:28] NARRATOR: The brain rebuilds the source each time we remember, and it can get it wrong.

[3:37] NARRATOR: Later studies found that improving someone else's idea
[3:41] NARRATOR: makes you more likely to claim it later.
