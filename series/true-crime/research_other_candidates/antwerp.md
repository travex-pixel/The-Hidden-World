# Dossier: Antwerp Diamond Centre heist (15–16 Feb 2003). Leonardo Notarbartolo and the "School of Turin"

## Research method and limits
- Research date: 2026-10-11.
- **Limit:** direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by the network egress policy. All claims come from **WebSearch result excerpts** of the listed URLs. I did not read the full pages, including the Wired article itself.
- No Belgian court documents were reachable. Dutch/Italian-language court reporting was not searched in depth.
- **Heavy caution:** much of the popular narrative (the vault-layer "checklist", nicknames, the replica vault, the panic attack at the dump site, the insurance-fraud theory, the "$20 million" figure) traces back to **Notarbartolo's own account to Joshua Davis (Wired, March 2009)**. CJR, the book *Flawless* and the Belgian police dispute that account.

## People and roles
- **Leonardo Notarbartolo:** Italian, convicted as ringleader. He was a tenant of the Diamond Centre.
- **Ferdinando Finotto, Elio D'Onorio, Pietro Tavano:** convicted accomplices.
  - The nicknames "the Monster" (Finotto) and "Speedy" (Tavano) come from the Wired account.
  - Wired also describes "the Genius" and "the King of Keys", **never identified or convicted** (per Wikipedia/recaps).
- **Agim De Bruycker and Patrick Peys:** Belgian Diamond Squad detectives who led the investigation (secondary; they appear in the Netflix film).
- **August Van Camp:** landowner who found the rubbish (Wikipedia/secondary).
- **Joshua Davis:** Wired writer, author of the March 2009 piece "The Untold Story of the World's Biggest Diamond Heist".
- **Scott Andrew Selby and Greg Campbell:** authors of *Flawless: Inside the Largest Diamond Heist in History* (2010). The book disputes Notarbartolo's account.

## Established facts (multiple contemporaneous/independent sources)
1. Over 15–16 Feb 2003 the Antwerp Diamond Centre's basement vault was breached and **123 of 160** boxes opened. Police estimated the loss at **~$100 million**. No alarm sounded.
2. Rubbish linked to the gang was dumped near the E19 and found by a landowner. It included evidence tying the crew to the crime.
3. Notarbartolo was detained at the Diamond Centre on 21 Feb 2003.
4. **May 2005 court verdict:** four Italians convicted; Notarbartolo got 10 years + €1M fine, the others 5 years + €5,000.
5. **The loot has never been recovered**, per AP 2003 at the time and later outlets including Libero 2025 and the Netflix film publicity.
6. Guinness World Records lists it as the largest diamond heist ($100M+) and attributes the gang's downfall to "DNA evidence left behind on a carelessly discarded sandwich".

## Claims mainly from Notarbartolo's Wired account (treat as his claims)
- The detailed **vault-layer list**:
  - a combination lock with ~100 million combinations
  - an infrared heat detector (defeated with hairspray)
  - Doppler radar
  - a seismic sensor
  - a light sensor
  - a magnetic-field sensor
  - a locked steel door, etc.
  - Some of this is reported independently (e.g. there was no alarm), but the **specific bypass techniques are from his telling**.
  - Script-safe wording: "a vault protected by multiple layers of sensors and locks."
- **Tiny camera hidden in a briefcase** to film the vault, and a **full-scale replica vault** built for rehearsal. These are disputed by *Flawless*/CJR.
- **Nicknames** and the unidentified "Genius" and "King of Keys".
- **The dump scene:** Speedy had a panic attack and scattered the rubbish off the E19. This is his narrative of how the evidence came to be strewn.
- **Insurance-fraud theory:** he says a diamond dealer commissioned the job and that dealers had removed stock beforehand. He says the real haul was only about **$20 million**.
  - This was disputed: reviewers note much of the vault was reportedly uninsured, and CJR says the Belgian police dispute the account.
  - It has also been criticised as relying on an antisemitic trope (My Jewish Learning, 2009 [A19]; IDEX [A20]).
  - **Do not present any of this as fact.** If used at all, frame it as "his story, which police reject".
- **Payment:** Wired says Davis did not pay him. *Flawless*'s authors say Notarbartolo had demanded money from journalists (Salon review [A21]).

## Evidence presented / what the court established
- The court convicted on what reports call **circumstantial evidence**: the rubbish items, the sandwich DNA, and documents such as the invoice and business card. The emeralds found at a safe house are noted in secondary sources.
- The court found Notarbartolo guilty as the leader. Sentences and fines are as above.
- **The court did not establish the whereabouts of the loot or the identity of any other participants.**

## Disputed / unknown / contradictions
- **Whose DNA was on the sandwich.** Most sources (Guinness, Wikipedia, NatGeo-cited) say **Notarbartolo**. One secondary blog (thecinemaholic) ties a sandwich-shop receipt and footage to **Finotto** buying the salami. One outlier names a "Marc Bertoldi" (unreliable).
  - Script-safe wording: "DNA on a half-eaten salami sandwich helped tie the crew to the crime." Name Notarbartolo only with "reportedly".
- **Box count.** 123 of 160 (CBS/JCK/AP) vs a "109 of 189" claim (one Substack). **Use 123.**
- **Loot value.** ~$100M (police estimate) vs Notarbartolo's ~$20M claim. Netflix publicity quotes "$100 million to half a billion". **Use "an estimated $100 million+".**
- **Dump location.** E19 near Antwerp/Brussels is the consensus. Outliers say the Ardennes forest (skyjems) or the E40. **Use the E19.**
- **Videotape.** The tapes were taken from the Diamond Centre's surveillance system and reportedly found in the dumped rubbish (ABC Primetime, recaps). This is less well sourced than the sandwich.
- **Re-arrest 2013.** The reason is unclear (parole violation/warrant). Libero only.
- **Finotto's 2007 arrest / death in 2022:** single secondary source.
- **Appeal:** one source mentions the "court of appeal of Antwerp" for the 10-year sentence. Not confirmed.

## Corrections to the brief
- The brief's facts are broadly correct. Clarifications:
  - The bag was dumped **off the E19** on private land and found by the landowner. It was reported strewn rather than a single neat bag.
  - The sandwich is "half-eaten salami" in most accounts.
  - Sentence: **10 years (+ €1M fine), May 2005**. Paroled 2009 and re-arrested 2013 (Libero).
  - "Video tape of the diamond centre": the surveillance tapes taken from the building were reportedly in the rubbish. This is secondary-sourced.

## Strongest storytelling angle
A vault said to be guarded by layer upon layer of sensors was beaten without a single alarm. The crew was undone by litter, including a half-eaten salami sandwich dumped beside a motorway. Then comes the twist: the diamonds have never been found, and the only "full story" comes from the convicted thief himself, whose version police reject.

## Most compelling verified revelation
Guinness World Records itself credits the downfall of the "largest diamond heist" to DNA on a discarded sandwich. More than 20 years later, the estimated $100M haul has never been recovered.
