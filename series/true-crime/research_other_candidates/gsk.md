## People and roles
- **Joseph James DeAngelo Jr.:** defendant. Former police officer (Exeter 1973–76, Auburn 1976–79).
- **Anne Marie Schubert:** Sacramento County District Attorney who led the joint prosecution. The sacda.org page shows her office soliciting community stories. Her title is consistent across coverage, but her role was not confirmed in an excerpt on an official page.
- **Paul Holes:** named as the lead investigator in PBS/Mercury News excerpts. His agency affiliation was not verified here.
- **Barbara Rae-Venter:** genetic genealogist who worked with Holes and the FBI on the GEDmatch matches (Wikipedia pointer, KTVU interview [G27], Slate review of her book *I Know Who You Are* [G28]).
- **Steve Grippi:** Sacramento Chief Deputy DA who confirmed the genealogy method on 26 Apr 2018.
- **Judge Michael Bowman:** sentencing judge.
- **Curtis Rogers:** GEDmatch co-founder. He told AP: "This was done without our knowledge."
- **Multiple county DAs** prosecuted jointly. Contra Costa and Orange are confirmed as partners [G1], [G21]. The full list was not verified.

## Established facts
1. Investigators uploaded crime-scene DNA to **GEDmatch**, found distant cousins, and built out family trees that led to DeAngelo.
2. They confirmed him with surreptitious DNA:
   - a **car-door-handle swab** (18 Apr 2018, Hobby Lobby, Roseville)
   - a **tissue from his trash** (23 Apr 2018)
3. He was arrested 24 Apr 2018 at age 72. He was a **former police officer** who had been fired in 1979.
4. On 29 Jun 2020 he pleaded guilty to 13 murders and 13 kidnapping-for-robbery counts and admitted dozens of uncharged crimes.
5. On 21 Aug 2020 he was sentenced to 11 consecutive life terms without parole, plus 15 life terms with parole and 8 years.

## Allegations vs. established
- **The "Visalia Ransacker" burglaries** (1974–75) were linked by investigators. That link is part of the case lore, and some coverage includes it.
  - Whether these crimes fall under the admitted "uncharged crimes" was **not verified** in this research.
  - Exeter is near Visalia, but do not script this link as established without checking.
- An ABC11 URL slug mentions "visalia-ransacker", so mainstream coverage connects the two. Which Visalia-era crimes, if any, are among the admitted uncharged crimes was **not verified**.

## Evidence presented
- No trial was held. Evidence was summarised through the plea hearing and victim statements.
- DNA: crime-scene profiles matched the tissue sample.
- The plea release cites more than 1.3 million pages of discovery.

## What the court established
- Guilty pleas, and convictions on 13 counts of first-degree murder and 13 counts of kidnapping to commit robbery.
- Sentence: 11 consecutive LWOP terms, plus 15 life terms with parole, plus 8 years.
- Admissions of 161 uncharged crimes involving 61 victims, per the official release. These were **admitted, not convicted**, mostly because statutes of limitation had run on the rapes.

## Disputed / unknown / contradictions
- **"13 rape-related counts" vs "13 kidnapping" counts.** Some AP/NPR copy describes the second set of 13 counts as rape-related. The **official release** says **kidnapping to commit robbery**. The prosecutors charged kidnapping because the statutes of limitation on the rapes had expired (Pleasanton Weekly, quoting Contra Costa). **Use the official wording.**
- **Victim counts.**
  - The official release says 13 murders, 13 kidnappings, plus 161 uncharged crimes / 61 victims.
  - AP says "87 victims at 53 crime scenes across 11 counties".
  - These are different counting bases, so don't mix them in one sentence.
- **Which databases were used.**
  - 2018 public account: GEDmatch. FamilyTreeDNA, Ancestry, 23andMe and MyHeritage all denied involvement.
  - Later reporting (LA Times, ~Dec 2020, seen only via a secondary summary at The Naked Scientists [G29]) says investigators also used **FamilyTreeDNA and MyHeritage**, the latter without the company's knowledge.
  - Script-safe wording: "a public genealogy database, GEDmatch".
- **Fake profile:** NYT-reported, relayed by The Conversation and others. GEDmatch says it was done "without our knowledge".
- **The door-handle sample:** "likely match" vs the tissue as the "conclusive" link. The brief's phrase "discarded DNA from car door handle" is slightly inaccurate. The door handle was a **surreptitious touch-DNA swab, not discarded material**. The **tissue from the trash** was the discarded item.
- **Source sample for the upload** (Ventura County rape kit, collected 1980): secondary only.
- **Number of trees** ("more than 25") and number of relatives (10–20): secondary only.
- **Arrest vs booking date:** arrested 24 Apr, booked early 25 Apr.

## Corrections to the brief
- The guilty plea was **29 Jun 2020**. Sentencing was **21 Aug 2020**.
- "Discarded DNA from car door handle and a tissue": the **door handle was swabbed, not discarded**. Only the **tissue was discarded** (in his trash).
- The plea was 13 murders + **13 kidnapping-for-robbery** counts, not rape counts.

## Strongest storytelling angle
The killer never gave his DNA to any genealogy site. Distant relatives did, by sharing theirs. Investigators uploaded a crime-scene profile, climbed his family tree from third cousins down to one 72-year-old ex-cop in Citrus Heights, then waited for him to leave his DNA on a car door at a craft store.

## Most compelling verified revelation
The man behind 13 murders was himself a **former police officer**, fired in 1979 for shoplifting a hammer and dog repellent. He was identified through his **relatives' DNA** on a free genealogy website.

## Why it suits a 60-second Short
- A fully visual, non-violent chain: a family-tree animation, a DNA double helix, a car door handle and a tissue.
- A twist ending: he was once a cop.
- A clear modern relevance hook: your cousin's DNA can identify you.

**Sensitivity:** the case involved many rapes. Keep it non-graphic and avoid victim imagery.
