## 2. People and roles

- **The Somerton Man**: unidentified officially. The researchers' candidate is **Carl "Charles" Webb** (b. 16 Nov 1905, Footscray, Victoria), electrical engineer and instrument maker, aged 43 in 1948. Parents Richard August Webb and Eliza Amelia Morris Grace (per Abbott via ABC).
- **Dorothy Jean Webb (née Robertson)**: Webb's wife, married 1941, later separated. Abbott suggests she had moved to South Australia, which could explain the trip to Adelaide (ABC 2022-07-26). That is Abbott's hypothesis.
- **Thomas Erskine Cleland**: SA City Coroner, ran the 1949 inquest.
- **John Dwyer**: pathologist at the autopsy.
- **John Bain Lyons and wife**: evening witnesses, 30 Nov 1948.
- **Det. Sgt R. L. Leane** (and **Det. Lionel Brown**): police investigators who received the *Rubaiyat* and traced the edition.
- **Anonymous Glenelg man**: found the *Rubaiyat* in his car. Police withheld his identity at his request.
- **Jessica "Jestyn" Thomson (née Harkness), 1921-2007**: nurse whose phone number was in the book. She denied knowing the man. Her identity was protected for decades.
- **Alf Boxall**: former army officer who had received a *Rubaiyat* from "Jestyn" in 1945. Found alive, so he was not the dead man.
- **Kate Thomson**: Jessica's daughter, 2013 *60 Minutes* claims.
- **Robin Thomson**: Jessica's son, a ballet dancer, once theorised to be the Somerton Man's son. Abbott says DNA ruled this out (2022).
- **Prof. Derek Abbott**: University of Adelaide, led the identification. Married Robin Thomson's daughter Rachel Egan in 2010.
- **Dr Colleen Fitzpatrick**: US forensic genealogist (Identifinders), co-led the 2022 identification.
- **Astrea Forensics**: US lab that sequenced the hair DNA.
- **Vickie Chapman**: SA Attorney-General who approved the 2021 exhumation. **John Rau**: AG who refused it in 2011.
- **SA Police (Major Crime) and Forensic Science SA**: official investigators. The **SA State Coroner** makes the formal determination.

## 3. Established facts (well sourced)

1. A man was found dead on Somerton beach, Adelaide, on the morning of 1 December 1948. [NEWS: Smithsonian, Washington Post, ABC]
2. Autopsy found an enlarged spleen and congested organs; no poison was detected. [NEWS: Smithsonian]
3. The 1949 inquest **made no finding**. It was adjourned sine die because the evidence was too inconclusive. The coroner said he would be prepared to find death from a poison, but could not say who gave it or where the man died. [OFFICIAL: SA State Records]
4. The coroner's inquest file is **71/1949 (GRG 1/27)**, open at State Records of SA. A **later inquest file** also exists in State Records' custody, but it is restricted (under 60 years old) and requests go to the Coroner's Court. State Records holds no police files because the case is still open. [OFFICIAL: SA State Records]
5. A scrap reading "Taman/Tamam Shud" was found in a fob pocket. It was torn from the final page of a *Rubaiyat* handed in by a Glenelg man in July 1949. That book held faint handwritten letters (the "code") and a phone number traced to a local nurse. [OFFICIAL: SA State Records; NEWS: ABC 2021-05-19]
6. "Tamam shud" is Persian for "ended" or "finished". It is the closing phrase of FitzGerald's *Rubaiyat*. Police translated it with a Persian-English dictionary. [NEWS: ABC 2021-05-19 "six clues"]
7. The remains were exhumed from West Terrace Cemetery on 19 May 2021 by SA Police and Forensic Science SA. [NEWS: ABC, InDaily]
8. In July 2022, Abbott and Fitzpatrick publicly named Carl "Charles" Webb, based on genetic genealogy from hair in the plaster bust. [NEWS: ABC, Smithsonian, Washington Post]
9. As of the latest report found (ABC, 6 July 2026), **SA Police had not yet delivered its report to the coroner, and there is no official confirmation of the identity.** [NEWS]

## 4. OFFICIAL STATUS (critical)

- **Identity: NOT officially confirmed.** SA Police and Forensic Science SA have not publicly verified the Webb identification. Their 2022 position was "cautiously optimistic", further DNA work needed, and identity "will ultimately be determined by the Coroner". As of 6 July 2026, police were still preparing their report to the coroner, gave no date, and called the investigation "complex and ongoing". No inquest, coronial finding or court ruling on identity was found up to 11 Oct 2026.
- **Expected next step:** the police report goes to the coroner, who decides whether to hold an inquest and then publishes findings. Abbott (not police) said he expected findings by the end of 2026. **That is Abbott's expectation, not an official timetable.**
- **Cause of death: never determined.** The 1949 inquest made no formal finding. Since 2022, police have been described (by Abbott, via ABC) as focusing on identity, not cause of death. No later official cause of death was found.
- **Court findings: none.** There has been no criminal case. The only judicial step is the 1949 inquest (no finding), plus a later inquest file that is restricted.

**Safe script wording:** "In 2022, researchers named him as Carl 'Charles' Webb, a Melbourne engineer. Police say the coroner will make the final call, and as of mid-2026 that ruling hadn't come. How he died is still unknown."

## 5. Allegations and theories (NOT established)

- **Soviet spy / Cold War espionage**: based on the period (1948, early ASIO era), the unbroken "code", the removed labels and Kate Thomson's 2013 claims. **No documentary evidence found.** Present as speculation only.
- **Jessica Thomson knew him / he was the father of her son Robin**: the son link is reported as **ruled out by DNA** (Abbott, ABC 2022-07-30). That she knew him is a family claim (Kate Thomson, 2013) and unproven. She denied it to police. She died in 2007.
- **Suicide vs. murder**: the 1949 medical witnesses leaned to poison, possibly suicidal. The coroner said he could not say who administered it. Abbott has floated suicide linked to marital breakdown (secondary reports, e.g. nichelifeonline "toxic marriage"). Unproven.
- **Specific poisons (digitalis, ouabain)**: named in later retellings. The inquest suppressed the poison names. **Do not state as fact.**
- **Webb came to Adelaide to find his estranged wife**: Abbott's hypothesis (ABC 2022-07-26).
- **"Keane" clothes were hand-me-downs from Webb's brother-in-law**: Abbott's hypothesis. In his words, "We can't say for sure, but we can speculate."

## 7. Disputed or unknown

- **Who he was, officially**: pending the coroner.
- **How he died**: unknown. No poison was detected in 1948.
- **Where he died**: the coroner said in 1949 he could not say. Night witnesses never saw the face clearly, so the body may have been moved (speculative).
- **What the "code" means**: unsolved. The 1949 Navy view as quoted was "neither a code nor a cipher". Letter-frequency analysis suggests the initials of English words (University of Adelaide student projects).
- **Why the labels were removed.**
- **How the *Rubaiyat* got into the car**, and whether it was even his.
- **The phone number's significance**: the nurse denied knowing him.
- **Webb's movements after about April 1947.**

## 8. Contradictions between sources

- **Cigarette**: unsmoked on chest vs. partly smoked on lapel vs. unlit on collar. [Washington Post vs. other retellings]
- **Body position**: sitting up vs. lying flat.
- **Spelling**: "Taman Shud" in the inquest file vs. "Tamam Shud" in most media; "Tamám" with accent in popular usage.
- **When the *Rubaiyat* was found**: found in the car around late Nov 1948, **handed to police in late July 1949**. Some retellings conflate the two dates.
- **When the scrap was found**: some say at the first search, others months later (e.g. April 1949). Unresolved here.
- **"Kean" vs "Keane"** and which garment carried which name.
- **Abbott's confidence**: 99.9% (2022) vs. 99.99% (2026). Both are his own statements.
- **Code first line**: the 1949 press transcribed "MRGOADABD"; modern sources give "WRGOABABD".
- **Media headlines say "solved" or "identified"** (e.g. nowtolove "solved!", UPI "solved with DNA"). **Officially it is not confirmed.** Avoid "solved" as an unqualified claim.

## 10. Strongest storytelling angle

"For 74 years, he had no name." A well-dressed man is found dead on an Adelaide beach. In a tiny fob pocket is a scrap of paper with two words: *Tamám Shud*, "It is finished." Those words were torn from a book of poetry that turned up in a stranger's car, holding a code no one has cracked and a nurse's phone number. Seventy-three years later, a single hair caught in his plaster death mask gives scientists a name. Officially, he is still waiting for one.

The twist works for an animated short: **a hair from a 1949 plaster cast cracked identity, but not the death.**

## 11. Most compelling verified revelation

**One rootless hair, about 5 cm long, embedded in the 1949 police plaster bust yielded the DNA** that researchers say identifies him as Carl "Charles" Webb, a Melbourne electrical engineer. It is also verified that the coroner had still not ruled as of July 2026. Secondary hook (Abbott's hypothesis, so label it as such): the "Keane" name on the tie may have come from Webb's own brother-in-law.

## 12. Why it suits a 60-second Short

- Instantly visual, non-graphic iconography: beach at dawn, a scrap of paper, a poetry book, cipher letters, a plaster face, a DNA strand.
- A clean three-act shape: mystery (1948), clues (1949), DNA twist (2021-22), plus an open ending (coroner pending).
- No gore needed. The death itself is not visually described.
- Satisfies "solved?" curiosity while staying accurate ("named, not confirmed").
- Risk: many clues. Pick three (scrap, book+code, hair DNA).
