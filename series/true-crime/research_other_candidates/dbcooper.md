## 2. People and roles

- **"Dan Cooper" / "D.B. Cooper"**: the unidentified hijacker. FBI description: a quiet man, apparently in his mid-40s, business suit, white shirt, black tie, brown eyes, about 5'10" to 6'0" (descriptions from two flight attendants).
- **Florence Schaffner**: flight attendant who received the note.
- **Tina Mucklow**: flight attendant (22) who acted as liaison and stayed aboard.
- **Captain William Scott**: pilot (secondary sources).
- **Brian Ingram**: found the money at Tena Bar in 1980, aged 8.
- **Larry Carr**: FBI Seattle case agent, about 2007-2009.
- **Curtis Eng**: FBI special agent on the case from 2010; involved in the 2016 wind-down.
- **Ayn Dietrich-Williams**: FBI Seattle public affairs, issued the 2016 statement.
- **James Long**: reporter who said in 2016 that he caused the "D.B." error.
- **Suspects the FBI publicly ruled out or did not confirm**: Richard McCoy (FBI: did not match the flight attendants' descriptions, among other reasons), Kenneth Christiansen (did not match the physical description), Duane Weber (excluded by tie DNA per FBI 2007), Lynn Doyle Cooper (no DNA or print match, 2011).
- **Tom Kaye / Citizen Sleuths**: tie particle analysis, 2017.
- **Tom Colbert / Mark Zaid**: FOIA suit, 2016.
- **Dan Gryder; Chanté McCoy and Rick McCoy III**: McCoy parachute claim, 2022-2024.

## 3. Established facts (FBI-sourced)

1. On 24 Nov 1971, a man calling himself Dan Cooper hijacked Northwest Orient Flight 305 (Portland to Seattle), claiming a bomb. [FBI]
2. He received $200,000 in $20 bills and parachutes in Seattle, released the passengers, then jumped from the plane after 8 pm somewhere between Seattle and Reno. [FBI]
3. He was never found or identified. [FBI]
4. "D.B." was a media mistake; he bought the ticket as "Dan Cooper". [FBI]
5. In 1980 a young boy found $5,800 in $20 bills on a Columbia River sandbar, with serial numbers matching the ransom. [FBI] It is widely reported as the only ransom money ever recovered. [NEWS/secondary; the exact FBI wording was not confirmed because pages could not be fetched]
6. He left a black J.C. Penney tie on the plane. The FBI obtained a DNA sample from it. [FBI 2007]
7. On 8/12 July 2016, the FBI redirected resources away from the case. Evidence is preserved, and the FBI still asks for physical evidence about the parachutes or money. [FBI]
8. Of the parachutes provided, he left behind/used a mix that included a training parachute sewn shut. The FBI parachute exhibit notes this. [FBI image repository; details should be re-checked verbatim]

## 4. What investigations officially established

- **No identification, no charge, no court finding** about the hijacker's identity.
- **FBI position (2007 onward):** Cooper was probably not an experienced skydiver, and he likely did not survive the night jump into wilderness, with poor gear and weather. This is the FBI's **assessment, not a proven fact.** (FBI 2007 "Redux"; Carr: he likely "never got his parachute open"; this wording is a paraphrase from search snippets, so re-check before quoting.)
- **Status:** "No longer actively investigating" since July 2016. Still technically open: the FBI accepts physical evidence. Note that media headlines said "closes" (CNN: "FBI closes the books"; CSMonitor: "closes notorious unsolved skyjacking case"), but the FBI's own wording is "redirected resources". **Use "suspended its active investigation" or "stopped actively investigating", not "closed".**
- **McCoy parachute (2023-2025):** The FBI has issued **no public conclusion**. A regional report says it returned the parachute in Dec 2025 without explanation. **Do not imply the FBI confirmed or rejected McCoy on the basis of this parachute.** The FBI case page historically lists McCoy as ruled out.

## 5. Allegations and theories (not established)

- **He died in the jump**: the FBI's favoured view, supported by the location of the 1980 money, but no body has been found.
- **He survived**: popular theory with no proof.
- **Richard McCoy**: family and Gryder claim; the FBI had earlier ruled him out. Parachute claims unverified.
- **Kenneth Christiansen**: magazine claim (New York Magazine 2007; later TV). Ruled out by the FBI on physical description.
- **Robert Rackstraw**: Colbert team's suspect (*The Last Master Outlaw*). Not confirmed by the FBI (not detailed in this session's sources).
- **Boeing/aerospace worker**: Citizen Sleuths' inference from titanium particles on the tie.
- **How the money reached Tena Bar**: buried, carried by river flooding, or by Columbia dredging (a 1974 dredge deposit theory is widely discussed but not sourced here). Unresolved.

## 6. Evidence

- Black J.C. Penney clip-on tie (DNA plus particles).
- Ransom: 10,000 x $20 bills, serial numbers recorded; $5,800 recovered in 1980; the rest ($194,200) never found.
- Parachutes left on the plane; the aft-stair placard found by a hunter in 1978.
- The note (reportedly taken back by Cooper, according to secondary sources; not verified here).
- Fingerprints from the plane, described as of little use (Geoffrey Gray via secondary sources).
- The ticket (FBI artifact page).
- The FBI Vault file (100+ numbered parts).

- Exact aircraft documentation (see contradictions).

## 8. Contradictions between sources

- **Aircraft type**: the FBI Portland field-office page and FBI video say a Boeing 727. One FBI Vault document refers to a 707. The 727 is correct per most sources (its aft stairs are central to the story).
- **Passenger count**: 36 passengers including Cooper vs. "36 other passengers" vs. 37 passengers and 6 crew in one source.
- **Reno landing time**: 10:15 pm vs. about 11:02 pm.
- **2016 date**: the press release says resources were redirected on 8 July 2016; the case page shows an update dated 12 July 2016.
- **"Closed" vs. "inactive"**: media say closed; the FBI says redirected/no longer actively investigating; one report quotes "open but not active".
- **McCoy parachute seizure date**: Sept 2023 vs. 2024 (both Cowboy State Daily).
- **New-file page counts**: 668 vs. 686 pages (Biography.com vs. AOL).
- **$5,800 vs. $6,000**: a blog argues the bundles should have totalled $6,000. Not established.
- **Suspect totals**: "over 800" vs. "over 1,000" suspects are unverified. **Do not use either number.**

## 9. Strongest storytelling angle

"The only hijacking in US history that's never been solved" is commonly said, but it was **not verified this session; avoid it.** A stronger, verifiable hook is this: **Nine years after a man jumped from a jet with $200,000, an 8-year-old digging a campfire hole on a Columbia River beach hit bundles of rotting $20 bills, and the serial numbers matched.** Then the twist: no man, no parachute, no more money. In 2016 the FBI stopped actively hunting him, but it still asks the public for one thing: the parachute or the money.

## 10. Most compelling verified revelation

The **10 Feb 1980 Tena Bar find**: $5,800 in decayed $20 bills whose **serial numbers matched the ransom** (FBI). It remains the only ransom money ever recovered. A second verified hook: his famous name is a **newspaper error**. He called himself "Dan Cooper" (FBI).

## 11. Why it suits a 60-second Short

- Iconic, non-graphic visuals: a man in a suit and sunglasses, a note, a briefcase, a 727's rear stairs dropping at night, a parachute, money bundles in sand.
- Clean structure: hijack, then jump, then nine years of silence, then a child finds the money, then the FBI stands down, then the hook "still unsolved".
- No violence or victim harm to depict. Very low sensitivity risk.
- A huge existing audience means competition for attention; differentiate with the "Dan, not D.B." twist or the "8-year-old's campfire" framing.
