Where neither the URL nor the extract shows a date, the citation says "date not shown in extract". Nothing here has been checked against the full article or a primary court document.
- **The search budget ran out partway through.** The shared limit of 200 web searches per turn ran out while this dossier was being researched. The cut came after the first two Gabby Petito searches. As a result:

  - Material marked **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** is the researcher's prior knowledge, with no search confirmation. Do not script it until a follow-up search pass confirms it. The user can send a follow-up message to continue the searches.
- **Source quality.** Wire, broadsheet and broadcaster sources are preferred. Items that came only from aggregator or content-farm sites (for example ar.newcarsz.com) or from tabloid chains are flagged and must not carry a claim on their own.
- **Dates.** Some extracts gave inconsistent relative dates ("x days ago"). Only dates in the URL path or stated explicitly in the extract are used.
- **"No report found after <date>"** means these search extracts held nothing newer. It does not mean nothing happened.
- **Series rules (applied below).** Attribute every allegation. State the presumption of innocence for anyone not convicted. Never present a charge as a verdict. Show all people as anonymous silhouettes. **Re-verify status on the production day.**

## 1. The Louvre crown jewels heist (Paris, 19 Oct 2025)

### Current status (latest dated item: July 2026; no anniversary coverage found yet)

- **What happened.**
  - On Sunday 19 Oct 2025 a gang used a truck-mounted furniture lift (also called a basket lift) to reach a balcony and window of the Apollo Gallery (Galerie d'Apollon). They cut the display cases with angle grinders and left with **eight items** of the French crown jewels, valued at about **€88 million (~$102 million)**.
    - NPR, 2025-11-01 (furniture lift mounted on a truck, angle grinders): https://www.npr.org/2025/11/01/nx-s1-5594194/suspects-charged-louvre-jewel-heist-paris ; also Time (date not shown in extract): https://time.com/7330415/louvre-suspects-charged-robbery/

  - The prosecutor said the lift was obtained on 10 Oct under a false pretext. A "moving job" was booked with the lift's owner through the classifieds site Leboncoin, and the owner was then threatened, without violence.
    - Yahoo/AP timeline (date not shown in extract): https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/timeline-jewel-heist-louvre-paris-152635303.html

### Established vs alleged

- **Established:**
  - The date and place.
  - The use of a truck-mounted lift.
  - Eight items taken, valued at about €88m (official estimate).
  - Eugénie's crown dropped and recovered.
  - The other jewels still missing.
  - People arrested and charged (mis en examen).
  - The director's resignation, and Leribault's appointment.
  - The Apollo Gallery reopened empty.
  - The inquiry findings, **as reported** by the outlets named.
- **Alleged:**
  - That any specific person took part. Charged people are presumed innocent.
  - The "partial admissions", which are the prosecutor's characterisation.
  - The existence of a mastermind or backer.
  - Where the jewels are.
  - That the "LOUVRE" password was still in use in 2025.

### Most striking verified turning point

**"Thirty seconds."** By inquiry findings reported in Dec 2025, the gang left only about 30 seconds before police and guards arrived. The prosecutor said that near-simultaneous arrival stopped them burning the lift and preserved the traces investigators later used. On the way out they dropped Empress Eugénie's crown, which was recovered damaged and is being restored (France 24, 2026-02-05). Do not quote a diamond count for the dropped crown: the extracts gave "nearly 2,000 diamonds" for the stolen Eugénie diadem, a different piece.

This gives the 60 seconds a clean structure: a lift, a window, minutes inside, a dropped crown, a near miss, and a year later seven jewels still gone.

### Sensitivities

- No individual victim was reported harmed in the extracts reviewed; injuries were not checked.
- The people charged are alive and **untried**. French law strongly protects the presumption of innocence: Civil Code art. 9-1 is background knowledge, not re-verified here.
- Museum staff and guards should not be blamed individually. The minister said staff followed protocol "to the letter".

### Legal / ethical risk: **MEDIUM**

The suspects are untried, and the investigation is live in France with judges still taking statements. The risks are:

- naming or depicting identifiable suspects;
- implying guilt;
- repeating the "mastermind" theory as fact;
- stating the password was live on the day.

The institutional and security story is low risk if attributed.

### Verdict: **RECOMMEND**, for the 19 Oct anniversary

Safest framing:

- "One year on: how the crown jewels vanished and why seven are still missing."
- Focus on the method, the security failures (attributed to the named inquiries) and the empty gallery.
- Say "several people have been charged; no one has been tried; all are presumed innocent."
- No names, no faces: silhouettes only.
- Present the mastermind claim as "two suspects reportedly told judges..., while other reporting found no trace".
- Re-verify the arrest, detention and recovery status on production day.

### Most striking verified turning point

Prosecutors' June 2020 naming of a suspect is the obvious hook. **It is not usable without accusing a living, uncharged man.**

The only safe verified beat is procedural: in Sept 2025 the man prosecutors call their suspect walked free, never charged over Madeleine, wearing an ankle tag, and the case remains unsolved. Even that invites inference.

### Sensitivities

- Madeleine's parents and her twin siblings are living. Background, not re-verified this session: the parents were themselves formally named suspects (arguidos) in 2007, a status lifted in 2008, and have been through years of litigation over accusations against them.
- Brückner is a living, uncharged suspect who gives media interviews.

### Legal / ethical risk: **HIGH**

- **Defamation by implication.** Featuring an uncharged, living man in a "who took Madeleine" episode almost inevitably implies guilt. This is a risk in England and Wales, where the core audience is, and in Germany, where personality rights and press-code naming rules apply (background, not re-verified).
- **Possible contempt or prejudice.** If he is later charged, a published episode could be argued to prejudice proceedings.
- **Platform policy.** Platforms are sensitive to child-victim content.
- **Silhouettes do not cure identification** when the context identifies him.

### Verdict: **AVOID** featuring Brückner

If the channel wants a Madeleine episode at all, treat it as a **"maybe, later"**. Base it only on the 2007 disappearance and the 2025 Portugal search as procedural facts, with **no suspect discussed**, plus a line that German prosecutors have an open investigation. Get a legal review before publishing.

Safest framing (if ever): "Nineteen years on, police in three countries are still investigating. No one has been charged."
