## Method and limits (read first)

- **The search budget ran out partway through.** WebSearch has a shared cap of 200 calls per turn across all agents. This session got about 21 searches before the cap was hit. Coverage is therefore uneven:
  - **Searched and reasonably covered:** the Netflix Top 10 from mid-September to 4 October 2026; October 2026 releases on Netflix, HBO Max, Hulu, Prime Video and Peacock; and a handful of US and UK headline cases since June 2026.
  - **Not searched at all:**
    - streaming releases for November 2026 to February 2027 (a single Netflix search found nothing);
    - BBC, Channel 4, ITV, Stan and ABC Australia schedules;
    - new Australian cases;
    - anniversary dates (none were re-checked online);
    - **all of section 4** (format guidance).
  - The gaps are listed in section 6 so a follow-up session can fill them.
- **No page was opened.** WebFetch and curl are blocked, so every figure comes from search-result extracts. Each row gives the URL the search engine quoted and the date of the coverage.
- **Other sources:**
  - **Repo dossiers.** Some anniversary dates are taken from dossiers already in this repo (`research_other_candidates/*.md`, `research_case_0*.md`, `sources.md`). Those files cite primary or major-outlet URLs from an earlier session, and these rows name the dossier file.
  - **Model knowledge.** Some items come from my own knowledge, which runs to about mid-2026. They are marked **"unverified (model knowledge)"** and given **no URL**. Treat them as leads to check, not facts to script from.
- **Netflix "views"** are hours viewed divided by runtime. The Tudum week runs Monday to Sunday. The week of 5–11 Oct 2026 publishes on Tue 13 Oct, after this note.
- **Strength ratings** follow `audience.md`:
  - **strong:** an official platform figure, or one reported consistently by several major outlets;
  - **moderate:** a single credible trade or major-outlet extract;
  - **weak:** a tracker, an aggregator or a single minor outlet;
  - **unverified:** the sources conflict, I could not confirm it, or it comes from model knowledge only.
- **Legal risk scale:**
  - **low:** the case is closed and the people involved are dead, or the conviction is final;
  - **moderate:** the defendant is convicted but sentencing or an appeal is pending, or the subject is living and contentious;
  - **high:** the case is charged but untried, under active investigation, or unsolved with living people publicly accused.
  - In the **UK**, once someone is charged, the strict-liability rule of the Contempt of Court Act 1981 applies. Do not publish anything about a charged UK case beyond the bare facts.

## 4. What works for true crime on Reels and Shorts (2025–2026)

**Evidence status: thin. No searches were run for this section** because the budget ran out before it was reached. Everything below is model knowledge (to about mid-2026). Each item names the source as I remember it, gives **no URL**, and is rated **unverified**. Re-check each item before treating it as a rule.

| Topic | Finding (as remembered) | Source as remembered | Strength | Implication for Case Files |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum length | YouTube Shorts accepts uploads up to **3 minutes** (from about 15 Oct 2024). Instagram raised Reels uploads to **3 minutes** in Jan 2025. | YouTube blog / Help Center (Oct 2024); Adam Mosseri / Instagram announcement (Jan 2025) | unverified | The 60–90 s format is well inside both limits. I recall no published platform data showing that longer true-crime Shorts perform better. |
| How Shorts views are counted | From 31 Mar 2025, a Shorts "view" counts every start or replay with no minimum watch time. The older measure is kept as **"engaged views"** in Analytics. | YouTube announcement, Mar 2025 | unverified | When comparing episodes, use engaged views and average percentage viewed, not raw views. |
| Instagram ranking signals | Mosseri has said the main Reels signals are **watch time**, **likes per reach** and **sends per reach** (DM shares). Sends weigh most for reaching non-followers. | Mosseri videos and posts, early 2025 | unverified | Give each Short one shareable, surprising fact, such as "the floppy disk", the kind of thing people DM to friends. This matches the three Shorts that worked for us. |
| Hook testing | **Trial Reels** (from about Dec 2024) shows a Reel to non-followers first, so a hook can be tested before followers see it. | Instagram / Mosseri, Dec 2024 | unverified | Use it to A/B test two cold opens per episode. This is the most practical way to answer "how long should the hook be?" for our own audience. |
| Originality and reposts | In 2025 Instagram said it would favour **original** content. Accounts that mainly repost other people's content lose recommendation eligibility, and videos carrying other apps' watermarks have long been deprioritised. | Instagram creator announcements, about Apr–May 2025 | unverified | Upload clean native files with no TikTok or CapCut watermark. Cross-posting our own original episodes is not reposting. |
| AI labelling (Meta) | Meta applies an **"AI info"** label when it detects industry-standard AI signals or the creator discloses AI use. | Meta newsroom, 2024 | unverified | AI narration may trigger the label. I recall no published evidence that the label itself reduces reach. Evidence is thin. |
| AI disclosure (YouTube) | Creators must disclose **realistic** altered or synthetic content, such as a real person's cloned voice or realistic fake footage. Clearly animated or illustrated content is generally exempt. | YouTube Creator Studio disclosure policy, Mar 2024 | unverified | Our 2D illustrations probably do not need disclosure. **Never** clone a real person's voice. |
| YouTube monetisation | The YPP "repetitious content" policy was renamed **"inauthentic content"** on about 15 Jul 2025. It targets mass-produced, templated or repetitive videos, which was widely read as aimed at AI "slop". | YouTube Help / YPP update, Jul 2025 | unverified | This is the **biggest structural risk** for an AI-narrated channel. Keep each episode visibly researched (on-screen sources, original scripts, varied visuals) and avoid a one-template-fits-all look. |
| Victim simulation | In about Jan 2024 YouTube's harassment policy began prohibiting content that **realistically simulates deceased minors or victims of violent events narrating their own deaths**. This was aimed at AI true-crime videos. | YouTube policy update, Jan 2024 | unverified | **Hard rule: never give a victim an AI voice or first-person narration.** Our current format already complies. |
| Graphic content | YouTube age-restricts or removes graphic violent content, and age-restricted Shorts lose reach. | YouTube Community Guidelines | unverified | Keep our existing restraint: no wounds, no bodies, evidence-led storytelling. |
| Hook length, caption style, "Part 1/2", posting cadence | **I know of no reputable published study for 2025–26 on these.** "Hook in the first 1–3 seconds" is common creator advice, not platform data. I recall no platform data on "Part 1/2" series performance. | — | **evidence thin** | Test them on our own channel: Trial Reels for hooks; one two-part pilot (Tupac → Biggie); and a 30-day fixed cadence, comparing skip rate and engaged views. |
