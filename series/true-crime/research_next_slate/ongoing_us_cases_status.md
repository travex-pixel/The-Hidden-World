## 1. Gilgo Beach: Rex Heuermann (Suffolk County, NY)

As part of the deal he also admitted killing an eighth woman, Karen Vergata (disappeared 1996), with whom he was never formally charged. The extracts say he waived his right to appeal and agreed to cooperate with the FBI Behavioral Analysis Unit. The trial had been set for September 2026.

### Established fact vs. allegation
- **Established:**
  - guilty pleas to seven murders (Barthelemy, Waterman, Costello, Brainard-Barnes, Taylor, Costilla, Mack);
  - an in-court admission to killing Karen Vergata, who was never charged;
  - the sentence;
  - the September 2025 admissibility ruling (a ruling that the method was reliable enough to admit, not a finding that the DNA proved guilt).

### Most striking verified turning point (60-second spine)
**"A pizza crust in a Manhattan trash can."** Prosecutors' 2023 filing says a slice of crust, recovered by surveillance officers, gave investigators a DNA link to a hair on a victim. In April 2026, before any jury heard it, he pleaded guilty. This works as "the clue that cracked it", with the clue attributed to the court filing and the ending (plea, sentence) stated as fact. The Avalanche, the 2022 lead that came first, is an alternative opening.

### Sensitivities
- **Victims' families** are very vocal and spoke at sentencing (one relative: "A million years isn't enough"). Several victims were sex workers, so avoid any stigmatising wording.
- **Heuermann's wife and two adult children were never charged.** Do not depict, name or imply anything about them.
- **Other Gilgo Beach remains** are not part of his plea, including the unidentified victims and the case of Shannan Gilbert. Do not suggest he killed anyone beyond the seven victims and Karen Vergata.

### Legal and ethical risk: LOW–MEDIUM
The case is closed and the conviction is by plea, so the defamation risk on guilt is low. The medium element is accuracy around the clues: mitochondrial versus nuclear DNA, and presenting filing claims as findings. Victim dignity is also a concern.

### Verdict: RECOMMEND
Framing: **"the clue that cracked it"**. Attribute the clues to the court filing and state the plea and sentence as fact.

## 3. Duane "Keffe D" Davis: killing of Tupac Shakur (Las Vegas, 1996)
