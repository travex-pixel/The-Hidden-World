Description:
```
Erdős's unit distance problem (1946): among n points in the plane, how many pairs can be at distance exactly 1? Erdős conjectured the square grid's growth was best possible. On 20 May 2026 OpenAI announced that a general-purpose reasoning model had built point sets with n^(1+ε) unit distances, disproving the conjecture. Nine mathematicians (Alon, Bloom, Gowers, Litt, Sawin, Shankar, Tsimerman, Wang, Wood) published a human-verified version; the result was formalised in Lean 4.

Context: in October 2025 OpenAI staff claimed GPT-5 had "solved" 10 Erdős problems; it had only found existing papers, and the posts were deleted.

The "AI pattern" shown is an illustration (the real construction uses algebraic number theory). Grid count is exact: a 20×20 grid gives 1,744 unit pairs.

Sources: Alon et al., arXiv 2605.20695; Scientific American; TechCrunch (20 May 2026); Pach–Raz–Solymosi (arXiv 2507.15679); erdosproblems.com; Epoch AI; The Decoder (Oct 2025).
```

## Ready answers
- **"Did it really solve it on its own?"** The construction was the model's; humans then shortened and checked the proof, and Lean formalised it. The reviewers call the original proof valid.
- **"Is this like the October claims?"** No. Those were literature lookups. This is a new construction, and the same mathematician who called October a "misrepresentation" (Thomas Bloom) is a co-author of the verification paper.
- **"Which model?"** OpenAI hasn't named it; it's described as an internal general-purpose reasoning model.
- **"So what's the use?"** None for the dots themselves. The implication is methodological: machine-found, machine-checked mathematics.
