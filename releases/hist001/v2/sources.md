## C1 · established
In mid-13th-century England the silver penny was the only coin in everyday circulation; round halfpennies and farthings were introduced in Edward I's recoinage (from 1279–80). Rare earlier round halfpennies exist (Henry I, Henry III c.1220–22).

## C2 · established
Before 1279 pennies were cut into halves and quarters ('cut halfpennies', 'cut farthings') and these circulated as small change; many were probably cut at the mint.

## C3 · established
The Voided Long Cross penny (1247) extended the cross to the edge of the coin, mainly to deter clipping. Cut pieces follow the cross; the claim that the cross was designed as a cutting guide is NOT established and is not made.

## C5 · estimate
An unskilled labourer in late-13th-century England earned roughly 1d–1½d a day (sometimes 2d); much farm work was paid partly in kind.

- [Social and economic history: Appendix 2 (VCH Middlesex)](https://prod.british-history.ac.uk/node/15304) — British History Online (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)
- [Standards of Living in the Later Middle Ages: Wage-earners (C. Dyer)](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/standards-of-living-in-the-later-middle-ages/wageearners/FD4F413E2C27CE55E7B911D0B02DD6A9) — Cambridge University Press (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)

## C6 · established
The Assize of Bread and Ale (c.1266–67, codifying older practice) fixed the weight of standard loaves such as the farthing loaf according to the price of wheat; short-weight bakers were amerced (fined) and repeat offenders could be pilloried.

- [Assize of Bread and Beer](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/breadbeer.asp) — Fordham University Internet Medieval Sourcebook (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)
- [Assize of Bread and Ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assize_of_Bread_and_Ale) — Wikipedia (accessed 2026-10-11; tertiary; search snippet only)

## C7 · established
Peasant houses in 13th-century England typically had an open central hearth without a chimney; smoke escaped through the roof.

- [Peasant houses in Midland England](https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/peasant-houses-in-midland-england-how-the-black-death-prompted-a-building-boom.htm) — Current Archaeology (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)

Timber framing (cruck framing in the Midlands, North and West), wattle-and-daub walls and thatched roofs were standard peasant building materials.

## C9 · estimate
Around 1300 roughly 80–85% of England's population lived in the countryside (towns c.15–20%: Britnell, Dyer).

- [How Urban was Medieval England?](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/how-urban-was-medieval-england) — History Today (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)

## C10 · established
Unfree tenants (villeins) owed labour services ('week-work') on the lord's demesne as part of their rent, often two or three days a week and more at harvest, organised by the reeve; obligations varied widely by manor.

- [Elsworth: Economic history (VCH Cambridgeshire)](https://prod.british-history.ac.uk/node/8835) — British History Online (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)
- [HIST 371 lecture outline (manorialism)](https://sites.uwm.edu/carlin/?p=417) — University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)

## C11 · established
Weekly markets were granted to lords by royal charter; many were granted under Henry III (e.g. Queen Camel 1264). Many villages had no market of their own.

- [Gazetteer of Markets and Fairs in England and Wales to 1516](https://archives.history.ac.uk/gazetteer/gazweb1.html) — Institute of Historical Research / Centre for Metropolitan History (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only)
