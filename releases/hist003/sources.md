## C3 · established
The Statute of Labourers (1351), following the Ordinance of 1349, pinned wages to the customary rates of 1346-47 or the years just before the plague, and obliged the able-bodied to work.

A sudden death had to be viewed by the coroner with a jury drawn from neighbouring vills; the first finder raised the hue; burying a body before the coroner's view was punished.

## C13 · established
A felon could take sanctuary in a church for up to 40 days, then confess to the coroner and abjure the realm: assigned a port (most often Dover), a set number of days, and the king's highway, carrying a wooden cross and stripped of goods but his clothes. Leaving the highway made him liable to be killed. In Norfolk in 1353 an abjurer was given 12 days to leave from Dover.
