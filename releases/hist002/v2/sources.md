## P1 · established
Boon-works (precariae, 'bedrips'): extra harvest days owed by the whole household (custumals often exempted the wife), on top of week-work; the lord fed the workers.

## P2 · established
The reeve was usually a villein, often elected by the township for a year (Seneschaucy, c.1270s), sometimes appointed; works were counted on tally sticks alongside written accounts.

## P4 · established
Records distinguish boon-days with ale from 'dry' ones: Anglo-Norman 'ale' and 'sec' bederep ('ale bedereps', 1298); Latin precariae siccae; the Ruislip custumal (by 1248) has 'water' vs 'ale' days. 'Wet boon' itself is NOT attested and is not used.

- [bederep](https://anglo-norman.net/entry/bederep) — Anglo-Norman Dictionary (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## P6 · established
Suit of mill: tenants owed grinding at the lord's mill and paid multure, often reckoned around 1/16 (1/13 for unfree tenants in some northern manors).

## P7 · established

## P8 · established
c.1331 Abbot Richard of Wallingford forced St Albans townspeople to surrender their hand mills and set the stones into the floor of the parlour (its doorway) 'as a record and memorial' (Thomas Walsingham).

- [Memory, materiality, and trauma: revisiting the parlour floor, St. Albans, 1381 (R. M. Gammie)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13642529.2025.2473858) — Rethinking History 29:2 (2025), Taylor & Francis (accessed 2026-10-11; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## P10 · established
On 15 June 1381 rebels prised up the millstones, broke them and distributed the pieces 'as the bread that has been blessed is distributed... in the parochial churches' (Walsingham). Accounts of the aftermath vary.
