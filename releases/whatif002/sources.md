## E1 · established
Earth's surface at the equator moves east at about 1,670 km/h (about 465 m/s); the speed falls with the cosine of latitude, to about 1,180 km/h at 45 degrees and almost zero at the poles.

## E2 · established
The speed of sound in air at 20 degrees C is about 343 m/s, or about 1,235 km/h, so the equator's rotation speed is faster than sound.

- [Speed of sound in air](https://hypertextbook.com/facts/2000/CheukWong.shtml) — The Physics Factbook (hypertextbook.com) (accessed 2026-10-13; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## E3 · established
If the ground stopped, anything not attached to it (people, vehicles, oceans, the atmosphere) would keep moving east at the rotation speed (inertia).

- [What would happen if Earth stopped rotating?](https://www.maptive.com/what-would-happen-if-the-earth-stopped-rotating/) — Maptive (accessed 2026-10-13; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## E4 · estimate
Our calculation: slowing from 465 m/s to zero in one second is an average deceleration of 465 m/s squared, about 47 times the 9.81 m/s squared of gravity (about 47 g).

- [How fast does the Earth rotate?](https://www.spacecentre.nz/resources/faq/solar-system/earth/rotation-speed.html) — Space Centre NZ (accessed 2026-10-13; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## E9 · established
Tidal friction from the Moon is slowing Earth's rotation: the tidal braking alone adds about 2.3 ms per century, and eclipse records show an observed average of about 1.7-1.8 ms per century; at that rate a 25-hour day is about 200 million years away.

- [Earth's days are getting longer by two milliseconds a century](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/120604-trending-science-earths-days-are-getting-longer-by-two-milliseconds-a-century) — CORDIS, European Commission (accessed 2026-10-13; search snippet only; page not fetched)
- [Tidal friction](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Astro/tidfrict.html) — HyperPhysics, Georgia State University (accessed 2026-10-13; search snippet only; page not fetched)
