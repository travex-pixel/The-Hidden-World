Tonight at nine, gravity switches off everywhere on Earth. For ten seconds. Then it comes back. We follow those ten seconds around the world: Times Square counting down, a Sydney jogger who barely leaves the ground, San Francisco's fog taking off, every pigeon in Westminster, kangaroos at Uluru who never land, an ocean rising at one g, a Moon that barely notices, and the moment it all comes back down. Then the twist: the force that just dropped an ocean on Sydney loses to a fridge magnet.

Ten seconds of zero gravity, anywhere on Earth. What's the first thing you'd do? Comment below.

What's real: the rotation speeds, hydrostatic balance (why air and water would rise), a skydiver's terminal velocity, the kangaroo record, the Moon's orbital speed and gravity's weakness are sourced below. The big numbers (one metre, 300 m, 490 m) are our own arithmetic and are labelled ESTIMATE on screen; what the air and sea do when gravity returns, and what Earth's interior would do, are labelled HYPOTHETICAL. Gravity cannot switch off, and everyone in the film is fictional.
