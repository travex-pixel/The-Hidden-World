## G1 · established
Earth's surface moves east with the spin at about 1,670 km/h at the equator, falling with the cosine of latitude (about 1,390 km/h at Sydney's 34 degrees south).

## G3 · estimate
Our calculation: with gravity gone, the spin's unbalanced centripetal demand (v squared over r, about 0.023 m/s squared at Sydney) would lift a standing person about 1.2 m in ten seconds.

- [How fast does the Earth rotate?](https://www.spacecentre.nz/resources/faq/solar-system/earth/rotation-speed.html) — Space Centre NZ (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## G4 · estimate
Our calculation from hydrostatic balance (dP/dz = -rho g): the upward pressure-gradient force on air and water is normally balanced by gravity, so without gravity both accelerate upward at about 9.8 m/s squared: 98 m/s and about 490 m after ten seconds.

- [Hydrostatic equilibrium of atmosphere](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node206.html) — University of Texas at Austin (R. Fitzpatrick) (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)
- [The atmosphere's pressure structure: hydrostatic equilibrium](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Meteorology_and_Climate_Science/Fundamentals_of_Atmospheric_Science_(Brune)/02%3A_Thermodynamics/2.02%3A_The_Atmospheres_Pressure_Structure_-_Hydrostatic_Equilibrium) — Penn State METEO 300 / LibreTexts (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## G5 · established
A belly-to-earth skydiver's terminal velocity is about 54-55 m/s (roughly 200 km/h); an updraft faster than that would carry a person upward.

## G7 · estimate
Our calculation: a body accelerating upward at 9.8 m/s squared rises about 310 m in eight seconds and 490 m in ten; after gravity returns it coasts higher before falling back. Presented as a cartoon, not a model of a real ocean.

- [Hydrostatic equilibrium of atmosphere](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node206.html) — University of Texas at Austin (R. Fitzpatrick) (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## G8 · established
The Moon's mean orbital speed is 1.022 km/s (NASA fact sheet); in ten seconds it travels about 10 km, so a straight-line drift over ten seconds is a negligible change to an orbit about 384,000 km across.

- [Moon Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html) — NASA NSSDC (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## G10 · established
Gravity is the weakest of the four fundamental forces; a small magnet can lift a paperclip against the gravitational pull of the entire Earth.

- [How weak is gravity?](https://ova.it.com/fundamental-forces/how-weak-is-gravity) — ova.it.com (fundamental forces explainer) (accessed 2026-10-14; search snippet only; page not fetched)
