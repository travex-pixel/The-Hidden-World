## W5 · estimate
Our calculation: humans and many animals prefer to walk at a Froude number v^2/(gL) of about 0.25. Taking her leg length as about 18 m (roughly 0.53 of her 33.86 m heel-to-head height), v = sqrt(0.25 x 9.81 x 18) = 6.6 m/s, about 24 km/h. New York to Paris is about 5,837 km great-circle, so about 245 hours, or ten days, without stopping. Water drag and the Froude rule's limits for very large walkers are ignored.

- [Statue Statistics](https://www.nps.gov/stli/learn/historyculture/statue-statistics.htm) — National Park Service (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)
- [Should shorter legs take quicker strides? (Froude number and walking speed)](https://runningmagazine.ca/?p=6382) — Canadian Running (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)
- [Dynamic similarity in walking (Fr ≈ 0.25 at preferred speed)](https://www.sfu.ca/locomotionlab/assets/dynsimwalk1997.pdf) — Simon Fraser University Locomotion Lab (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)
- [New York to Paris flight distance](https://airadvisor.com/en/routes/new-york-to-paris-flight-time) — AirAdvisor (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## W8 · estimate
Our calculation: sea pressure rises about one atmosphere per 10 m (NOAA), so about 380 atmospheres at 3,800 m. A hollow statue open to the sea would fill with water and the pressure inside would match the outside, so the thin skin would not be crushed; this is our reasoning, not a tested result.

- [Water pressures at ocean depths](https://pmel.noaa.gov/eoi/nemo1998/education/pressure.html) — NOAA Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)

## W15 · disputed
Carl Lipo and Terry Hunt proposed (2011-12, with a 2025 follow-up study) that Rapa Nui's moai were 'walked' upright with ropes, rocking side to side; a replica was moved this way in tests. Other archaeologists say this does not prove it happened.

- [Easter Island's statues actually 'walked', and physics backs it up](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/5830/easter-islands-statues-actually-walked-and-physics-backs-it-up) — Binghamton University (accessed 2026-10-15; search snippet only; page not fetched)
