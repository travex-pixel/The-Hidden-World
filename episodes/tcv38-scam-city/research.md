# Case File 38: Myanmar's scam cities (Myawaddy, KK Park, Shwe Kokko)
Research ledger, verified 2026-10-13. Episode id `tcv38-scam-city` (short cut, 44–52 s, "Danger Zones" series).
This is an ONGOING situation: no individual is accused on screen.

## Method and limits
- Web search extracts only (12 searches, "standard" mode). WebFetch/curl are blocked, so no article was read in full.
  Every narrated claim rests on at least two independent search-result extracts. Primary UN/FBI documents themselves
  were not reachable; their figures are taken from the UN's own news pages (news.un.org, UNOG newsroom) and from at least
  one independent outlet reporting the same figure.
- Reuters and BBC articles did not surface directly; AP (syndicated via local stations and NPR), AFP (via Arab News,
  Malay Mail), VOA, The Record, Bangkok Post, OCCRP, the U.S. Treasury and the UN were used.

## Fun fact (one card)
- **The compounds ran on smuggled satellite internet: after the KK Park raid, SpaceX said it had disabled more than
  2,500 Starlink kits near suspected scam centres in Myanmar** (Starlink is not licensed there; the army said it seized
  30 terminals at KK Park). Arab News/AFP; The Daily Star; Gulf News. Typed only, no logo.

## Lesson (one card)
- **A stranger who texts by mistake, gets friendly, then talks crypto is a scam. Don't reply; never move money to a
  platform they suggest.** FBI Miami warning (NBC 6); Massachusetts Office of Consumer Affairs (mass.gov "wrong number
  texts" alert); AARP.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "Thousands released after the power cut" as direct cause: the narration says only that Thailand cut power and that
  thousands were freed (in the same month's crackdown); sources tie the releases to the coordinated crackdown.
- "Thousands fled to Thailand" after the October raid: AP says 1,500+; not narrated (card avoids a number).
- Worldwide loss totals (UNODC $18–37 bn for 2023; $88–114 bn for 2025 across East/Southeast Asia, Australia, NZ):
  secondary coverage only and regional; the FBI IC3 U.S. figure is used instead.
- The October 2026 Irrawaddy headline ("Myanmar fraud empire rebuilds"): headline only, not used.
- Claims that compounds had karaoke bars/shops/hospitals: not verified in two extracts; not used.

## Ethics decisions
- No faces of real people; all workers are generic figures of several nationalities shown as victims (bowed, walking
  out free), never as villains. No guards with weapons, no violence, no injuries. Demolition shows empty buildings.
- No individual named or drawn (ongoing situation). The single named entity (KNA) is typed, attributed, with its denial.
- Phone UI, chat bubbles and the fake "trading app" are our own generic designs, no app or brand logos.
- Hook framing: the danger is the specific compounds on the border, not Myanmar or its people.
