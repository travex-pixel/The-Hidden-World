# Case File 62: Hollywood's favorite conman (tcv62-abagnale-con)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Status: Frank Abagnale (born 27 April 1948) is a living person. He has never accepted the critics' findings. Nothing in this episode states as fact anything that is only Alan C. Logan's argument: the doubts are attributed ("he claimed", "an author checked the records", "records say", "his fans say") and his rebuttal is on screen.

## Method and limits
- 15 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are not used, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page, book or archive record was read.
- The search tool could not reach The Guardian, BBC, NYT, AP, LA Times, The Atlantic or The New Yorker (blocked to the crawler). The brief's "CBS / NPR / Guardian coverage" of the doubts could therefore **not be verified**; what was found: WHYY (an NPR member station) interview with Logan; Boing Boing (2021); the Pretend podcast (Javier Leiva, 2022); Wikipedia (Frank Abagnale; Great Meadow Correctional Facility); Attrition.org's errata project; book reviews (Portland Book Review, Goodreads). CBS and The Washington Post were found only for the film and his consulting career, not for the doubts.
- The prison dates rest on New York State Archives records as reported by Logan and repeated by Wikipedia, Pretend and Attrition.org. They are presented as "records" found by "an author", never as our own finding.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **San Francisco Chronicle:** the early doubts came from the Chronicle's **Stephen Hall in 1978** (after Abagnale's Tonight Show appearance), not a 2002 piece (WHYY; Wikipedia). Ira Perry of The Daily Oklahoman also wrote a line-by-line debunking at the time (WHYY). **Not narrated** (time); recorded here.
- **Paula Parks:** the brief called her a Pan Am flight attendant. Sources do **not** support that: Logan (WHYY) says Abagnale, dressed as a **TWA** pilot "for a few weeks", befriended a flight attendant, Paula Parks, and followed her around; another extract calls her a Delta flight attendant. Her airline is disputed and she is a private individual, so she is **not named or shown**.
- **"Abagnale acknowledged embellishment":** the reported wording (his 2002 website: co-writer Stan Redding interviewed him "about four times" and "over-dramatized and exaggerated" parts) was found only second-hand (a Goodreads thread, Looper, Wikipedia). **Not quoted** (fails the two-independent-source rule for a quote); paraphrased in the ledger only. The book's own disclaimer (names, dates and places changed) is used instead as the fun fact.
- **Pan Am:** he was never employed by Pan Am (Wikipedia). His documented federal case involved fake Pan Am payroll checks (ten checks; "less than $1,500"; one mirror gives $1,448.60). The exact amount is single-sourced: **not used**.
- **FBI:** Logan says the FBI has made no public statement about Abagnale's work for it (WHYY). The ending therefore says "He became a paid fraud consultant" (the Washington Post's 2002 wording) (Washington Post 2002, AARP) and does **not** claim FBI work.
