# Research ledger: Case File 37, the cocaine plane crash in Papua New Guinea (tcv37-png-plane)

Verified: 2026-10-13. Legal status as of 13 October 2026.

## Method and limits
- 10 WebSearch calls (standard mode). WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so everything below is from **search-result extracts only**, not full pages. Every claim used in narration or on a card has at least two independent sources.
- Sources: Australian Federal Police (2020 and 2026 media releases), ACIC and ABF joint releases, BBC News, Australian Aviation, The New Daily (AAP), InQueensland, The National (PNG), Asia Pacific Report, PINA, Islands Business, RNZ (via Kaniva Tonga), PacLII (the PNG Supreme Court record), Wikipedia (transport), Oxford Business Group, AAP.

## Established facts
- **26 July 2020:** a light aircraft (reported as a Cessna 402C, a light twin) left Mareeba Airport, Queensland, for PNG. The AFP alleged it flew at about 3,000 ft to avoid radar detection. (AFP 2020; ACIC; Australian Aviation)
- It landed at a remote, makeshift airstrip at Papa Lea Lea (also written Papa-Lealea), north / north-west of Port Moresby, Central Province. It **crashed while attempting to take off**, into trees at the end of the runway. (AFP 2020 and 2026; BBC; Asia Pacific Report; InQueensland)
- **Load:** 28 bags; 611.1 kg of cocaine in the PNG court allegations (PNG's police minister also said 611 kg); early AFP/police statements said "more than 500 kg". (PINA; Islands Business; Asia Pacific Report; BBC; AFP 2020)
- **Value:** the AFP (2020) estimated it at "more than $80 million". Later figures differ: A$141 million (PNG court reporting) and "about $800 million at the time" (ABF/AFP 2026). Narration uses only "over 80 million dollars, police said".
- **AFP on the cause:** the AFP said it "cannot rule out that the weight of the cocaine had an impact on the plane's ability to take off" (attributed; not a finding). (AFP 2020 via Australian Aviation; The New Daily)
- The wreck was found empty; the pilot was missing. (InQueensland; BBC; The National)
- **28 July 2020:** the pilot, David John Cutmore (Australian, of Melbourne), presented himself at Australia's mission in Port Moresby (reported as the High Commission by some outlets and the consulate by others); he was arrested and charged with illegal entry (Migration Act s16(1)(a); no passport). He pleaded guilty and was fined K3,000. Later reports say he was also ordered to pay K37,000 in total over Civil Aviation Act charges. (InQueensland; PINA; The National "Pilot found, charged" and "Aust pilot fined K3,000"; Asia Pacific Report)
- **October 2022:** the PNG National Court at Waigani (Justice Teresa Berrigan) sentenced him to **18 years** after he pleaded guilty to money laundering by receiving criminal property (the cocaine), under the Money Laundering and Counter Terrorist Financing Act. Court reporting said he was promised A$1 million. (PINA; Islands Business)
- **15 January 2025:** the PNG Supreme Court dismissed his appeal against sentence (Cutmore v State [2025] PGSC 4; SC2684). (PacLII; RNZ via Kaniva Tonga)
- **Australia:** five men were charged in 2020 (Melbourne-based syndicate with alleged links to Italian organised crime; arrests in Queensland and Victoria; all faced life). On **16 July 2026**, members of the syndicate were sentenced to a **combined 54 years** for conspiring to import the cocaine. (AFP 2026; ABF 2026; Australian Aviation 2026; AFP 2020)
- The cocaine never reached Australia. (AFP 2020 and 2026)

## Allegations / unresolved discrepancies (not narrated)
- The 2026 release, as extracted, describes three men (22 years, 21 years and 7 years, which adds to 50), against a "combined 54 years" headline. A fourth sentence or a different breakdown may exist. Narration says only "syndicate members got 54 years between them". The breakdown and the count are not used.
- The pilot's age (51, 52 or 57) varies by report and is not used. Whether he also faced charges in Australia was not confirmed in the extracts and is not used.
- "High Commission" or "consulate": the card says "Australia's mission in Port Moresby".

## Fun fact with sources
- **Port Moresby, PNG's capital, has no road link to Lae, the second-largest city (nor to the Highlands); air links much of the country.** (Wikipedia, Transport in PNG; The National, "Opposition to road illogical"; Oxford Business Group 2020)

## Lesson with sources
- Offers of big money to carry or move a package are how drug syndicates recruit couriers. The AFP's warning: "If it's too good to be true, it probably is." (AFP 2023, fake UN drug-mule scam; AAP 2023) Context: court reporting said the pilot was promised A$1 million.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- That the pilot was named "David Charles Sydney": the sources name him David John Cutmore. He is not named in the video.
- That the plane was a single-engine Cessna: it was reported as a Cessna 402C, a light twin, so it is drawn as a generic light twin.
- That the cocaine definitely caused the crash: this is only the AFP's "cannot rule out", and it is attributed.
- The $800 million and A$141 million values, and the "worth $80 million" phrasing without attribution.
- Who the people loading the bags were: "two locals" in court reporting. They are not named or characterised, and are drawn as one anonymous silhouette.

## Ethics decisions
- The pilot is a real, convicted person. He is shown only as a silhouette, never with a face, is tagged NOT A LIKENESS, and is not named in narration, on cards or in the caption.
- The judge is a generic silhouette. PNG and Australian police are generic figures with no badges or crests. PNG officers have locally appropriate skin tones and plain uniforms, with no caricature.
- No drugs are shown; the bags are plain wrapped sacks. Nobody was hurt in the crash, and the crash shows no fire or injury (tag "NOBODY WAS HURT").
- Framing: the "danger zone" is the specific situation (smugglers using a remote bush strip), not Papua New Guinea or its people. The fun fact explains why such strips exist.
- The plane has no registration, logo or lettering, and the gate has no flag or emblem. All cards are our own typed graphics.
