## Reported / attributed (narrated with "Reportedly" or on a card with attribution)
- **Two years of surveillance.** PNG's police minister said in 2020 that the AFP had run a "covert two-year surveillance operation" on the syndicate (Cairns Post; Asia Pacific Report); VICE reported the gang was already being watched as part of a two-year investigation. Narration: "Police were already watching them. Reportedly, for two years." Card: "'TWO-YEAR' WATCH, PNG'S POLICE MINISTER SAID". The minister also said the AFP asked PNG police for help in May 2020 after intelligence that the syndicate planned to land at a remote strip (not used).
- **Travel north (allegation, card only).** The Queensland Joint Organised Crime Taskforce alleged the group travelled from Melbourne and Sydney to Atherton between 19 and 25 July 2020 and arranged for the Cessna to fly illegally from Mareeba. Card: "Police alleged the group came north to Atherton". (AFP 2020; ACIC 2020; The Express)
- **Organised-crime links: NOT USED.** The AFP said the syndicate had "alleged links to Italian organised crime"; one outlet named a specific mafia group. Neither is narrated or carded (allegation, not part of the convictions as extracted).

## Statistic (counter)
- **6.8 tonnes of cocaine in one year.** ACIC National Wastewater Drug Monitoring Program, Report 24: estimated national consumption of cocaine in the year to August 2024 was a record 6.8 tonnes, up 69 % on the previous year (ACIC Report 24 snapshot; Al Jazeera 15 Aug 2025; InDaily/AAP 15 Aug 2025). Report 25 (year to Aug 2025, published 2026) says cocaine rose a further 17 % and was again a record, but its tonnage was not in the extracts, so the Report 24 figure is used with its period on the card ("year to Aug 2024").
- **Card arithmetic:** 6,800 kg / 611.1 kg (the plane's load per the PNG court) = 11.1 → "≈ 11 PLANELOADS LIKE THIS ONE".

## Fun fact (SUCCESs)
- **The cocaine did reach Australia, eventually, to be burned.** It was sent to the AFP for forensic testing and storage in Australia and incinerated at a secure facility in Queensland on 28 November 2024, watched by senior PNG and Australian police; the PNG court had ordered its destruction. (Australian High Commission PNG; Islands Business; PINA)

## Lesson (save card, for the viewer)
- AFP/ABF 2026 joint warning to young Australians: syndicates recruit "suitcase couriers", mostly under 30, give instructions over encrypted platforms, and offer about $30,000-$60,000; the maximum penalty for importing border-controlled drugs is life imprisonment (National Tribune; Mirage News, both carrying the joint release). AFP 2021: a substantial payment for simply receiving and passing on goods should raise alarm bells. Crime Stoppers: 1800 333 000.
- Rows: "BIG CASH TO CARRY A BAG" / "ORDERS VIA ENCRYPTED APP" / "MAX PENALTY: LIFE".

## Discrepancies and claims NOT used
- **The 54-year breakdown.** Extracts of the 2026 release give a Niddrie man, 39: 22 years (13 non-parole) and a Maribyrnong man, 37: 21 years (12 non-parole); the third term was not in any extract I could get (the brief's "7 years" would make 50, not 54), and one summary called 54 years the combined term of four men in Australia. Unresolved, so only "54 years between them" is said; no count of men is given for the sentences.
- **Names.** The releases' extracts identify the sentenced men by suburb and age only. They are not named (convicted, but not named in two independent sources found, and names add nothing).
- **Value.** $80 million (AFP 2020) vs about $800 million (AFP/ABF 2024-2026) vs A$141 million (PNG court reporting): no value is used in Part 2.
- **The four PNG men** sentenced to 18-19 years in Sept 2024 (AFP 2026; RNZ): not used (time).
- "Bosses": not used. The convictions are for conspiracy; one man was charged in 2020 with directing a criminal syndicate, but the outcome of that charge is not in the extracts. The end card says "PLOTTERS" (conspirators).
