## Method and limits
- Web search extracts only (standard search mode, 18 October 2026). Full article pages were not opened; every URL below is a
  search-result page whose extract carried the fact. Each narrated claim maps to at least two independent outlets.
- Wire copy counted once: AP via Fox News and Courthouse News = ONE source (AP); The Local France's story is built on AFP /
  France Info. The Drinks Business and Wine List Confidential both cite Le Point for the Taillevent theft, so for that item
  the independent sources are effectively Le Point (via two trade outlets) plus the Daily Mail; this is the thinnest item
  and the narration keeps only what all of them carry (a hole from next door, a rod, about 20 bottles, about EUR 50,000).
- Newest reporting checked on 18 October 2026: no arrests reported for any of the three thefts.

| claim | sources |
|---|---|

- "A wealthy resident's cellar" (the brief): the sources say a private cellar of an apartment building; the owner is not
  named or described, so the reel says "a private cellar" and does not draw or describe the owner.
- An exact 2017 date: reports were published 29-30 August 2017, but the prosecutor said the break-in could have happened
  any time from late July; the reel says only "2017". (One aggregator says "early hours of Tuesday 29 August": not used.)
- Bottle values per bottle ("up to EUR 1,000 each") and the exact wines: not consistently reported; not used.
- Maison Rostang (Paris, July 2019, a hole cut in the cellar wall, about 150 bottles, about EUR 400,000-600,000): verified
  but not used (too similar to item 2); a candidate for a later part.
- Kurniawan in this episode: kept for Part 3 instead (it is a fake-wine fraud, not a break-in, and fits a "fakes" part).
- Any suggestion that "cataphiles" (catacomb explorers) were involved: not reported; not used.
