## Established facts
### 1 · The pool-table tunnel (Douglas, May 1990)
| claim | sources |
|---|---|
| In May 1990 law enforcement (Mexican federal police and U.S. Customs) raided a house in Agua Prieta, Mexico, and a warehouse in Douglas, Arizona, linked by a tunnel (about 200-270 ft; sources vary, so no length is narrated). Card "DOUGLAS, ARIZONA · MAY 1990". Narration: "First, 1990: a secret tunnel under a pool table." | 99% Invisible; NBC News (2018, the tunnel video shown at a federal trial); Houston Chronicle (2014, via PressReader); Longreads (2014); Tucson Citizen (1999) |
| The entrance was in the house's game room, under a pool table that sat on a concrete floor panel (about 10 x 10 ft) lifted by a hydraulic system; the tunnel had electric lights. Narration: "Hydraulics lifted the floor, pool table and all." | Houston Chronicle (pool table "set atop a 10-by-10 foot concrete panel", the panel "rose into the air like a hydraulic lift"); 99% Invisible ("a hydraulic system to lift away flooring that was covered by a pool table"); Longreads ("hydraulic system ... trapdoor underneath a pool table") |
| The trigger: the Houston Chronicle says an officer turned a faucet knob; Longreads says a hidden lever. Two versions, so the narration names neither (no "tap" joke). | Houston Chronicle; Longreads |
| The tunnel's architect was later convicted and sentenced to 18 years (U.S. Attorney). Not narrated; nobody is named or drawn. | Lawfuel (U.S. Attorney release); Longreads |

### 3 · The elevator tunnel (Tijuana -> Otay Mesa; announced January 2020)
| claim | sources |
|---|---|
| Announced 29 January 2020: the longest smuggling tunnel ever found on the U.S. Southwest border, about 4,309 ft (Border Patrol), from an industrial building in Tijuana to the Otay Mesa area of San Diego. (A Border Patrol figure of 4,068 ft is measured from the border; "over three-quarters of a mile" in total.) Narration: "Then came the longest one ever found." / counter "4,309 FT". | AP (via ABC News 4 / KTVU / Fox 9 / Miami Herald); NBC News; Border Report (Nexstar); CBC; El Universal; La Prensa San Diego (CBP text) |
| An average of about 70 ft underground, well below the border wall; about 5.5 ft tall and 2 ft wide. Narration: "4,309 feet. 70 feet under the border." | AP; Border Report; CBC |
| It had a rail and cart system, forced-air ventilation, high-voltage electrical cables and panels, a drainage system and an ELEVATOR at the entrance (Border Patrol). Narration (hook): "Smugglers built a border tunnel with an elevator." / "Rails, power, and an elevator at the entrance." | AP; Fox News; Border Report; La Prensa (CBP: "an elevator at each entrance"); NBC News |
| Found in August 2019; Mexican law enforcement found the entrance in a small industrial building in Tijuana; U.S. investigators then mapped it. Card "TIJUANA · FOUND AUG 2019". | AP; El Universal; NBC News |
| Agents found several hundred sandbags blocking the suspected former exit in the Otay Mesa warehouse district. FUN FACT card "ITS SUSPECTED OLD EXIT: / BLOCKED WITH / HUNDREDS OF SANDBAGS". Narration: "The old exit? Blocked with sandbags." | AP; Border Report; Fox News |
| An incomplete offshoot (a side branch that stops after several hundred feet) suggested smugglers were building a replacement exit. Closing line: "The new exit was still not finished." | AP; Border Report; Fox News |
| No arrests or drug seizures were reported at the announcement; a Border Patrol agent said it was "exclusively a drug tunnel" in his experience (quote not used). No later arrest found (18 Oct 2026). "Smugglers built" is the reported framing (a smuggling tunnel); no person or group is named. | AP; Border Report |
| The previous record was a San Diego tunnel found in 2014 (2,966 ft per AP; another figure 3,259 ft). Not narrated (sources differ). | AP; Border Report |

### The statistic and the fun fact
- Statistic (count-up): 4,309 FT, the longest smuggling tunnel found on the Southwest border (Border Patrol via AP; Border Report; NBC News).
- Fun fact (SUCCESs: simple, unexpected, concrete): its suspected old exit was blocked with hundreds of sandbags (AP; Border Report).

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "With an elevator" for the 2014 or 2016 tunnels: not checked, so the episode does not say the 2020 tunnel was the only one with an elevator.
- The faucet vs. lever trigger of the 1990 lift (sources differ); the 1990 tunnel's length (200-270 ft) and its link to a named cartel boss (prosecutors showed its video at his trial; 99% Invisible says a direct link was not proven): not narrated.
- Meth and heroin amounts in 2022, the 2014 record length, the "4,068 ft from the border" figure: not narrated.
- "Cartel" for the 2020 tunnel: no source names who built it, so the narration says "smugglers".

## Ethics decisions
- No person is named or drawn as an offender: the 1990 architect (convicted) and the 2022 six (charged) are never shown; the
  2020 builders are unknown. The single figure near a tunnel at work is not drawn; only agents/officers appear, as generic
  figures with no badge, patch, seal or flag, holding only flashlights.
- No weapon, no violence. The drugs are plain taped packets. No logos; the warehouse, house and buildings are generic.
- Places named on cards only where sourced (Douglas, Agua Prieta, Otay Mesa, Tijuana).
