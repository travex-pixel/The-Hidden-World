  in extract" is recorded in `episode.json`.
- No quote, date, ruling or source was invented. The only words quoted on screen are "no doubt" (Massachusetts Attorney

- "No report found" means the searches returned nothing. It does not prove that nothing happened.

## Status (closed historical case)

- Albert DeSalvo was **never tried** for any of the Strangler killings. He was convicted in January 1967 of unrelated
  armed robberies and sexual assaults (the "Green Man" case) and sentenced to life. He was killed in Walpole State Prison
  in November 1973 (25 or 26 Nov; sources differ); no one was charged with his death.
- In July 2013 Suffolk County DA Dan Conley, Attorney General Martha Coakley and Boston Police announced that DNA linked
  DeSalvo to the January 1964 murder of **Mary Sullivan** (19). That link concerns **her case only**. No DNA survives from
  the other cases, officials said, and whether DeSalvo committed all of the killings is still debated.
- No newer development was found. No one else has been charged in any of the thirteen deaths.

## Myths and claims NOT used

- **"DNA proved DeSalvo was the Boston Strangler."** False as a general statement. The DNA links him to Mary Sullivan's
  murder only. The narration says exactly that.
- **"DeSalvo confessed to 13 killings" vs "11".** Sources differ (Biography: 13; NBC: 11). The narration says only that he
  confessed "to the killings".
- **The 2001 private DNA tests** (by forensic scientist James Starrs, on exhumed remains) that reportedly found other male
  DNA not matching DeSalvo. Reported via the NYT (Yahoo extract) and Elaine Sharp (AJC); superseded by the 2013 official
  testing. Not narrated, to avoid confusion.
- **Coakley's "most likely responsible for the others".** Opinion; not used.
- **Victim count beyond Sullivan, names of the other victims, the methods, crime-scene details, "semen" or any graphic
  term.** Not used. No victim is named except Mary Sullivan.
- **The exact date of the bottle collection.** Not found (the lab work resumed in 2012; the match was announced 11 July
  2013), so no date is given for it.
- **"Strangler Bureau" / Edward Brooke's 1964 task force.** Verified (Wikipedia, EBSCO) but cut for length.
- **The nephew whose bottle was collected:** his name appears in some reports; it is deliberately not recorded here.

- **Victims:** never depicted. Mary Sullivan is named respectfully, because she is central to the DNA result, which her
  family (her nephew Casey Sherman) publicly welcomed. No other victim is named; the memorial card reads "In memory of the

  is noted on screen.
- **Casey Sherman** is not named on screen or in narration ("her nephew"); he is a public author whose words were widely
  reported. He is not depicted.
- **Places:** the 1960s Boston street, the 2013 street and the cemetery are generic illustrations, tagged as such. No coffin

- **No imitation** of police, court, DA or newspaper material, logos or real documents; all cards are the series' own
  typed graphics. The water bottle is a plain generic bottle with a blank label.
- **Fairness:** the accuracy limit (Sullivan only; all thirteen debated) is narrated, and the DeSalvo family's dispute is on
