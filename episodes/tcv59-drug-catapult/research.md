# Case File 59: The drug catapult (tcv59-drug-catapult)

Research ledger, 2026-10-16 (all accesses on this date). Status: nobody was ever identified or charged for the catapult; the smugglers fled and no arrests were reported.

## Method and limits
- 14 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are not used, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read.
- AP's story was syndicated by NBC News, CBS News and the Honolulu Star-Advertiser; Reuters' by Arab News. These are counted as **two wire services** (AP, Reuters) plus independent outlets that reported on their own or with the video (Christian Science Monitor, NPR, Popular Science, The Register, PolitiFact 2017).
- No BBC piece was returned by search; none is cited. No Border Patrol press release was returned for the catapult; the Border Patrol spokesman's words are cited through Reuters and AP.
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support, and the disagreement is recorded below.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Place:** near Naco, Arizona / Naco, Sonora: confirmed (AP, Reuters, CSM). NPR's write-up says "south of Bisbee", which is the same area (Naco lies just south of Bisbee). The camera system was operated "at the Naco Border Patrol Station".
- **Date:** the launches were seen on the evening of **Friday 21 January 2011** (CSM: "Jan. 21"; AP: "Friday evening", story dated Wednesday 26 January). Confirmed: January 2011.
- **Who watched:** National Guard troops operating a remote video surveillance system for the Border Patrol: confirmed (AP via NBC/CBS; CSM; Popular Science).
- **Height:** the brief said "~10 ft". AP quotes a Mexican army officer: **"3-yard tall"** (9 ft); the Star-Advertiser headline extract says 10 ft; CSM says 9 ft. The screen says **"3-YARD"**.
- **Mounting:** on a **flatbed (trailer) towed by an SUV**, about **20 yards** from the border (AP). "Powered with elastic" (NPR / Tucson Sentinel). Confirmed.
- **Quantity seized:** **disputed.** About **45 lb** (NBC, Reuters via Arab News, CSM, PolitiFact) vs **35 lb** (AP via CBS; The Register "35lbs (16kg)"). The narration gives no seized weight; the end label says "About 45 lb (AP: 35)".
- **Getaway:** "the smugglers fled" (all). NBC alone says they fled in a Humvee: **not used** (single source).
- **"Medieval":** our own description of a catapult (NPR's headline: "Drug smugglers get medieval"). Not attributed to any official.

## Ethics decisions
- The smugglers were never identified: anonymous dark silhouettes, no face, ethnicity or costume suggested.
- No weapon is drawn or pointed; the soldiers carry only torches. No real force's insignia, flag or logo.
- Marijuana shown only as plain taped bundles and sealed cans. The camera frame in the hook is our own generic graphic, not the Guard's footage.
- Tone: absurd and ironic; no mockery of Mexico or Mexicans; the soldiers who seized the device are the competent ones.
