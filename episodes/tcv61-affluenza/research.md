# Case File 61: The affluenza case (tcv61-affluenza)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Legal status verified on 2026-10-17: Ethan Couch was adjudicated in juvenile court (intoxication manslaughter, December 2013), his case was moved to adult court in 2016, he served 720 days in the Tarrant County jail and was released on probation on 2 April 2018. Nothing here is an open charge.

## Method and limits
- 12 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read. AP copy is cited through the outlets that ran it (OKC Fox, Fox News, The Globe and Mail, Boston.com, KY3).
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support; disagreements are listed below.
- Nature: **fact** = reported consistently by independent outlets or a court record; **prosecutors' figure** / **testimony** / **report** = attributed on screen where it matters.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Victims' names:** confirmed: Breanna Mitchell, 24 (her SUV had stalled), and three people who stopped to help her: Hollie Boyles, 52, her daughter Shelby Boyles, 21, and youth pastor Brian Jennings, 41 (Wikipedia; KVUE profile; FOX 4 / FOX 5, April 2018). Nine others were reported injured.
- **BAC 0.24:** confirmed as a **prosecutors' figure** from a test about three hours after the crash, "three times the legal limit" (AP via OKC Fox; FOX 13/CNN; Wikipedia). Valium (diazepam) and marijuana were also reported; **not used**.
- **Date and place:** 15 June 2013, Burleson-Retta Road, Burleson, Texas (Wikipedia; KVUE). The pickup was his father's (reported as a red 2012 Ford F-350); the make is **not** drawn or said.
- **Sentence:** ten years' probation with residential treatment, **no jail time**, Judge Jean Boyd, juvenile court, December 2013 (extracts give 10 or 11 December; the screen says "Dec 2013"). Prosecutors had sought the maximum, 20 years.
- **"Affluenza":** the term came from defense psychologist Dr G. Dick Miller's testimony (affluent, permissive parents; "freedoms no young person should have"; no boundaries). The **judge did not say** affluenza was the basis of her decision; Couch's lawyer later said the same. "Affluenza" is not a recognised diagnosis (APA, per The Globe and Mail/AP); Miller later regretted using the word. The hook's "He grew up too rich" is the framing of the defense argument, immediately grounded by the testimony line.
- **Beer pong video:** a six-second video posted on Twitter in December 2015 appeared to show him at a party where beer pong was played (CBS Texas, FOX 4, The Root); the DA investigated; the extracts say the footage was unclear and not confirmed as him at the time. He then missed a probation appointment (10 Dec 2015). The screen says only "DEC 2015: A PARTY VIDEO".
- **How they were found:** the brief offered "a phone call ordering pizza / a cell phone". Confirmed version: the **Jalisco state prosecutors' report** said a U.S. Marshals Service agent tipped Mexican authorities to the location of a **phone that had been used to order Domino's pizza** (The Globe and Mail/AP; Toronto Star; KVUE; NewsOne). They had moved from the condo by then; a tourism operator pointed agents to their new apartment, and they were detained on 28 December 2015. The card says "a phone used to order pizza led U.S. Marshals to them" with the attribution row; the narration says "helped give them away".
- **720 days:** confirmed. Judge Wayne Salvant (adult court, April 2016) ordered four consecutive 180-day jail terms, one for each person killed (NBC News; The Root; KTLA). Released 2 April 2018 from the Tarrant County jail after 720 days (NPR; KERA; CBS Texas; KY3/AP).
- **Later events:** January 2020 arrest for a positive THC test (FOX 4; Boston.com/AP) is confirmed but **not used** (it is not the outcome of this story). **2024:** no extract confirmed a 2024 arrest or a positive alcohol test, nor the end of his probation (the 2018 extracts say six more years of supervision, i.e. to about 2024, but that is an inference). **Not used.**
