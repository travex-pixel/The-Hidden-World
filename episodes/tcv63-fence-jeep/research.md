# Case File 63: The Jeep stuck on top of the border fence (tcv63-fence-jeep)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Status: nobody was identified or charged in any of the three
incidents used; in every one the people in or around the vehicle fled into Mexico and the vehicle was left behind.

## Method and limits
- 12 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are not used, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read.
- Wire copies are counted once per wire: AP (CBS News, East Idaho News photo, KXXV, KGUN), CNN (Fox13). Independent reporting: NPR, Fox News, RIA Novosti (via GlobalSecurity.org), Phoenix New Times, InSight Crime, Arizona Daily Star, Yuma Sun (reprinted by Small Wars Journal), Homeland Security Newswire, KTAR, Tucson Sentinel; official: Marine Corps Air Station Yuma (marines.mil).
- Lead Stories (2019) notes that the CBS News page for the 2012 Jeep story showed a wrong (2019) date; the event is 2012. The 2012 date is fixed from NPR (1 Nov 2012), Fox13/CNN (1 Nov 2012) and RIA Novosti (2 Nov 2012).
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support; disagreements are recorded below.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Place of the hero Jeep (corrected):** the brief said "near Yuma, Arizona". The AP photo caption and CNN say "near Yuma, Ariz."; CBS/AP and ABC put the spot near the **Imperial Sand Dunes in California, about five miles west of the Colorado River and the Arizona line**, in the Border Patrol's **Yuma Sector**. The screen says "YUMA SECTOR · OCT 2012 / Imperial Sand Dunes, near the Arizona line"; narration names no state.
- **Date:** agents spotted the Jeep early on Tuesday **30 October 2012** (CBS/AP: "early Tuesday"); the AP photo is dated Wednesday 31 October; stories ran 31 Oct–2 Nov 2012. The screen says "OCT 2012".
- **Vehicle:** a (silver) **Jeep Cherokee** (Fox13/CNN, Autoblog, Arizona Daily Star, NPR). We draw a generic, unbadged silver SUV.
- **Fence:** about **14 feet** tall (Phoenix New Times headline "14-foot fence"; Fox13/CNN "14-foot fence"; Arizona Daily Star "14-foot fence").
- **Ramps:** homemade/make-shift ramps on both sides; the SUV went up a metal ramp on the Mexican side and got "high-centered" on top while trying to go down the American-side ramp (Fox News; East Idaho News/AP; CBS).
- **People:** two men on the Mexican side were trying to free the Jeep when agents approached; they fled further into Mexico. No arrests (CBS; Fox13/CNN; RIA Novosti; East Idaho News).
- **Cargo:** the Jeep was **empty**; agents said it was probably loaded with contraband such as marijuana before (CBS; East Idaho News). Narration does not claim drugs were in it.
- **"2017 Arizona" (brief's candidate):** **not found.** No 2017 case of a vehicle stuck atop the fence turned up; the later case that verifies is **February 2019** (Cabeza Prieta, Ajo station), used as item 3.

## Ethics decisions
- Nobody was identified: the smugglers and runners are anonymous dark silhouettes, no face, ethnicity or costume suggested.
- Border Patrol agents are generic figures in plain green jackets with torches only: no badge, patch, flag or agency logo; no weapon drawn or pointed; no violence.
- The vehicles are generic, unbadged SUVs; "Jeep" appears only as spoken/plain text, never as a logo or grille design.
- Drugs appear only as plain taped bundles. Tone: absurd and ironic about failed ramps, never about Mexico or Mexicans or poverty.
- The lesson card is for the viewer himself (men 18–34), sourced to CBP and the UK FCDO.

**Editorial change (producer):** the punchline was "Every driver escaped." Because the April 2011 occupants' fate is disputed (CBS: taken into custody; MCAS Yuma / Yuma Sun: fled), it now reads "Most drivers escaped."
