# War room — Episode 009: *AI Just Beat an 80-Year-Old Maths Problem. It's About Dots.*

1. **CTO — the one true mechanism**
   - The problem: n dots on a plane; how many pairs can be exactly 1 apart? Erdős (1946) conjectured the scaled square grid's growth, n^(1+c/log log n), was the ceiling.
   - On 20 May 2026 a general-purpose OpenAI reasoning model built a family of point sets with n^(1+ε) unit distances (Sawin later: n^1.014). That growth rate eventually beats any grid, so the conjecture is false.
   - Why we can believe it: nine mathematicians published a short human-verified version the same day, and the proof was formalised in Lean, whose small kernel accepts only well-typed proof terms.
2. **CEO — five hooks (picked #1)**
   1. "You think AI does maths by looking it up. Last October, that's all it did."
   2. "A maths problem a child can understand just fell to AI."
   3. "AI beat an 80-year-old maths problem. It's about dots."
   4. "OpenAI got laughed at in October. In May, it did it for real."
   5. "Erdős was wrong. It took a machine 80 years later to show it."

   #1 opens on a belief most viewers hold (and that was literally true in October 2025), breaks it, and sets up the stakes. #3 is used as the title.
3. **Sceptic (34.7 s, middle of 55.8 s):** "It's dots. Who cares?" The script concedes it ("Nobody is building anything with this", on screen: "no product · no app · no money in it") and then shows the real implication: a wall of tens of thousands of proof lines turning green as Lean checks them. CANNOT BE BLUFFED.
