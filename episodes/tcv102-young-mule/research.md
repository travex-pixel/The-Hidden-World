# Case File 102: The Suitcase from Bangkok (ONE SMUGGLER, format v10)

Research ledger built 25 October 2026. Lane: young mule, airport. Candidate from the channel brief (row 102)
**verified and concluded**: Bella May Culley, a British woman from Billingham, Teesside, born 2006, aged **18 when
arrested** at Tbilisi International Airport, Georgia, on 10 May 2025. She **pleaded guilty under a plea agreement** and
a Tbilisi court **found her guilty on 3 November 2025**. The sentence was time served (five months and 24 or 25 days) plus a
500,000-lari fine her family paid. She was freed the same day and flew home to the UK on 4 November 2025. No later

## The story in order

1. **Before.** An 18-year-old from Billingham travels in South-East Asia in spring 2025 (the Philippines in April per
   S15; Thailand from early May). *How she came to carry a bag is not established.* Her account is coercion by a gang in
   Bangkok, which her lawyer described as British expats (S16). No source shows a social-media recruitment.
2. **The search.** She stops checking in with her mother. The family, Cleveland Police and the press treat her as a
   missing person in Thailand, last seen around Pattaya (S11, S15).
3. **The trip.** On 10 May 2025 she passes the automated passport gates at Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport (S10) and
   flies via Sharjah, UAE (S4) to Tbilisi.
4. **The find.** At Tbilisi International Airport a luggage inspection finds about 12 kg of marijuana and 2.068 kg of
   hashish in her travel bag (S1). Charged with buying, storing and importing a particularly large quantity, with up to
   20 years or life (S1, S7). Her family learns she is not missing but in a Georgian jail (S11, S12).
5. **What she said.** She pleads not guilty. In court she says she was tortured in Thailand and forced to carry the
   suitcase. Her lawyer says she was threatened with a hot iron and tried to alert officials in Thailand (S2, S3, S16).
   Thailand's immigration bureau releases CCTV and says her claims have "absolutely no factual basis" (S10), while
   acknowledging she may have been manipulated by gangs (S5). She tells the court she is pregnant (S16; later confirmed
   by all release coverage).
6. **The deal.** First terms: two years plus a 500,000-lari payment, and the family struggles to raise it (S8, S12). On 3 Nov
   2025 the terms are revised. She pleads guilty, the court finds her guilty and sentences her to time served (about six months)
   plus the 500,000-lari fine her family paid. The prosecutor cites her confession, age and condition (S2, S3, S6).
7. **After.** She walks out of court to her mother, "happy and relieved" (S6, S13), and flies home to Luton on
   4 Nov (S14). Nothing found since.

## The ONE object for frame 0

**Her travel bag / suitcase, being unzipped.** What the sources establish: the drugs were **in her luggage / travel bag**
(MIA: "travel bag", S1; AP/Reuters: "luggage", S2/S3; her lawyer and court reports: "the suitcase", S16, S12), found in a
**luggage inspection** on arrival (S1). Inside were about **12 kg of marijuana and about 2 kg of hashish** (S1, S2, S3).

Not established: the colour (the brief's "pink suitcase" is **not sourced**: draw a neutral, dark hard-shell or soft
bag), the brand, and how the drugs were packed. The "34 hermetically sealed packages + 20 of hashish" detail is
single-sourced (S18). For the art, blocks wrapped in plain plastic is a reasonable **illustrative** picture of
compressed cannabis and hashish, but it must not be narrated. Hashish = small dark-brown slabs, marijuana = green
compressed bricks. A label "ILLUSTRATION" is not needed if the caption claims nothing about the packing. Do not show a
scanner image: no source says an X-ray found it.

## Two-sided end-card question

**DUPED OR GUILTY? 👇** Both sides are on the record. **Duped:** she said a gang tortured and threatened her into
carrying the bag (S2, S3, S16), and even Thai officials allowed she may have been manipulated (S5). **Guilty:** she
pleaded guilty and a court convicted her (S2, S4), and Thai CCTV shows her passing passport control normally without
seeking help (S10). Alternative: "WOULD YOU HAVE CARRIED IT? 👇" (weaker: it invites judgement of her).

## The human beat (why an 18–24-year-old sends it)

The **missing-person flip**. Her family spent days believing an 18-year-old had vanished on a backpacking trip, and then
learned she was in a cell 4,000 miles away with a bag of drugs (S11, S12, S15). Every 18–24-year-old has a mate who is
travelling alone or about to, and a mum waiting for a text. The send line: **"SEND TO THE MATE WHO'S BACKPACKING THIS
YEAR."** (Do not use "the mate who'd carry a bag for a stranger": it implies a choice the sources do not establish
and blames the victim if her account is true.) Secondary beat: her family paid about £138,000 to bring her home (S5,
S6), which is a parent's answer.

## Ethics notes

- **Naming:** permitted. She pleaded guilty and was convicted (S2, S4, S9), was an adult (18) at the time (S1, S3), and
  is named in every outlet (AP, Reuters, Sky, PA, RFE/RL, Civil.ge). Use "Bella Culley" once, slowly. Do not use her
  middle name on the cover. Do not show a likeness: use the dark silhouette per house style (NOT A LIKENESS).
- **No victim-blaming:** present coercion as *her account*, and the CCTV as *Thailand's account*. Never call her "the
  drug mule" as a label on screen, and never "lied". No jokes about her, the pregnancy or the family.
- **Family:** her mother may be referred to as "her mum" or "her family". Do not name the parents, and do not show the
  family or reconstruct the reunion as a likeness. Describe the fine as paid "by her family" without judgement.
- **Pregnancy and child:** the pregnancy is in the court reasoning (S2, S3), so it can be said ("pregnant") once at most,
  in the consequence. Nothing about the child, its birth or paternity (S16 is single-sourced and private).
- **No minor:** she was 18 at arrest (born 2006). She was never a minor in this story.
- Do not depict Thai officials as complicit: they denied it (S10).

## Sensitive handling

- **Pregnancy:** mention it at most once (in the consequence, as the court's reasoning), and never as a punchline.
- **Torture claim:** always framed as *her* claim ("She said…"), and always followed by Thailand's response (the CCTV, "no factual basis").
- **Family:** no family names on screen or in the voice-over. Say "her family" or "her mum" only.
- **Likeness:** a dark silhouette only (NOT A LIKENESS). No face, no reconstruction of the reunion or of the family.
