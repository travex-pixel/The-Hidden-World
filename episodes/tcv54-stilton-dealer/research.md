# Case File 54: The Stilton dealer (tcv54-stilton-dealer)

Research ledger, 2026-10-16 (all accesses on this date). Legal status verified on 2026-10-16: Carl Stewart pleaded guilty and was sentenced in May 2021; the case is closed.

## Method and limits
- 14 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read. The BBC, Liverpool Echo and Guardian reports were not returned by search and are not cited; the Merseyside Police statement is cited through the outlets that quoted it.
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support, and the disagreement is recorded below.
- Nature column: **fact** = reported consistently by independent outlets or an official record; **police account** = Merseyside Police's statement, attributed on screen where it matters.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Sentence:** 13 years and six months at Liverpool Crown Court: confirmed (AP via The Republic, CBS News, The Register, FOX TV stations, KCRG/CNN, ABC6, Irish Legal News).
- **Sentencing date:** the brief said "May 2021": confirmed only to the month. The Register's report is dated 25 May 2021; a CNN report syndicated on 30 May 2021 says "last Friday". The exact day is not stated in any extract, so the screen says "MAY 2021" and no day is given.
- **Who lifted the prints:** the brief said "Merseyside Police / NCA". Extracts attribute the fingerprint work to **Merseyside Police** ("his palm and fingerprints were analysed from this picture and it was established they belonged to Stewart"). The NCA led Operation Venetic nationally; it is not credited with the fingerprint work on screen.
- **Who cracked EncroChat:** a French-Dutch joint investigation team (set up April 2020 with Eurojust, Europol participating) put "a technical device" in place to get past the encryption; interception ended on 13 June 2020. "Hacked" / "cracked" is the press wording; the narration says "cracked".
- **His age:** 39 at sentencing (all extracts). Not narrated.
- **"In his hand":** the photo showed a block of Stilton held in his hand, with no face visible (Merseyside Police via PEDESTRIAN.TV, Irish Legal News, CBS). The hero shot is an illustration of that, not the photo.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
Spoken cue: "Save this before your next post."
Rows: "FINGERTIPS IN SHARP FOCUS" / "HIDDEN LOCATION DATA" / "REFLECTIONS IN YOUR EYES"; credit row "Japan's NII · FBI · U.S. Army · Tokyo case, 2019".
- Row 1: Japan's National Institute of Informatics (Isao Echizen, January 2017) copied fingerprints from photos taken about 3 m away when fingers were in focus and well lit (Arab News https://www.arabnews.com/node/1037351 ; Malay Mail https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/life/2017/01/12/japan-researchers-warn-of-fingerprint-theft-from-peace-sign/1290881 ; Reuters archive https://reuters.screenocean.com/record/164471 ). Sceptics (Mashable extract) say the right lighting is uncommon; the row says "sharp focus", the condition the researchers gave.
- Row 2: photo files can carry GPS coordinates (EXIF); an FBI intelligence analyst warned that posted images can reveal a "pattern of life" (FBI https://www.fbi.gov/resolveuid/0f5ea92c08fe475cac1915a95a6ac351 ; Patch, FBI release https://patch.com/pennsylvania/pottstown/fbi-warns ); the U.S. Army warned that embedded photo data could tip off criminals (https://www.army.mil/article/51268/ ).
- Row 3: in 2019 a man in Tokyo found singer Ena Matsuoka's home from a station / bus stop reflected in her eyes in selfies (police account as reported by CBS News https://www.cbsnews.com/news/japan-man-accused-assault-pop-star-tracked-using-social-media-selfies-google-street-view-today-2019-10-09/ ; The Register https://www.theregister.co.uk/2019/10/10/stalker_japan_eyes ; The Next Web https://thenextweb.com/news/stalker-japan-reflection-eye-camera-phone ). The victim is not named on screen.
- No UK official (ICO / NCSC) page on photo metadata was returned by search; the brief's suggested UK sources are therefore not cited.
