# Case File 94 — Fake Watermelons Full of Meth (WHERE THEY HID IT · 5)

Research ledger, built 21 October 2026. Engine v8, lane: customs. Every case is a seizure announced by U.S. Customs and
Border Protection (CBP), so each is a settled fact (the drugs exist and were found); nobody accused is named or drawn.

## Method and limits

- Web search (standard mode), 9 queries, 21 Oct 2026. Full pages could not be opened from the build environment
  (WebFetch returned DNS errors for cbp.gov and hstoday.us), so every claim below rests on **search-result extracts**,
  cross-checked between at least two independent outlets. The CBP newsroom release for the watermelon case is cited as
  carried by HSToday and FreshPlaza, and as quoted by AP and NPR.
- Access date for every URL: 2026-10-21.
- The years of the two Pharr cases (March 2026, August 2026) come from the outlets' publication dates in the extracts
  (FOX 7 Austin dated 29 March 2026; FreightWaves dated August 2026). 27 March 2026 was a Friday, matching FOX 7's
  "CBP announced on Friday". Re-check both dates before posting.

## Corrections to the brief and claims NOT used

- **How the watermelons were spotted:** the brief suggested "the imaging scan, the dog, the weight, as reported". No
  extract mentions a dog, an imaging scan or the weight for the watermelon truck: the driver was referred to secondary
  and the cargo was offloaded. The gotcha says only that ("Officers unloaded it."). The scanner and the dog are used
  where they are reported: the leeks (both) and the blackberries (the scan).
- **"Painted" melons / "inflatable" disguises:** one outlet says the packages were painted two shades of green and one
  calls them inflatable; AP and CBP say wrapped in paper coloured to look like watermelon skins. The reel follows AP/CBP
  ("paper skins").
- **The leek value:** $10.75 million (CBP via most outlets) vs $10.8 million (KRGV): narrated as "nearly $11 million",
  labelled $10.75M · CBP. The Blaze's "95 packages" is a typo against CBP's 395; not used.
- **Hidden how, in the leeks:** CBP did not disclose how the packages were concealed, so the reel shows crates in the
  trailer and says only that the shipment was declared as leeks.
- **Blackberry gotcha wording:** the scan came first, then the physical search that extracted the packages; the label
  says NON-INTRUSIVE SCAN, THEN A SEARCH so "One scan later" is not read as the scanner counting packages.
- Not used: the KTVH line placing Otay Mesa in Arizona (wrong; it is in San Diego, California); Operation Apollo (true
  but a tangent); the September 2026 cabbage/tomatillo/cilantro and October 2026 jalapeño Pharr seizures (verified in
  one extract each only).

## Ethics decisions

- The hook states a seizure (a fact), agent-first: "Smugglers dressed meth up as watermelons." (CBP: "disguised as
  watermelons"). No accused person is in the hook. The brief's longer hook ("…five million dollars of meth…") ran 3.2 s and
  the recogniser misheard "hid" as "hit", so the number moved to the second line.
- The Otay Mesa driver is never named or drawn; one typed label says he was turned over to HSI and that no name was
  released. No arrest was announced in either Pharr case, so no driver is mentioned or drawn there.
- Officers are generic figures with no insignia; no weapons; the dog is calm and unharmed (a sitting alert).
- No logos, liveries, plates or real interfaces: the scanner image is our own grey silhouette; the manifest is our own
  blank form with one typed word.
- The joke is on the disguises (paper melons, a truck "of leeks", a dog that agreed with the scanner), never on a person.
