## Corrections to the brief
- **Forged signatures:** the brief said the fake invoices, contracts and letters with forged signatures were emailed to finance staff. Per the DOJ (as quoted in coverage), the phishing emails to staff asked for payment of invoices, while the forged invoices, contracts and letters that appeared to be signed by the victim companies' own executives (with false corporate stamps) were **submitted to banks** to support the transfers. The narration only says "he emailed their staff fake invoices"; the forgery is a fun-fact card ("HE FORGED SIGNATURES OF THEIR OWN EXECUTIVES") that does not say where the papers went.
- **Total:** the DOJ plea release says "over $100 million"; the DOJ sentencing release title says "over $120 million"; Bloomberg $121 million; others $122-123 million. Narration: "Over $100 million"; chip: "$120M+". "About $121 million" is not stated on screen as a DOJ figure.
- **Sentence date:** December 2019 (several outlets on 19-21 Dec 2019); the exact day was not confirmed in two extracts, so the episode says "In 2019" / "DEC 2019".
- **Did not use "between 2013 and 2015 Google then Facebook" as a sequence**: one extract (Bloomberg, via a secondary) puts Google's loss in 2013 and Facebook's in 2015, but only one source; the narration says "Then Facebook paid too" only as story order, and the dates card says 2013-2015.

## Fun fact (card)
"HE FORGED SIGNATURES OF / THEIR OWN EXECUTIVES." DOJ plea release (as quoted in coverage) and CNBC/Fortune 2017 both report that the forged invoices, contracts and letters appeared to be signed by executives of the victim companies. SUCCESs: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, a story.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
Spoken cue: "Paying a bill from an email? Save these three checks."
Rows: "CALL A NUMBER YOU KNOW" / "CHECK THE EXACT ADDRESS" / "URGENT? NEW BANK? STOP."; credit row "FBI · Action Fraud (UK police)".
- Verify payment requests and changed details by calling a number you already have (on file), never the number in the email: FBI BEC guide https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/private-sector-partnerships/email-compromise_508.pdf ; FBI Phoenix https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/phoenix/news/press-releases/fbi-tech-tuesday-business-email-compromise-bec-fraud ; Action Fraud "Stop, Challenge, Protect" as carried by the National Business Crime Centre https://nbcc.police.uk/crime-prevention/cyber-and-fraud/bank-mandate-fraud and PSNI https://www.psni.police.uk/safety-and-support/keeping-safe/protecting-yourself/scams/mandate-fraud .
- Check the sender's exact address: look-alike addresses and domains (FBI guide; FBI Phoenix; UK council/police guidance notes "often there is a minor change").
- Urgency and last-minute changes to bank details are red flags (FBI guide; Action Fraud / NBCC).
These apply to the viewer himself (rent, a deposit, a car purchase, a builder's invoice, a "your bank details changed" email).
