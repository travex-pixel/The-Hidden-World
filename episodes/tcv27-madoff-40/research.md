# Case File 27: Bernie Madoff, the biggest Ponzi scheme ever, ended by a confession to his sons (40-second cut): research ledger

Date: 2026-10-12. Episode id `tcv27-madoff-40`, prefix `md27`. Closed case (guilty plea 2009; subject died 2021).

## Method and limits
- 12 WebSearch calls in "standard" mode. WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so only **search-result extracts** were read, never full pages. Every source record in `episode.json` says so in `accessed`.
- Primary material located: the US Attorney's (SDNY) December 2008 arrest release and criminal complaint (justice.gov); the Justice Department's 2024 distribution release; the trustee's 2026 seventeenth-distribution press release (madofftrustee.com); a Senate Banking Committee press release on the SEC inspector general's report. Secondary: ABC News, AP (via Daily Herald, NBC News, theGrio, KOMO), CNN / CNNMoney, ProPublica, PBS NewsHour and Frontline, CBS News, Reuters graphics, The Jerusalem Post, Accounting Today, Claims Journal, MPR News, Fox News (AP), The Times of Israel (JTA), Axios, City A.M., the Massachusetts Securities Division, CounterPunch, Wikipedia (recovery page; used only for the latest recovery total).
- Not reached in extracts: the SEC OIG report itself (OIG-509) and a New York Times article on the confession. The IG findings are taken from contemporaneous news and the Senate release.

## Established facts (used in narration; claim ids in episode.json)
- **M_largest**: prosecutors / DOJ call it the largest Ponzi scheme in history (DOJ 2024 release; Axios; SDNY). Basis of the headline "THE BIGGEST PONZI SCHEME EVER".
- **M_65bn**: statements showed about $65 billion. A court (AP via Fox News) described statements in which about $17.3 billion of investments had "ballooned to more than $65 billion" as reflecting "impossible transactions"; the trustee puts lost principal at about $17.3-17.5 billion (Times of Israel/JTA; Wikipedia). Bloomberg-era reports cite about $19 billion principal; the 2008 complaint said "at least approximately $50 billion". Narration: "His clients' statements showed about $65 billion. Most of it was fake profit." Card: "ON CLIENT STATEMENTS: ABOUT $65 BILLION" / "PRINCIPAL ACTUALLY LOST: ABOUT $17.5 BILLION (TRUSTEE)".
- **M_smooth / M_no_trades**: statements showed steady gains (about 1% a month, hardly ever a losing month: Frontline; "consistent 10 percent returns... unrealistic, if not impossible": Massachusetts Securities Division); investigators found the trades on the statements were not made (CounterPunch; SEC IG coverage; SDNY). The statement on screen is our own typed design with illustrative figures and the note "OUR OWN DESIGN · ILLUSTRATIVE FIGURES".
- **M_markopolos**: Harry Markopolos took his analysis to the SEC's Boston office in 2000 (IG report via Accounting Today; Boston College profile in search results says May 2000) and warned again (2001, 2005 memo "The World's Largest Hedge Fund Is a Fraud", "highly likely" a Ponzi scheme: ProPublica; PBS; CBS). ProPublica mentions memos "starting in 1999"; narration uses the conservative "From 2000".
- **M_ig**: the SEC inspector general (David Kotz, September 2009) found the SEC missed numerous red flags, never ran a thorough investigation, and found no improper ties between staff and Madoff (Accounting Today; Claims Journal; Senate release). Narration: "The SEC's own watchdog later said it missed the red flags."
- **M_confessed / M_big_lie**: on 10 December 2008 Madoff told his sons Mark and Andrew that the business was a fraud (City A.M.; AP via Daily Herald; ProPublica; ABC). **Wording as reported**: the federal complaint says he told "senior employees" it was "all just one big lie" and "basically, a giant Ponzi scheme" (complaint via LawFuel; ABC); Andrew Madoff's later account quotes "It's all been one big lie" (AP via Daily Herald). Both versions share "one big lie", which is all the narration uses; the card says "'ONE BIG LIE' · WORDING AS REPORTED".
- **M_reported**: the sons reported him to federal authorities through their lawyers within hours (AP via Daily Herald; City A.M.; CNN 2011). The lawyer (reported elsewhere as Martin Flumenbaum) is not named: not confirmed in the extracts.
- **M_arrest**: FBI agents arrested him at his Manhattan home on the morning of 11 December 2008; charged with securities fraud (SDNY release; ABC; City A.M.). Not framed as an anniversary; card says "DECEMBER 2008".
- **M_plea**: 12 March 2009, guilty plea to 11 felony counts before Judge Denny Chin; taken into custody (ABC; Jerusalem Post; Reuters graphic).
- **M_sentence**: 29 June 2009, 150 years, the maximum (CNNMoney; NBC/AP; MPR).
- **M_died** (card): died at FMC Butner, North Carolina, 14 April 2021, aged 82 (AP via theGrio, KOMO).
- **M_recovery** (card): trustee Irving Picard has recovered more than $14 billion (about $14.6 bn by Dec 2023; $15.3 bn reported as of March 2026 by Wikipedia; trustee's 2026 release puts aggregate customer payout near $15.38 bn). Card says "MORE THAN $14 BILLION" (conservative; two sources).

## Allegations / inquiry findings (attributed)
- The SEC inspector general's findings are presented as the IG's ("its own watchdog later said...").
- Reports that investigators doubted the sons' account (anonymous officials, NBC New York) are NOT used; the sons were never charged and the narration accuses them of nothing.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "$50 billion" (the 2008 complaint's first estimate) and "$64.8 billion" exact figures: the narration uses "about $65 billion" for the statements.
- Exact Madoff wording beyond "one big lie" ("finished", "absolutely nothing", "giant Ponzi scheme"): sources differ on setting (complaint: "senior employees") and wording.
- Madoff's plans to distribute $200-300 million to employees and family before surrendering (SEC account via Bloomberg): not needed.
- That the SEC was "bribed" or "corrupt": the IG found no evidence of improper ties.
- The sons' later deaths: excluded entirely (sensitive; suicide).

## Ethics decisions
- Madoff and Markopolos are illustrated representations tagged 'ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS' (generic figures, no photo reference). Madoff is never shown with cash or any victim.
- The sons are drawn ONLY as anonymous silhouettes, never with faces, never at the arrest or in court; they are not named on screen; the narration says only that he confessed to them and that they reported him.
- No victim or investor is named or depicted. The end question ("Madoff or the regulators?") is a debate prompt; the facts (guilty plea; IG findings) are on cards.
- No real document is imitated: the account statement is our own typed design without a firm name or logo; the memo and report are blank or our own typed cards; no SEC, FBI, court or trustee material is reproduced.
- Pronunciation (offline recogniser, every line in isolation): "SEC" -> "S E C", "SEC's" -> "S E C's", "FBI" -> "F B I", "$65" -> "sixty five" + "billion" -> "billion dollars", "150" -> "one hundred fifty", "Madoff" -> "Maydoff" (the default was heard as "burning it off"). Reworded: "But the trades..." lost its first word, now "Yet the trades..."; "missed red flags" was heard as "misread flags", now "missed the red flags".
