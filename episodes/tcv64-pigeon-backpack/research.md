# Case File 64: The pigeon with a backpack (tcv64-pigeon-backpack)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Format: an escalating 3-item list of animals caught smuggling
(engagement v2). Nobody is named or depicted: no human offender was identified in any of the three cases.

## Method and limits
- 10 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are not used, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read.
- The search tool cannot reach bbc.co.uk / bbc.com / theguardian.com directly. The BBC report is cited through outlets that relay it (Citi FM, Fox 13, Fox News quoting it). **No Guardian article was found or cited.** Al-Rai (the Kuwaiti paper that broke the story) is cited through the BBC and VICE (photo credit "via Al-Rai").
- The Sri Lanka story is mostly AFP copy (Inquirer, Gulf News, The New Daily count as one wire); independent confirmations are the Sunday Observer and the Daily FT (both Sri Lankan) and VICE (citing the Aruna newspaper).
- Where extracts disagree, narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support, and the disagreement is recorded below.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **"Police arrested a pigeon"**: not what happened. **Customs officers** caught it near the customs building in **Abdali**, close to the Iraqi border (al-Rai via BBC; Fox News; IFLScience). Narration: "Kuwaiti customs caught it". No arrest of anyone is reported. "It did not ask for a lawyer" is used only as a deadpan joke.
- **Drug and count**: **178 pills** is consistent across all extracts. The drug is **disputed**: an al-Rai journalist told the BBC it was a form of **ketamine** (IFLScience headline "178 ketamine pills"); Scripps stations, VICE and Fox 13 say **ecstasy** ("almost 200 ecstasy pills"). Narration says "pills"; the label says "Reportedly a form of ketamine".
- **Date**: reported 24-26 May 2017 (Citi FM URL 2017/05; Fox 13 2017-05-25). Screen: "MAY 2017".
- **Colombia**: confirmed **Bucaramanga**, **January 2011** (The Register 19 Jan 2011; Colombia Reports; Time). Load about **45 g** (40 g marijuana + 5 g cocaine paste, called "bazuco" in the wire copy). Police said it was **too heavy to clear the walls** (Time / Telegraph; commander Jose Angel Mendoza on the excess weight via ChooseHelp: quote not used). One page dates it March 2011: outlier, not used.
- **Sri Lanka "drug cat"**: confirmed, **Welikada Prison**, caught **1 August 2020**, escaped the next day from the room where it was held. Load: "nearly two grams" of heroin (one extract: 1.8 g), two SIM cards, a memory chip, in a small plastic bag tied around its neck.
- **Other candidates not used**: Costa Rica 2016 / Panama 2021 prison cats and a 2015 Costa Rica prison pigeon (not searched in two sources within budget; the three above are funnier and verified); drug mule dogs and donkeys (not searched). Brief said "use only what verifies".
