# Case File 101: research ledger (ONE SMUGGLER, format v10)

Built 25 October 2026. Lane: smuggling, one person. Candidate from the brief: the 91-year-old Sydney man and the cocaine soap.

## Verdict, first

**The soap candidate verifies, but it fails the format: the charge was dropped.** Victor Twartz (91 when charged, 92 when
it ended) was arrested over 4.5 kg of cocaine hidden in 27 bars of soap carried from New Delhi to Sydney Airport in July
2015. On 12 February 2016, shortly before his District Court trial, Commonwealth prosecutors abandoned the case without
giving a reason. The AFP itself said there was "certainly some evidence" he had been "legitimately scammed ... and
exploited". He was never convicted and never pleaded. In law he is a man who was accused and then cleared. A "DUPED OR
GUILTY?" end card would ask strangers to vote on whether a cleared 92-year-old is a criminal. That is the "victim as the
joke" problem the brief rules out. **Do not make 101 from this case as a smuggler story. Do not name him.**

**Proposed replacement (verified, pleaded guilty, widely named): Ronald Lord, 71, of Montreal.** He was stopped at Gatwick
on 7 February 2025 off a flight from Barbados with about 8 kg of cocaine behind the seat-back panel of his mobility
scooter. He said he didn't know how it got there, but **the screw from that panel was in his pocket**. He pleaded guilty
and got six years (Croydon Crown Court, 5 September 2025). Sections A–C below are the full Ronald Lord ledger. Section D
keeps the soap case on file, with its ledger, in case it is ever told as a scam-victim story.

`case_spec.json` in this folder is written for **Ronald Lord**, not the soap case. Its `next_file_tease` ("THE TEENAGE
MULE") assumes 102 verifies; change it if 102 changes.

The folder keeps the id `tcv101-soap-grandad` because the brief created it. If Lord is approved, renaming it to something
like `tcv101-scooter-pensioner` is the builder's call.

## Method and limits

- Web search only (standard and extended modes), 22 queries, 25 Oct 2026. WebFetch failed with a DNS error
  (au.news.yahoo.com, khaosodenglish.com), so **every claim rests on search-result extracts**, not full pages. Access
  date for every URL: 2026-10-25.
- The search tool refuses domain-restricted queries for smh.com.au, theguardian.com, news.com.au, bbc.co.uk,
  independent.co.uk, telegraph.co.uk, dailymail.co.uk and similar. Their coverage could not be checked directly. Where
  this ledger mentions the SMH, it does so through outlets that quote it.
- Independence: AP copy syndicated through Fox News, CBS, Global News, Khaosod and others counts as **one** source (AP).
  The ABC and AP are independent of each other. For Lord, the **NCA release** is the primary source, and **CBC** did its
  own reporting with officials (it adds details the release lacks). Global News, LBC, The Nightly and Yahoo/AOL mostly
  relay the NCA, so they are corroboration, not independent confirmation.
- Single-source details are marked and kept out of the narration.

## B. Ronald Lord: the story in order

1. **Who he was.** A 71-year-old pensioner from the Montreal area (Châteauguay, per CBC). The sources give nothing else
   about him (job, family), so the script says nothing else.
2. **The lure.** Not established. CBC alone reports phone messages about being paid for a "work trip". The NCA's reading
   is that he counted on his age: a pensioner would be "less of a target".
3. **The trip.** Barbados, then a flight to London Gatwick, arriving 7 February 2025, travelling with a mobility scooter.
4. **The search.** Border Force X-rayed the scooter. About 8 kg of cocaine sat in a void behind the seat-back panel.
5. **What he said.** He was coming to sightsee after a week's holiday in Barbados, and he didn't know how the cocaine got
   into the scooter. Airline records showed three days, not seven. The panel's screw was in his pocket.
6. **The outcome.** Guilty plea on 5 August 2025, six years on 5 September 2025, Croydon Crown Court.

## C. Ronald Lord: build notes

**The object for frame 0 (half-reveal):** a mobility scooter's padded seat back, with hands backing out the last screw
of the rear panel and the panel tilting open a crack, contents still dark. On the gotcha line the panel comes away and
shows packed blocks in the void. The sources say only: cocaine "hidden in a void in the seat back panel", found by X-ray,
about 8 kg. **Nothing verified describes the scooter's colour, model or the wrapping of the packages.** Draw a generic
scooter and plain wrapped blocks, and add no brand. An X-ray-style inset of the seat at the gotcha works as the evidence
close-up and does not depend on any unseen photo.

**Evidence close-up (9–20 s):** one screw in an open palm (L8), or a split boarding-pass graphic reading
"BARBADOS · 7 DAYS?" against "3 DAYS" (L5–L6).

**Two-sided end-card question (grounded):** "WHAT GAVE HIM AWAY: THE SCREW OR THE 3 DAYS? 👇". Both clues are in the
record (L6, L8), so the question asks viewers to argue about evidence, not about a cleared man's guilt. Alternative:
"WOULD YOU HAVE SEARCHED THE SCOOTER? YES / NO 👇", which plays on the NCA line that a pensioner would be less of a target.
Send line: "SEND TO THE MATE WHO'D LEAVE THE SCREW IN HIS POCKET".

**The irony / human beat:** he said he had no idea how the cocaine got behind the panel, and the screw from that panel was
in his pocket. A second beat: he bet that nobody searches a pensioner on a mobility scooter, and the NCA said so out loud
(L11).

**Ethics and naming:** he pleaded guilty, was sentenced, and is named by the NCA, CBC, Global News, LBC and others, so
**use the name**. "Pensioner" is the NCA's own word. Nothing reported says whether he needs the scooter. **Do not imply
he faked a disability, and don't make the scooter itself the joke.** Do not mix this up with a different case: Casimiro
De Lemos Francisco, 56, who was not disabled, was sentenced over cocaine in an electric wheelchair at Manchester Airport
(also off a Barbados flight). Don't narrate the unproven payment detail (L12). Don't say who built the void. The plea
establishes knowing importation; the reports don't say he packed the scooter himself. "Hid" and "turned ... into" are
fine; avoid "built" and "packed it himself".

**What could NOT be verified (Lord):** who recruited or paid him (CBC only); where the scooter was loaded; whether he
uses a scooter day to day; his background; the scooter's appearance; any sentencing remarks beyond the NCA quote. No
BBC, Guardian or Sky story was reachable through the search tool.

**Proposed script outline (≤ 75 words; about 57 spoken, about 30 s):**
(0 s, panel cracking open) "A pensioner turned his scooter into a stash." (by 8 s, panel off) "Border Force X-rayed it.
Eight kilos of cocaine behind the seat." (by 12 s) "Ronald Lord, seventy-one, Montreal. Said he'd had a week in
Barbados. Airlines said three days." "He said he had no idea how it got there. The panel's screw was in his pocket. Six
years." (callback) "One screw short." End card, NEXT FILE.

### Story in order (as the record supports it)

He was a retired oral surgeon in Sydney, aged 91. Online contacts (World Bank or banker emails, a $10 million inheritance,
a business contract) paid for a trip to New Delhi. His family saw the emails and told police, and police warned him, but
he went. At the gate he was handed a bag of "gifts": 27 bars of soap. At Sydney Airport on 8 July 2015 the soap tested
positive for cocaine, about 4.5 kg. He told the ABC he had scratched the soap and seen white streaks. He was charged, the
AFP said publicly he looked scammed, the trial was rushed because of his age, and on 12 February 2016 prosecutors dropped
it with no reason given.

### The object, as the sources describe it

Bars of soap in "packages" (27). His own words describe white streaks inside when scratched. Nothing found gives a brand,
colour or wrapping.

### What could NOT be verified (soap case)

- **That he "reportedly died later".** No report of his death was found under his name. Don't say it.
- "Australian Border Force" by name. Only "police"/AFP and an airport baggage search appear in the extracts.
- The SMH, Guardian and news.com.au pages directly (the tool blocks them). The SMH "World Bank" detail is second-hand.
- Why prosecutors dropped the case.
- "Online romance": the record says online *business/inheritance* contacts, not romance. The brief's "online-romance
  lure" is wrong for this case. (Romance-scam mules are a different pattern, e.g. Maria Exposto, Malaysia: also acquitted
  in the end, in 2019, so also unusable.)

### Ethics (soap case)

He was charged but never convicted and never pleaded, and the case was abandoned. Under the brief's rule **he is not
named**. More than that, his story is a scam victim's: the AFP said so at the time of the charge. If it is ever used, it
can only be told as "scammers used a 91-year-old as a mule", with no name, the dropped charge stated plainly, no
"duped or guilty" vote, and the AFP warning about the elderly as the point. That is a different format from ONE SMUGGLER.
