# Case File 33 (40-second cut): the Times Square car bomb attempt, 1 May 2010, and the engine-block VIN

## Method and limits
- Research in this build environment is from **search-result extracts only** (WebFetch and curl are blocked); 10 WebSearch calls
  in standard mode. The URLs cited are the search-result URLs; full pages were not read. Each narrated claim maps to a claim in
  `episode.json` with at least two sources from different outlets (DOJ/FBI, AP via member stations, CBS, NBC, NPR, WBUR, WNYC,
  CNN transcript, Fox, Al Jazeera, ABA Journal, Computerworld, Courthouse News, Reuters via Volokh, NYT via Medill).
- Closed case: Faisal Shahzad pleaded guilty to all ten counts on 21 June 2010 and was sentenced to life in prison on

## Established facts (used in narration or on cards)
1. **The SUV.** A Nissan Pathfinder was left on West 45th Street just west of Broadway (Times Square) on the evening of Saturday
   1 May 2010, in a no-standing zone, with its hazard flashers on and the engine running; a small amount of smoke was coming from
   it. (CBS "Times Square Vendor: We Dodged a Bullet"; NBC New York "Heroic Times Square Vendors Honored"; NBC Connecticut.)
   On screen the vehicle is a generic dark SUV with no plate, badge or logo; the make is never said or shown.
2. **The vendors.** Two T-shirt street vendors, both military veterans (Lance Orton, Navy; Duane Jackson, Army), noticed the smoke
   and alerted a mounted police officer; the block was cleared and the NYPD bomb squad (with a robot) examined the SUV. They are
   NOT named on screen (card: "two T-shirt vendors, both veterans"). (CBS; NBC New York; American Legion; NBC Connecticut.)
3. **The device failed.** The device did not detonate; police described it as crude/amateurish. Card only: three propane tanks,
   two gasoline containers, fireworks, alarm clocks and wires; fertilizer found was not of an explosive grade. (Washington Times
   3 May 2010; Spokesman-Review/AP; MPR/AP; DOJ and FBI releases.) Nothing of the device is drawn: only smoke from the car.
4. **Officials' stakes line.** Mayor Bloomberg: "avoid what could have been a very deadly event"; Commissioner Kelly: "would have
   caused a significant fireball". Narration says only "Officials said it could have been very deadly." (Express Tribune/AFP;
   Al Jazeera 3 May 2010; VOA via GlobalSecurity; Washington Times.)
5. **The dashboard VIN had been removed.** (WNYC; WBUR/AP; Salon/AP; AP via Vindicator.) One CNN transcript line says the
   dashboard strip was not scratched off; the weight of reporting (AP, NYPD spokesman) is that it was removed, and the card says
   "removed".
6. **The VIN on the engine block.** Investigators found the number stamped on the engine (some reports: engine and axle) and
   traced it to the registered owner; NYPD spokesman Paul Browne called the find "pivotal". (WNYC; WBUR/AP; Computerworld;
   NYT via Medill.) The number itself is never shown (masked characters).
7. **The Craigslist sale.** The owner (not a suspect; not named on screen) had sold the Pathfinder about three weeks earlier for
   cash after advertising it online including on Craigslist, with no paperwork; the seller described and identified the buyer.
   Reported prices differ ($1,300 AP; $1,800 CNN), so no amount is said. (CNN transcript 4 May 2010; NYT via Medill; Salon/AP;
   AP via Vindicator; Reuters via Volokh.)
8. **The prepaid phone.** The buyer's number was a prepaid cellphone; the FBI obtained the vendor's records and linked the phone
   to Shahzad (calls to Pakistan, a fireworks store; a match against entry records). (Reuters via Volokh; Computerworld;
   Courthouse News; ABC7/AP.)
9. **Faisal Shahzad**, 30, a naturalized U.S. citizen born in Pakistan, living in Connecticut. (WBUR/AP; NPR; DOJ; FBI.)
10. **No-fly list.** Shahzad was added to the no-fly list on 3 May 2010 after being identified; the airline had not yet updated
    its copy of the list, so he could buy a ticket and board; a Customs and Border Protection check of the final passenger
    manifest caught his name. Afterwards airlines were required to check updates within two hours. (ABA Journal; CBS "No-fly
    list procedures changed"; Al Jazeera 6 May 2010; CBS "Emirates Airlines plays key role".)
11. **The ticket.** He reserved the flight on the way to the airport and paid in cash at the counter. (Fox News; Salon/AP.)
12. **The flight.** On the night of Monday 3 May 2010 he boarded an Emirates flight to Dubai (Flight 202 in most reports) at JFK.
    The airline is not named on screen; the cabin and aircraft are generic. (Fox "Fellow passengers"; CBS pilot/ATC audio;
    NYCAviation; WBUR/AP.)
13. **Called back.** The plane had left the gate and was taxiing (passengers: about five minutes) when air traffic control told
    the crew to return to the gate; CBP officers removed Shahzad from the plane; he was identified/arrested at about 11:45 p.m.
    Narration: "The plane had left the gate. It was called back." / "He was taken off the plane." (Fox; CBS audio; NYCAviation;
    CBS no-fly; Salon/AP.) Accounts differ on whether he was formally arrested on board or on the jet bridge; "taken off the
    plane" fits both.
14. **About 53 hours** after the SUV was found. (WBUR 5 May 2010; MPR/AP 5 May 2010; NPR 4 May 2010.)
15. **Plea.** On 21 June 2010 he pleaded guilty before Judge Miriam Goldman Cedarbaum to all ten counts, with no plea agreement.
    (DOJ release 10-ag-721; NPR transcript; FBI New York release.)
16. **Sentence.** On 5 October 2010 he was sentenced to life in prison (a mandatory life term on the charges). (FBI New York;
    CBS; NBC New York.)
