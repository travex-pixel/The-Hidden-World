## Established facts (used in narration; claim ids in episode.json)
- **C_escape**: escaped Altiplano maximum-security prison on 11 July 2015 through a tunnel the government put at 1.5 km (AP: "1-mile"/"more than 1,500 metres"; The Herald: "nearly 5,000 ft"); his second escape (Al Jazeera, AP via WGN and FOX 4, PBS, CSM). Narration: "a mile-long tunnel"; card "ABOUT 1.5 KM (~1 MILE)".
- **C_tunnel**: the tunnel opened in the shower area of his cell; lit and ventilated (PVC piping), about 1.7 m high and 80 cm wide (Al Jazeera), with rails and a motorcycle adapted to run on them, found inside and used to move earth (AP, CSM, CBS, VICE/AP). Narration: "The tunnel opened in his cell's shower. Inside was a motorcycle on rails." Whether Guzmán himself rode it is disputed (a 2019 trial witness said someone else drove it, per VICE), so the narration says only that it was inside.
- **C_run**: about six months on the run, described as Mexico's most-wanted man (CSM headline, Wikipedia, NPR). Label only.
- **C_film**: Attorney General Arely Gómez (Jan 2016): "an important aspect that allowed us to locate him was that we discovered Guzmán's intention to make a biographical film, for which he established contact with actresses and producers" (AP via Dallas Morning News; NBC; TheWrap; TheJournal.ie; El Universal). Narration: "Then he wanted a movie about his life. He reached out to actresses and producers."
- **C_tracked**: Gómez: "the follow-up work allowed us to document meetings between attorneys of the now-detainee and these people" (AP; TheJournal.ie; TheWrap). Narration: "Investigators tracked his lawyers meeting with them."
- **C_ag** (the HOW): Gómez called the film plan "an important aspect" in locating him (sources as above). Narration: "Mexico's attorney general said that helped locate him." Card quotes only "an important aspect" (AP's translation). It was one lead among several (surveillance of the Los Mochis property is also reported).
- **C_raid**: before dawn (about 4:30 a.m.) on 8 January 2016 Mexican marines raided a house in Los Mochis, Sinaloa (ABC11, NPR, Fox/AP, AFP). Narration: "Marines raided a house in Los Mochis." The gunfight (five gunmen killed, one marine wounded) is NOT narrated or shown.
- **C_drain**: he and a lieutenant left through a hidden passage behind a closet mirror into the storm-drain system and came out of a manhole (NPR, Fox/AP, AFP, ABC11). Narration: "He fled through a storm drain."
- **C_caught**: after taking cars, he was stopped and caught on a highway outside the city (NPR: "authorities caught him on a highway in a stolen car"; AP: stopped by federal police). Narration: "And then police caught him on a highway."
- **C_verdict**: 12 February 2019, after a three-month trial in Brooklyn federal court, a jury convicted him on all 10 counts (DOJ x2, JURIST, NBC). Narration: "A Brooklyn jury convicted him on all ten counts."
- **C_sentence**: 17 July 2019, Judge Brian Cogan: life imprisonment plus 30 years, $12.6 billion forfeiture (DOJ x2, Scripps). Narration: "He got life, plus thirty years."
- **C_extradited / C_appeal** (cards only): extradited January 2017 (JURIST, Al Día, DOJ); Second Circuit upheld the conviction in January 2022 (JURIST, Al Día); held at ADX Florence, Colorado (Al Día; also several 2026 reports of failed requests about his confinement, low-quality extracts, not used).
- **C_2001** (card only): 2001 escape from Puente Grande, "reportedly" in a laundry cart (AP via FOX 4 and WGN; Wikipedia). Accounts vary; attributed on the card.

## Allegations / inquiry findings (attributed)
- The role of the film contacts is the Mexican attorney general's account, so narration attributes it ("Mexico's attorney general said") and the headline says "HELPED CATCH", never "got him caught". Interior Minister Osorio Chong called the actress lead "one more piece of information".
- NBC reported that Hollywood producers had told U.S. officials about the project; not used.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- That he rode the motorcycle through the tunnel (widely reported in 2015, disputed by 2019 trial testimony).
- His "infatuation" with an actress as the cause of capture (officials' and tabloid framing; also names a person who was never charged).
- The October 2015 meeting and the magazine interview: the actor and actress are NOT named, drawn or alluded to (assignment rule; the actress was treated as a witness and accused of nothing).
- Net-worth, cartel-revenue and body-count figures; the 2024 events involving his sons and Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada (not relevant to this story).
- "Convicted on 18 July 2019" (one extract's error; 12 Feb 2019 verdict, 17 July 2019 sentence per DOJ).

## Ethics decisions
- Guzmán is a convicted person in a closed case: he appears only as a rim-lit SILHOUETTE tagged 'ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS' (no face, no mugshot, no escape-footage or capture-photo frame recreated), never with weapons, drugs or cash, never heroic, and none of his words are quoted.
- No violence, victims, weapons or drugs: marines and police are torch-carrying silhouettes without insignia; the raid is a house approached before dawn; the arrest is a stopped car under a light bar.
- The cell, tunnel, hideout, street, highway and courtroom are generic illustrations, labelled as such. The clapperboard is our own symbolic prop with blank, illegible chalk fields (no real film, title or company).
