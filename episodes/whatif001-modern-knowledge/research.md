# Fact-check: "What If You Woke Up in 1340 Knowing the Future?" (What-If 001)

**Setting:** an English village and its manor in late summer 1340, eight years before the Black Death.
**Accessed:** 2026-10-12.

## Method and limitations (read first)

* **Pages were not opened.** WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every page, so the full text of each source was never
  read.
* **Every source below was seen only as a search-result snippet.** Quotes, line numbers and figures are as the
  snippets give them. No URL was invented: each one appeared in a search result.
* **Check before broadcast.** Compare every on-screen badge against the full source.
* **What is fiction.** You, Tom, Agnes, Walter the reeve, Master Hamo, Wat the smith, Sir Ralph, the village and
  Wat's machine are fictional, as is everything they say. Tom's "What man artow?" is a real Chaucer line, but it is
  roughly fifty years too late and London English, and the episode labels it as borrowed.
* **What is speculation.** The alternate-history branch (scene w14) and the guess that the yard changes make fewer
  children sick (scene w04) are speculation, labelled HYPOTHETICAL on screen.

Tags: **established** (documented) · **estimate** · **disputed** · **plausible** (interpretation or expert
judgement) · **illustrative** (fiction or speculation).

### Dramatised in the script (not claimed as fact)
* **Agnes vouching for you and the thatching job:** the job is illustrative. The pay rate comes from the 1351 statute
  (W6).
* **Hamo's counting house:** the scene is fiction. The methods (tallies, Roman numerals, counters on a chequered
  cloth) are W10.
* **"Call in every debt the Italians owe him":** fiction. Only the banks' failures are claimed (W11c).
* **The steam engine:** Wat's machine is fiction. The dates are W13.
* **Sir Ralph and the hall laughing:** fiction.

## Not used (unconfirmed or wrong)
* **Myths:**
  * "Nobody drank water."
  * "Cannon won Crécy" and "first cannon ever".
  * Salvino degli Armati inventing spectacles in 1286.
  * "Florence banned zero / Arabic numerals entirely."
  * "Quarantine began as forty days in 1377."
  * "Rats alone spread plague."
* **Avoided:** witch-hunts in 1340s England; any percentage for how much a modern person would understand; any exact
  plague landing date (one site gives "8 May 1348").
* **Cut for time (true, but not in the episode):** England's first known paper mill, John Tate's Sele Mill near Hertford, evidenced 1495–98 (S35); Gutenberg's shop by 1450.
* **Unconfirmed:**
  * "all labourers 1½d";
  * wool custom of 40s / 53s 4d in 1340;
  * the Bishop of Bath & Wells's 1349 letter;
  * the exact wording of Edward III's 1349 letter;
  * Wilkinson's accuracy as "the thickness of an old shilling";
  * laws against political prophecy in 1340.
