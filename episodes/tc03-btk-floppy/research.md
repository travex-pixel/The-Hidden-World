## Ethical and legal review (Phase 3)
- **No victims, crime scenes, violence or method details**, and no explanation of the self-chosen name "BTK" (its meaning is graphic). The ten victims are acknowledged with ten candle lights and a closing memorial line, not names or images.
- **No likeness of Dennis Rader.** He is identified by name card only, after the narration establishes the evidence.
- **The daughter's role is handled minimally** ("A DNA test confirmed the link"). The research file records the details, but her name and the medical nature of the sample are not put on screen.
- **Avoided:**
  - the disputed legal mechanism for obtaining the sample (warrant, subpoena or court order)
  - the disk's colour and brand, and the cereal brand (reported inconsistently)
  - the unverified "favourite lie" quote
  - the unverified name of the forensic examiner
- **Drawn props are generic and labelled:** ILLUSTRATION / NOT THE ACTUAL BOX / AD TEXT AS REPORTED / ILLUSTRATION · SIMPLIFIED (the forensic screen) / NOT THE ACTUAL BUILDING. No newspaper masthead or real document is imitated.
- **Wording errors found and fixed in production review:**
  - "TV station" was misheard as "three-station", so it became "television station"
  - "undone" was misheard as "and done", so it became "ended by"
  - "Thirty-one" was spoken with a break, so it became "31"
  - sound effects that masked the hook line and the word "Christ" were moved and lowered

## People and roles
- **Dennis Rader:** BTK ("Bind, Torture, Kill"). He was president of the Christ Lutheran Church council and lived in Park City, KS.
- **Lt. Ken Landwehr:** Wichita PD, head of the BTK task force. He later called the floppy-disk answer his "favorite lie of all time" (per Refinery29 and Oxygen; the original quote source was not verified).
- **Det. Cheryl James:** reported to have placed the "Rex" ad under the alias "Cyndi Johnson" (Refinery29 / Simply Forensic; secondary only).
- **Det. Randy Stone:** forensic examiner credited with recovering the deleted file. **Only one secondary blog** names him (simplyforensic.com), so this is not corroborated.
- **Pastor Michael Clark:** Christ Lutheran Church. He confirmed that Rader brought in a floppy disk in January to print a council agenda (AP via LJWorld/CBS/NBC).
- **Kerri Rawson (née Rader):** Rader's daughter. Her medical sample was used for the familial DNA test without her knowledge. She later wrote a memoir and is the subject of the 2025 Netflix film.
- **Judge Gregory Waller:** Sedgwick County District Court. He took the plea and imposed the sentence.

## Established facts (multi-source agreement)
1. BTK resurfaced in March 2004 with a letter to the Wichita Eagle that included evidence from the Wegerle killing.
2. In a note left in a cereal box (Jan 2005), he asked police whether a floppy disk could be traced.
3. Police answered through a Wichita Eagle classified ad with the agreed phrase "Rex, it will be OK". In effect they told him it was safe, which was a deliberate deception.
4. The disk was sent to **KSAS-TV (Fox affiliate)** on 16 Feb 2005.
5. A deleted Word document on the disk carried metadata naming **Christ Lutheran Church** and a user named **Dennis**. The deleted document was a church council agenda.
6. Rader was arrested 25 Feb 2005, pleaded guilty to 10 first-degree murders on 27 Jun 2005, and was sentenced 18 Aug 2005 to 10 consecutive life terms (minimum 175 years).

## Allegations vs. established
- Every killing was admitted in Rader's own plea and allocution. The court accepted the guilty pleas to **10 counts**. There was no trial.
- Speculation about further victims, such as a 1976 Oklahoma cold case, is **not established**. His daughter publicly disputes it (Fox News [S20]).

## Evidence presented (as reported)
- The floppy disk and its metadata, DNA (the familial match, then a direct match after arrest), the communications, and keepsakes and index cards found in searches. Landwehr testified at sentencing about the index cards (NBC [S27]).

## What the court ultimately established
- Guilty pleas to 10 counts of first-degree murder, convictions entered, and 10 consecutive life sentences (minimum 175 years without parole). Judge Waller imposed the maximum.

## Disputed / unknown details
- **Legal process for the daughter's sample:** sources variously say warrant (ABC News / Reader's Digest), subpoena (Stanford Law abstract, Crime Library) or court order (NPR 2008).
  - **Do not state the mechanism with certainty.** Safe wording: "police obtained, with court authorisation, a tissue sample from his daughter's medical records."
  - The institution is also reported inconsistently. ABC and Reader's Digest say her college health centre, Wikipedia says Kansas State University, one site says "University of Kansas medical clinic", and others say "hospital". Safe wording: "a medical sample from her university clinic."
- **The ad:** exact run date not verified. Who placed it is also unclear (James under an alias vs Landwehr). Some sources add extra text to the ad wording.
- **Who received the disk:** KSAS-TV is the consensus. Boing Boing (2005) said KAKE, which is likely confusion with the January postcard to KAKE.
- **The cereal box:** brand reported as Post Toasties vs Special K. Dates given as 8 Jan (left) vs 25 Jan (postcard) vs 27 Jan (found).
- **Forensic examiner's name (Randy Stone):** single secondary source.
- **"Rader thought he'd erased identifying information":** reported paraphrase of Landwehr (AP).
- **The March 2004 letter:** date given as 17 or 19 Mar, and the number of photos as two or three.

## Contradictions vs. the brief's listed facts
- "Familial DNA from his daughter's Pap smear sample (obtained via a warrant/subpoena) confirming the match." The sample was a **familial (near) match** that established the source was closely related to her, i.e. her father. It **corroborated** the floppy-disk lead and did not, on its own, identify him. Sources conflict on the legal instrument (warrant, subpoena or court order).
- "10 consecutive life terms": **correct**, with a minimum of 175 years and parole eligibility only after that (A&E's "possibility of parole after 175 years" wording is technically the same thing).
- "KSAS-TV in Feb 2005": **correct**, delivered 16 Feb 2005.
- The ad: correct that it was a newspaper classified ad (Wichita Eagle) with the text "Rex, it will be OK."
- Number of victims pleaded to: **10**.

## Strongest storytelling angle
The killer asked the police directly whether a floppy disk could betray him. They answered, through a newspaper classified ad, "Rex, it will be OK." Two weeks later he mailed the disk, and a single deleted file carried the words "Christ Lutheran Church" and "Dennis".

## Most compelling verified revelation
A deleted Word document's metadata, consisting only of a church name and a first name, ended a 31-year hunt (1974 to 2005) within about 9 days of the disk's arrival (16 Feb to 25 Feb 2005).

## Why it suits a 60-second Short
- A clean three-beat structure: the question, the lie, the file.
- It can be told entirely through objects (a cereal box, a newspaper ad, a floppy disk, a metadata field), so no violence needs to be depicted.
- The victims can be acknowledged respectfully with a number and dates, not imagery.

**Sensitivity note:** the daughter's DNA is a privacy story. Treat Kerri Rawson as an unwitting party. She has said she would have given DNA willingly if asked (ABC News).
