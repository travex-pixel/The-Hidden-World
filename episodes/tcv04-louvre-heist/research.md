# Research ledger: the Louvre crown-jewels heist (Paris, Sunday 19 Oct 2025)

**Case File 04 · episode `tcv04-louvre-heist` · legal status verified on 2026-10-11.**
This case is ongoing. Several people have been charged (*mis en examen*) in France. None has been tried or convicted, and all are presumed innocent. Every accusation below is attributed to the person or body that made it.

## Method and limits
- **Research date:** 2026-10-11, the production day. Starting point: `series/true-crime/research_next_slate/international_and_landmark_status.md`, section 1 (dossier dated 2026-10-11).
- **Searches:** 14 web searches this session, with a cap of 15. Two used extended mode, both for the Aug–Oct 2026 status check. One further call was refused by the tool because of a domain filter.
- **Search extracts only.** WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every fact rests on search-result extracts. No full article, primary court document, prosecutor's release, audit report or museum page was read. Each source's `accessed` field in `episode.json` says so.
- **Dates.** A date is given only where the URL or the extract states it. Otherwise the source says "date not shown in extract", or gives the month implied by the story.
- **Absence of evidence.** "No report found" means these extracts held nothing newer. It does not mean nothing happened.
- **Sources not used.** Aggregators, content-farm blogs and Wikipedia appear only as leads, never as the basis of a claim. Examples: mojoauth.com, botcrawl.com, hackr.io, fr.wikipedia.

## Allegations (attributed)
- **Who took part.** Charging is an accusation. The people charged are presumed innocent. The script says only "two men were charged" and "more followed". It names, describes and depicts no one.
- **"Partial admissions" (29 Oct 2025).** This is the Paris prosecutor's characterisation (Al Jazeera 2025-10-29). It is **not used**.
- **A backer or mastermind.** As relayed in July 2026, two suspects reportedly told the investigating judges they were recruited by a backer. The prosecutor called that "a hypothesis under consideration" that "cannot be asserted as more certain than any other" (blue News, ~Apr 2026). This is **not used**, because the sources conflict.
- **The Belgian lead (June 2026).** Investigators followed phone records to Belgium (Euronews 2026-06-02; Artnet). This is **not used**.
- **The 2014 password.** This is a reported audit finding, not an allegation against any person. **Used, with attribution.**
  - What was reported: as reported by *Libération* and relayed by CNN (2025-11-06), LBC, TechRadar and Tom's Hardware, a **2014 audit by ANSSI** (France's cybersecurity agency) found that the password for the server running the Louvre's CCTV network was "LOUVRE". A Thales product was protected by "THALES".
  - ANSSI's response: ANSSI told CNN the audit "cannot be considered representative of the current level of security."
  - Was it still in use in Oct 2025? **Not established.** TechRadar says this is unclear. ABC News quoted one unnamed employee saying it was still the password at the time of the robbery. That is a single, unconfirmed claim and is **not used**.
  - Script wording: "A 2014 audit reportedly found its CCTV password was... LOUVRE. Still in use in 2025? That hasn't been confirmed."
  - On-screen card: "2014 AUDIT · AS REPORTED BY LIBÉRATION" and "IN USE IN 2025? NOT CONFIRMED".

- **Charges.** Several people have been charged, and counts differ by outlet:
  - AFP (Jan 2026): four alleged thieves and one alleged accomplice, the woman being released under judicial supervision.
  - Artnet (mid-2026): "at least 11" arrested, five charged.
  - ABC News (July 2026): 11 arrests, some released.
  - The script avoids a number: "two men were charged. More followed."
- **Trial.** **None has been tried.** No trial date or referral to trial was found in any extract up to 2026-10-11.
- **The jewels.** **Seven items are still missing**; only Eugénie's crown has been recovered. Its restoration was announced in Feb 2026, and no report of completion was found.
- **Aug–Oct 2026 re-check (this session).** Four searches were run, two of them in extended mode, one of which was in French. They found **no new arrest, charge, trial date, prosecutor statement or recovery after July 2026**, and no first-anniversary coverage yet (the anniversary falls on 19 Oct 2026).

A follow-up search found nothing linking it to the Louvre. The figure does not match the €88m Louvre haul, and every dated Louvre report through July 2026 says the jewels are missing. It is treated as **a different case and not used**.
  - **Re-check on 18–19 Oct before any repost.**

## Fun facts with sources
Each fun fact appears in a `funfact` pop-up with a `sparkle` sound effect on the word "Fun".

1. **Eugénie's crown: 1,354 diamonds, and only 10 were lost** (claim L_ff_crown).
   - What the sources say: the crown has 1,354 diamonds and 56 emeralds. After the heist it kept all 56 emeralds and all but 10 diamonds. One of its eight gold eagles is missing. The Louvre says it can be restored without reconstruction.
   - Sources: PBS NewsHour/AP (2026-02); Gulf News (2026-02); Express & Star/PA (2025-11-07); France 24 (2026-02-05; restoration).
   - Note: the dossier warned against the "nearly 2,000 diamonds" figure, which belongs to the stolen **diadem**, a different piece. That figure is not used.
2. **The Mona Lisa was stolen from the Louvre in 1911** (claim L_ff_mona).
   - What the sources say: it was stolen on 21 Aug 1911 and discovered missing on 22 Aug. It was recovered in Florence in Dec 1913, more than two years later, and returned to the Louvre in Jan 1914.
   - Sources: History.com (This Day in History); Britannica (Today in History); NPR 2025-10-20; PBS *Secrets of the Dead*.
   - The pop-up says "missing for over two years", and the narration says only "stolen from it in 1911". The thief's name is not used.
- **Considered but not used:** "the world's most-visited museum". It was not re-verified this session, so it was dropped.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "Exactly seven minutes." Timings conflict across outlets (4, 6–7, under 7, under 8), so the script uses "about seven minutes".
- "The password was LOUVRE on the day of the heist." This is unconfirmed (see above).
- Any diamond count for the stolen diadem, and "priceless" as a valuation. The script uses only the official ~€88m estimate.
- The mastermind or backer theory, and the claim that the jewels are still hidden in France (a single franceinfo extract with no date shown).
- Speculation that the stones were broken up or the gold melted down. Experts fear it, but it is unproven.
- The "partial admissions", suspects' ages, nationalities, home towns or DNA details. These could identify people or imply guilt.
- "The museum had just opened" and the visitor count on the day. Not verified this session.
- The €12m "recovered" item (see Status). It is not about the Louvre.
- The 2019 report on the unreinforced window and the camera that faced west. Both are verified in the dossier, but they were cut for time.
