# Case File 67: Birds in his underwear (tcv67-underwear-birds)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Status of the hero case: the Miami traveller is CHARGED (federal criminal complaint, U.S. Attorney's Office, Southern District of Florida) with smuggling goods into the United States; released on a 25,000-dollar bond; presumed innocent; no plea or trial reported as of 17 October 2026. The Fish and Wildlife Service said it is also investigating.

## Method and limits
- 9 standard web searches. WebFetch is blocked in this environment (DNS failure on abc11.com and kptv.com), so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read. The court complaint itself was not read.
- The Miami story came from the federal complaint as reported by ABC News (ABC owned stations + Good Morning America), Sinclair (CBS12 and syndication), Gray Media stations (KPTV, WCAX, Hawaii News Now, WTOC; CNN-style syndicated copy), WSB-TV (Cox), Fox News Digital and Telemundo 51. Counted as at least four independent newsrooms (ABC, Sinclair, Gray/CNN, Cox) plus Fox and Telemundo.
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Date: refined.** The arrival and arrest were on **Sunday 4 October 2026** (CBS12 and syndication; ABC says "Sunday"); first reports 6-7 October, not ~9 October.
- **Concealment: confirmed with nuance.** ABC (U.S. Attorney): "six plastic tubes that were sewn into his underwear with live birds inside"; CBS12/Gray: six plastic containers in pockets sewn into his underwear. Narration: "Six tubes were sewn into his underwear."
- **Species: confirmed** as a *preliminary* identification: eight yellow-faced grassquits (Tiaris olivaceus) and two blue grosbeaks (Passerina caerulea) (ABC, CBS12, Gray). CubaHeadlines named "Cuban grassquits" and "indigo buntings"; single, weaker source, not used. Both species are reported as protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act (CBS12, CubaHeadlines); not narrated.
- **What happened to the birds: NOT REPORTED.** No source found says whether they survived, were quarantined or where they went ("the birds' status was unknown" in one extract). Nothing is said about it on screen or in narration; the drawings show them alive in the tubes only at the moment of discovery.
- **Name:** reported (Alberto Hernandez Castillo, 40) but deliberately NOT used anywhere on screen, in narration or in the caption (accused only).
- **"Personal use":** he allegedly told officers the birds were for his "personal use" before invoking his right to remain silent (ABC/GMA; Gray). Used, attributed ("he allegedly said").
- **Customs form:** he marked "No" when asked on the customs declaration about bringing animals (ABC "court documents"; CBS12; Gray). Used as the fun fact.
- **Second Miami case (6 October 2026, 11 birds in hair-roller tubes):** CubaHeadlines only; single source, NOT used.
- **Arraignment 26 October:** one report only; not used.
- **List items chosen:** of the brief's suggestions, two verified with two or more independent sources each: (2) San Ysidro 2022, 52 reptiles hidden in clothing; (3) Futian port, Shenzhen 2024, 104 live snakes in trouser pockets. Tortoises in clothing and animals in a prosthetic leg were not searched further (budget) and are not used.
- **Item 3 details:** the year is **July 2024** (announced 9 July 2024), not 2023. Species vary between outlets (milk, corn, western hognose, bull, Texas rat / pilot black snakes) and venom statements conflict (one outlet: hognose "mildly venomous"), so neither species nor "non-venomous" is narrated. Whether he was arrested is not reported; not claimed.
