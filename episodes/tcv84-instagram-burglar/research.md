## Corrections to the brief
- **"Instagram":** FOX 10 (7 and 8 Oct), reading the court documents, says he followed the victims' Instagram accounts. AZFamily/KOLD says "he would scroll his social media feed" and the Scottsdale Police release (via YourValley) says "social media posts". Instagram is therefore FOX 10's specific wording; the hook keeps it because the series title and search terms depend on it, and because AZFamily's "social media" wording is consistent with it, but this is flagged as a risk in the report.
- **How he was caught:** two outlets agree that after a Sunday break-in at a central Phoenix home, police tracked him to his office and arrested him there. FOX 10 adds police drones and live cell-phone location tracking and places the office on McClintock Drive in Tempe; AZFamily places the office in Scottsdale. Narration says only "police tracked him, to his own office"; drones and phone tracking are not narrated (single source).
- **Scope and money:** police suspect him in "a dozen to more than 20" (FOX 10) / "more than a dozen" (AZFamily) burglaries; losses are reported from "$72k" (12News, per the coordinator) to "$400K+" (FOX 10 headline), with "hundreds of thousands of dollars" in FOX 10's body. The reel says "at least a dozen homes, police say" (counter 12+) and no dollar figure.
- **Charges:** AZFamily: charged with that burglary and one other in Scottsdale; the police release: booked on second-degree burglary and possession of burglary tools; other reports: "multiple felonies". The reel states no charge count: only ACCUSED · NOT TRIED.
- **Stolen items:** FOX 10 lists luxury watches, designer handbags, firearms, silver bars and a signed sports jersey. Not confirmed in a second extract, so no list appears on screen; firearms are not mentioned at all.
- **Martinsburg 2009 outcome:** the brief said he was convicted. No plea, trial or sentence was found in any source (the coverage ends at the arraignment and the $10,000 bail). The reel says "charged" and the label says "no verdict found".
- **The office of the 2010 DC case:** the 44-month sentence is reported by FindLaw and by Graham Cluley (27 + 17 months = 3.5 years); NBC News/NewsOne report the guilty plea. The pistol count is not mentioned in the reel.
