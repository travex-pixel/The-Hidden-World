# Case File 09: Agatha Christie's 11 missing days (3–14 December 1926)

Research ledger. Compiled 2026-10-11 for the cinematic Case Files episode `tcv09-agatha-christie` (12 scenes, about 78 s).

## Method and limits
- 12 web searches (standard mode) on 2026-10-11. WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so every source below is cited from its **search-result extract**, not a full read of the page. Each source record in `episode.json` says so in its `accessed` field.
- Starting point: `series/true-crime/research_next_slate/producer_checks_2026-10-11.md` (Agatha Christie section).
- Rule applied: every narrated fact and every on-screen fact needs at least two independent sources. Anything with only one source is either attributed ("reported", "the papers said"), kept off screen, or listed under "Myths / details NOT used".
- This was not a crime. No one was charged with anything. Christie was found alive and well.
- Real people (Agatha Christie, Arthur Conan Doyle) are illustrated representations, not likenesses, and are tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS" on screen. Police, volunteers, hotel staff and musicians are generic 1920s figures.

## Theories (attributed; none is presented as fact)
| Theory | Who holds it | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| A "fugue state": a rare, trance-like loss of identity brought on by trauma or depression | Biographer Andrew Norman | History Hit; A&E; National Geographic |
| Trauma and a depressive episode (grief for her mother, the collapse of her marriage, overwork) | Historian Lucy Worsley (2022 biography) | National Geographic; Pocklington Post |
| A plan to embarrass or punish her husband (proponents point to the name "Neele") | Unnamed proponents, as reported | National Geographic; Stylist |
| A publicity stunt | Rumour at the time; National Geographic notes she was already well known, so it seems unnecessary | National Geographic; Stylist |

The narration says: "Biographer Andrew Norman suggests a 'fugue state'… Others suspect revenge on Archie, or a publicity stunt." The on-screen cards carry question marks and the label "THEORIES · NONE PROVEN".

## Fun facts (each with two sources)
1. **Best-selling fiction writer of all time.** Guinness World Records lists Christie as the best-selling fiction writer, with an estimated 2 billion copies sold. Sources: Guinness World Records; Imperial War Museums (Lives of the First World War). On screen: "about 2 billion books sold" (the sources say "estimated").
2. **Sherlock Holmes's creator and the glove.** Conan Doyle asked a medium to "read" one of Christie's gloves. Sources: Arthur Conan Doyle Encyclopedia (reprints the Leeds Mercury, 21 Dec 1926); History Collection; Countryfile.

## Conflicts and caveats
- **Size of the search.** The National Archives holds a Surrey Constabulary letter to the Home Office saying that much press matter "was without foundation"; one search extract says it calls the aeroplanes, divers and multiple forces overstated, and it puts the search expenses at about £25. The letter was written to reassure the Home Office, so it may itself be self-serving. Decision: the figures are narrated as **reported** ("Over a thousand police were reported searching… Even aeroplanes, the papers said"), never as established numbers. The "15,000" figure is not put on screen.
- **Silent Pool.** Sources disagree on how close it is (on the edge of the lake vs a quarter-mile away). It is not named in the narration or on screen.
- **Car found.** Most sources say 4 Dec, the next morning. One mentions headlights still on. Headlamps are drawn in the illustration (labelled ILLUSTRATION), but not narrated.
- **Name at the hotel.** Teresa vs Theresa. The narration says "Teresa" (Worsley, Wikipedia). One Italian source says "Nancy Neele"; it is an outlier and is not used.
- **Date found.** Gizmodo and History Hit say 14 Dec. One Conan Doyle source says "Wednesday, 15 December" (the day the news was published). We use 14 Dec.
- **Glove origin.** One account says Doyle took the glove from her car; the Leeds Mercury says the medium was given it without being told whose it was. We say only that he took one of her gloves to a medium.

## Myths and details NOT used
- **Dorothy L. Sayers visited Newlands Corner** to look for clues (Gizmodo; History Collection; accounts link it to her novel *Unnatural Death*). Two sources, but both are popular-history sites, and the scene was cut to keep the runtime under 80 s. It can be used in a longer cut.
- "Aeroplanes were used for the first time in a missing-person search": single-sourced and contested by the police letter. Not used.
- "15,000 volunteers" as a fact on screen: an estimate. Narrated only as "thousands of volunteers", attributed to reports.
- The medium's reading ("she is alive"; "heard of on Wednesday"): only the Leeds Mercury reprint. Not used.
- That she wrote to Archie's brother, or that Archie was with Nancy Neele that weekend: not checked in this session. Not used.
- The 1979 film *Agatha* (Vanessa Redgrave, Dustin Hoffman) dramatised the episode: not confirmed by a search in this session. Not used.
- Any claim that she was "hiding", "faking" or "pulling a stunt": presented only as an attributed theory.
- Suicide speculation: reported at the time, but not used, out of kindness and because it is not needed to tell the story.

## Ethics decisions
- Kind framing of distress. Agatha is shown with dignity: walking out alone, a hand to her face, writing at night. No mockery and no "gotcha" tone about the fake name. The fugue and trauma theories are given first and the cynical ones last, all attributed.
- Archie is never accused and is not depicted. The rumours line is posed as questions that were asked at the time ("Was her husband to blame?"), and the claim notes that no one was charged.
- The medium (Horace Leaf) is not named on screen and appears only as an anonymous silhouette. The final dawn shot keeps Agatha away from the chalk pit, so it implies no danger.
- Nancy Neele is named only as the source of the surname, which is a matter of public record. She is not depicted.
- Generic places. The house is labelled "generic house, not the real home". The hotel, downs and study are generic illustrations, not replicas.
- No replica newspapers, hotel registers or police documents. The cards are our own typed graphics with plain text, and the newspapers have no mastheads and illegible text.
- End card: as the last episode of the batch, it teases no specific case. It is a call to action ("YOU PICK THE NEXT CASE").
