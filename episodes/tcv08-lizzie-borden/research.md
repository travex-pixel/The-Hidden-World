# Case File 08: Lizzie Borden (research ledger)

**Ledger date:** 2026-10-11 (production day). **Episode:** `tcv08-lizzie-borden`. **Status:** closed historical case. Lizzie Borden was acquitted on 20 June 1893; no one else was ever tried, and the killings of Andrew and Abby Borden remain officially unsolved.

## Method and limits
- I ran 11 web searches on 2026-10-11 and used the producer notes in `series/true-crime/research_next_slate/producer_checks_2026-10-11.md` (Lizzie Borden and Agatha Christie sections) and `audience_trends_oct2026.md` (*Monster* figures).
- WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment. Every source below is cited from **search-result extracts**; I could not open the full pages or the trial transcript.
- Rule used: a fact goes on screen or into narration only when at least two reputable outlets agree. Where outlets disagree, the episode uses the cautious wording ("about ninety minutes") or leaves the detail out.
- Forum reproductions of the trial transcript (lizzieandrewborden.com) were read for context only. They are not cited as sources.
- *Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story* (Netflix, 17 Sep 2026) is a scripted drama that fictionalises. Nothing in the script comes from it. It is mentioned in one line only, as the reason viewers know the name.

## Trial evidence (attributed)
- **The burned dress (prosecution witness).** Family friend **Alice Russell** testified that on the Sunday after the killings (7 Aug 1892) she saw Lizzie burning a dress in the kitchen stove. Sources: Biography, "Alice Russell and the Burned Dress"; Smithsonian; Britannica; Biography (trial). Accounts differ on small details (described as a cheap blue Bedford-cord dress; "coal stove" in Smithsonian), so the script says only "a dress" and "the stove".
- **"Paint" (her explanation, per Russell's testimony).** Lizzie said the dress was stained (or covered) with paint. Sources: Biography (Alice Russell); Smithsonian; Woman & Home. Russell herself was unsure whether the words were "covered in" or "covered with" paint, so no quotation is used.
- **Prosecution's argument.** The burning was destruction of evidence. Sources: Smithsonian; Biography (Alice Russell). It is narrated as "Prosecutors said...". Defence context (not narrated, kept for fairness): Russell testified she saw no blood on the dress, and Emma described it as an old, paint-stained dress. This came from one extract each, so it was not used.
- **Excluded: her inquest testimony.** The court kept Lizzie's inquest statements out of the trial. Sources: A&E (why acquitted); EBSCO Research Starters; Grazia. The reason is given differently across sources (no counsel present; she was effectively a suspect), so the script gives no reason. The judges are not named.
- **Excluded: the poison testimony.** Drugstore clerk Eli Bence said Lizzie tried to buy prussic acid on 3 Aug 1892, the day before the killings, and the court excluded this from the trial. Sources: A&E (why acquitted); EBSCO; HISTORY.com (rhyme article, "a strange attempt to buy poison the day before"). Narrated as "a clerk who said she tried to buy poison", which is attributed to him, not stated as fact. Sources give different reasons for the exclusion, so none is stated.

## Fun facts (pop-ups, each with two or more sources)
1. **The famous rhyme gets it wrong.** It calls Abby Lizzie's "mother"; she was her stepmother. Sources: HISTORY.com (Betsy Golden Kellem, rhyme legacy); Crime+Investigation UK (rhyme article). *Not used:* the rhyme's blow counts and other graphic details, which are also wrong.
2. **You can stay the night in the Borden house.** The Second Street house is now the Lizzie Borden House, a museum and bed and breakfast with overnight rooms. Sources: Fox News Travel; Boston Magazine (2015); Untapped Cities. The card says only "museum + bed & breakfast"; prices, owners and room names are left out because they change.

## Myths and details NOT used
- Blow counts from the rhyme, and any description of injuries or the scene. None is narrated or shown.
- Any claim that Lizzie "did it", or any motive (inheritance, the family quarrels, alleged relationships). These are speculation, and much of it comes from dramatisations, including *Monster*.
- The "hatchet head in the basement" story. It is never shown or mentioned; we show no weapon at all.
- "The jury deliberated 13 days" (a repost error) and "trial in 1894" (one-source error).
- That Alice Russell's testimony was "disallowed". One extract says so, but it is contradicted by the trial record that other sources describe.
- Ghost stories and hauntings from the B&B coverage.
- Lizzie's later name, "Lizbeth", and her bequest to an animal rescue league. Both are verified, but there was no room for them.

## Ethics decisions
- **No depiction of the victims or the crime.** Andrew and Abby never appear. The words used are "the killings" and "were killed". No blood, no body, no axe or hatchet anywhere in frame. There is no sofa in the parlour set, because of its association with the crime scene.
- **Acquitted woman.** Lizzie is an illustrated representation, not a likeness, tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS". She is never drawn with a sinister or guilty expression, only neutral, blank, worried or sad. The burning is staged as cloth in a stove. Narration attributes the prosecution's view ("Prosecutors said...") and gives her own explanation ("paint") first.
- **Generic figures.** Alice Russell, jurors, police, lawyers and neighbours are generic 1890s figures. Real judges and lawyers are not named or portrayed.
- **Our own graphics.** Every card, label and newspaper is our own typed graphic. No court document, transcript page, newspaper masthead or photograph is imitated. The houses are generic and are labelled as illustrations, not the real buildings.
- **No invented speech.** Nobody speaks except the narrator, and no quotes are put in anyone's mouth.
- **Drama versus record.** One line acknowledges the Netflix show; everything after it comes from the record above.
