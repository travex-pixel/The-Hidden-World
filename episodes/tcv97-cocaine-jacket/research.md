# Case File 97 — A Jacket Made of Cocaine (WHERE THEY HID IT · 6)

Research ledger, built 23 October 2026. Engine v8, lane: customs. Series rule for this one: drugs that were turned INTO
everyday things (not hidden inside them). Every case is a seizure by a named agency, so each is a settled fact (the drugs
exist and were found); nobody accused is named, and the two women arrested in Kathmandu appear only as silhouettes.

## Method and limits

- Web search (standard mode), 15 queries, 23 Oct 2026. Full pages could not be opened from the build environment
  (WebFetch returned a DNS error for english.ratopati.com), so every claim below rests on **search-result extracts**,
  cross-checked between at least two independent outlets. Hamro Patro republishes Ratopati, so the two count as ONE
  source; The Himalayan Times and Kantipur are independent of it and of each other.
- Access date for every URL: 2026-10-23.
- Where outlets disagree on a number (the Kathmandu weight, the Valencia weight and arrest count), the reel uses only what
  two of them agree on, or shows the range on screen.

## Corrections to the brief and claims NOT used

- **Kathmandu weight:** the brief's "about 4.26 kg" is Ratopati's figure; The Himalayan Times says 4.28 kg and Kantipur
  about 4 kg. Narrated as "Over 4 kilos".
- **The X-ray miss:** only Kantipur says the airport X-ray could not detect it; no second source. NOT used on screen or
  in the narration. **The tip:** no extract mentions a tip-off in the Kathmandu case (Kantipur says the bureau profiles
  arriving foreign nationals with suspicious travel details; single source, not used). The gotcha says only what two
  sources agree on: the search in Thamel and the second arrest.
- **The Manila case:** the brief said "liquid marijuana" and "Toronto-to-Manila". The extracts say the towels and duvets
  were suspected to be soaked with liquid **methamphetamine**, the kush was separate (4.7 kg), and the boxes came from
  **Canada** (no city given). Corrected; the on-screen label keeps "suspected".
- **Marble slabs (Spain, Feb 2026):** checked; the reports say cocaine hidden IN marble tables, countertops and sinks,
  not moulded into slabs. Outside the "turned into" rule; not used.
- **Dubai clothes soaked in liquid cocaine:** several Dubai cases exist (2012, 2018, an Australian tourist), but no year
  and verdict could be tied together in two sources. Not used.
- **Cardboard boxes impregnated with cocaine (Spain, May 2020)** and **cocaine chemically bound into charcoal (UK)**:
  verified in part, not used (three cases are enough; the 2020 case's coverage names people).
- Not used: the two women's names, ages beyond the claims ledger, nationalities (none needed for the story), the Qatar
  Airways route, Kantipur's annual Rs 467.1 million figure, the 2016 shampoo-bottle liquid cocaine case.

## Ethics decisions

- The hook states a seizure (a fact), agent-first with a making verb: "Drug smugglers turned cocaine into a jacket." No
  accused person is in the hook. "Drug smugglers" refers to the scheme the bureau seized, not to a named person.
- The two women are arrested, not convicted: never named, never given a face (dark rim-lit silhouettes, NOT A LIKENESS),
  and the allegation that one came to collect the drugs is attributed ("Police say").
- No arrest was reported in the Manila case: nobody drawn as a suspect there. The Valencia arrests are a typed label only.
- Officers, the examiner and the chemist are generic figures without insignia; no weapons; no force.
- No logos, plates or real interfaces: the X-ray image is our own false-colour drawing; the container and pallets are
  unbranded; the evidence room, the searched room, the warehouse, the X-ray bay, the port and the lab are illustrations.
- The joke is on the fakes (a cocaine jacket, soaked towels, pallets made of cocaine), never on a person.
