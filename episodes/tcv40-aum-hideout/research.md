# Case File 40 — the Tokyo subway sarin attack and the arrest of Aum Shinrikyo's leader (1995)
Research ledger, verified 2026-10-13. Episode id `tcv40-aum-hideout` (short cut, 44–52 s, "Danger Zones" series).

## Method and limits
- Web search extracts only (11 searches, "standard" mode). WebFetch/curl are blocked, so no article was read in full;
  every narrated claim rests on at least two search-result extracts.
- Reputable outlets reached: AFP copy (Malay Mail, Kuwait Times, Daily Maverick), Jiji Press (via Adnkronos), NPR (WLRN),
  CBC, CBS, AP (WRAL), The Japan Times (tag page), The Christian Science Monitor (1995), Maclean's 1995 (via The Canadian
  Encyclopedia), The Daily Telegraph (PressReader), HISTORY.com, Bungei Shunju (bunshun.jp, Japanese), Wikipedia.
- NHK World, Reuters and the BBC did not surface directly (CBS cites the BBC for the follower figure).

## Fun fact (one card, also narrated)
- **Police carried canaries in cages as gas detectors.** Time (April 1995, via a University of Pittsburgh Supercourse
  lecture extract: more than 500 police in protective gear "carrying caged canaries as gas detectors"); Maclean's 1995
  (via The Canadian Encyclopedia: "hundreds of police wearing gas masks and carrying canaries in cages"). Tied to the
  compound searches in March–April, so narration says only "They carried canaries to warn of gas" after the raid line.

## Lesson (one card)
- **In a chemical incident: get out to fresh air, take off outer clothing, call for help.** Ready.gov (FEMA, archived):
  get out without passing through the contaminated area / find clean air; NYC Health: remove shoes and outer layers and
  bag them; Arizona EIN: remove clothing in contact with the body. Narration: "In a gas attack, get out to fresh air, fast."

- The attack, the chemical, victims and injuries are never shown; the station is a taped entrance with masked responders.
- Asahara (convicted, executed) appears only as an anonymous silhouette, small, never heroic; no cult symbols, texts or
  words; no followers shown. Tag "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS" on his shots.
- Police, responders and the judge are generic Japanese figures with no insignia; no real signage, logos or documents.
- One unnamed memorial line: "Remember the fourteen who died."
- No stereotyping: the "danger" angle is a contrast with Japan's verified safety ranking.
