# Case File 56 — The dog with a bounty (Sombra, Colombia, 2018): research ledger

Verified: 2026-10-16. Builder: Case File 56 agent.

## Method and limits
- Research used web-search result extracts only (WebFetch and curl are not used in this environment); every URL below was
  seen as a search result on 2026-10-16 and is cited for the extract shown there.
- Each narrated claim maps to at least two independent outlets. Where outlets disagree (the bounty amount, the cocaine
  total), the script avoids the disputed precise figure or attributes it ("Reportedly") and shows the range on a card.
- Spanish-language outlets were used for local detail (El Espectador, El Heraldo, Blu Radio, RPP, Noticias RCN, Cambio).

- **D_stat (STATISTIC)** — 245 arrests credited to her work at the José María Córdova and El Dorado airports (police, July 2018).
  CBS/CNN; WTKR; Global News; El Espectador; El Heraldo.
- **D_move** — Moved from the Turbo/Urabá area to El Dorado International Airport in Bogotá, away from the gang's stronghold.
  CBS/CNN; NPR; AP; El Espectador/AFP.
- **D_guards** — At El Dorado she works with her usual handler plus extra officers as escort (CBS/CNN, WTKR, NPR); AP: after

## Allegations / attributed statements
- **D_cost** — A police spokesperson told The Telegraph that the high reward showed the impact she had had on the gang's
  profits (via BuzzFeed; CBS Texas; CBS Philadelphia). Narration paraphrases, no quote: "Police said the price showed what she
  cost them."
- El Espectador and others say the bounty came from Otoniel personally; the script only says the gang ("a cartel", "the
  gang's boss" for the capture), not that he personally ordered it.

## Fun fact (SUCCESs)
- **D_name** — "Sombra" means "shadow" in Spanish (AP: "whose name means 'shadow' in Spanish"; BuzzFeed; dictionary meaning).

## Statistic
- 245 arrests at two airports (Colombian police, July 2018) — counter in s04 with a credit row "COLOMBIAN POLICE, JULY 2018".

## Lesson (save card, for the viewer who flies)
- Rows: CARRY NOTHING FOR ANYONE · PACK YOUR OWN BAG · PAID TO CARRY? WALK AWAY.
- UK FCDO / NCA "Travel Aware": do not carry items across a border for someone else; you are responsible for anything you
  carry; an offer of money or a free holiday to carry items is a warning sign. https://travelaware.campaign.gov.uk/drug-smuggling-abroad/
- Smartraveller (Australian Government): do not carry items for someone else; do not accept new luggage or use bags that
  are not yours. https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/following-law/carrying-or-using-drugs
- PNG Customs: pack your bags yourself; do not carry anything for anyone else unless you know what it is.
  https://customs.gov.pg/border/drugs

## Myths and claims NOT used
- A precise bounty amount presented as fact (disputed: $7,000 vs $70,000).
- "10 tonnes" (totals vary 2 to 10 t across outlets).
- Sombra's retirement or adoption (unverified).
- That Otoniel personally ordered the bounty (reported by some outlets, not narrated).
- The C$91,000 / R900,000 / $100,000 conversions in some outlets (exchange-rate restatements of the same disputed figure).
