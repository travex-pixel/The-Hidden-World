# Fact check: "What If Earth Stopped Spinning for 1 Second?" (What-If 002)

**Accessed:** 2026-10-13.

## Method and limitations (read first)
* **Snippets only:** every source below was seen only as a search-result snippet. Pages could not be opened from the
  sandbox. No URL was invented; each one appeared in a search result.
* **The scenario is impossible.** No known process could stop Earth in one second. The film is a cartoon thought
  experiment and says so on screen. You, Sam, Kai, the street and the clock are fictional.
* **Labelled speculation:** the size of the ocean surge and what the restart would do are labelled HYPOTHETICAL. No
  source models a one-second stop followed by a restart.

## Trend research (why this concept)
A separate scan of what-if channels from 2025–26 picked this concept. It was also snippet-only, from third-party
trackers such as OutlierKit, vidIQ and ThoughtLeaders.
* **Format:** "familiar anchor + one extreme rule" drives the leading what-if channels. One tracker gives What If
  (@whatifscienceshow) about 8.9M subscribers and about 1.1M average views.
* **Topic:** "Earth stops spinning for one second" kept getting fresh explainers in 2024–25 (WION, Business Today).
* **Packaging:** short concrete titles, and thumbnail text of four words or fewer that does not repeat the title.
* **None of those figures appear in the film.**

## Claims used

| E0 | illustrative | The scenario, the characters and the street are fiction. | none |
| E1 | established | The equator moves at about 1,670 km/h (465 m/s). Speed scales with the cosine of latitude: about 1,180 km/h at 45°, close to zero at the poles. | S1, S2, S3 |
| E2 | established | Sound travels at about 343 m/s, or 1,235 km/h, at 20 °C. | S6 |
| E3 | established | Anything not attached keeps moving east at rotation speed, including the atmosphere and the oceans. | S3, S4, S5 |
| E4 | estimate | Our arithmetic: a drop of 465 m/s in 1 s is 465 m/s², which is 465 / 9.81 ≈ 47 g. | S1 (input speed) |
| E5 | established | Fighter pilots are trained to sustain about 9 g. Untrained people black out far lower (sources vary, around 4–6 g), so the film says only "long before that". | S7, S8 |
| E6 | established | The record tornado wind is about 301 mph ± 20 mph (≈484 km/h), measured by Doppler on Wheels at Bridge Creek–Moore on 3 May 1999. A reprocessed figure of 321 mph is unconfirmed and not used. | S9, S10 |
| E8 | established | There are no penguins at the South Pole; they live on the coast. The most southern colony, of Adélie penguins at Cape Royds, is about 1,400 km from the Pole. | S11, S12, S13 |
| E9 | established | Tidal braking adds about 2.3 ms per century to the length of the day. The observed average from eclipse records is about 1.7–1.8 ms per century (Durham-led study). | E10 | illustrative | The size of the ocean surge, and the restart as a second lurch, are speculation. | none |

## Not used
* **Dropped as unverified:** claims that the magnetic field or temperature would change in the one-second scenario.
* **Dropped as attributed but unverified:** ScienceABC's "atmosphere at about 1,770 km/h" credited to NASA. The film
  uses only the surface speed.
* **Not shown:** any death toll.
