# Case File 55: The real Cocaine Bear (tcv55-cocaine-bear)

Research ledger, 2026-10-16 (all accesses on this date).

## Method and limits
- 11 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read (including the original New York Times and AP reports of September and December 1985, which are cited only as quoted by later outlets).
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support, and the disagreement is recorded below.
- No paid APIs. Nature column: **fact** = reported consistently by independent outlets; **reported** = one party's account (here, the shop that displays the stuffed bear), attributed in narration ("said to be").

## Corrections to the brief
- **"The bear ate 75 lb / a $2 million bag":** wrong. The state medical examiner (Dr Kenneth Alonso) said the stomach was "packed to the brim with cocaine" but estimated only 3-4 g reached its bloodstream; Snopes: the bear ate far less than a pound and did not eat 75 lb. A 75-lb bag was a *different* bag found on 5 Nov 1985 (AP, via FOX 5 Atlanta). The "$2 million" value is not used. The narration says only "It ripped open 40 packets" and "Its stomach was packed with cocaine."
- **"$4,500 cash", "knives/guns":** not found in two extracts for his body (one extract lists "two handguns ... and knives" in the bag). Not used; no weapon is shown or mentioned.
- **What was on Thornton:** about **79 lb** of cocaine at his side / strapped to him (NYT 1985 as quoted; AP via ADN; others "about 75-80 lb"; reported street value ~$15 million). The counter uses 79 LB.
- **Where he died:** Knoxville, Tennessee, 11 Sept 1985; extracts differ between the resident's **backyard** and a **gravel driveway**, so neither word is used (label: "Found dead that morning by a resident").
- **Stuffed bear:** displayed by the Kentucky for Kentucky Fun Mall in Lexington, but its chain of custody rests on the shop's own account and WAVE 3 / Saving Country Music report doubts; the narration says "said to be". The Waylon Jennings / Reno pawn-shop story is **not used** (single-party account).
- **Date the bear was found:** most extracts say about three months later, around 20 Dec 1985 (NYT report 23 Dec 1985); one says a hunter found it in November. Narration: "Three months later"; label "DECEMBER 1985".
