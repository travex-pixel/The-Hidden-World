# Case File 106: research ledger (ONE SMUGGLER, format v10): THE CABIN CREW

Built 26 October 2026. Lane: customs (an airport crew checkpoint). One person, a concluded case.

## Verdict, first

**Marsha Gay Reynolds, the JetBlue flight attendant who ran from a crew checkpoint at LAX, qualifies.** On 18 March 2016
she came to the "Known Crewmember" checkpoint at LAX Terminal 4 with her crew credentials. She was picked at random for
extra screening. She dropped her bags, took off her heels and ran barefoot down an up escalator. Her abandoned luggage
held about 60 lb (just under 27 kg per the plea release) of cocaine in 11 packages. The next day she flew to New York,
and on 23 March she surrendered to the DEA at JFK. In December 2016 she **pleaded guilty** (federal court, Los Angeles)
to conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute cocaine. She then **testified against her supplier**, Gaston Brown,
who was convicted at trial in February 2018. In April 2018 she was **sentenced to time served** (about two years in
custody) plus three years' supervised release, and the judge cited her cooperation. Brown got 165 months (about 14 years)
in July 2022.

One person, a plea and a sentence, more than two independent sources for every narrated claim, and a sourced "how she
was caught" moment (she ran). The half-reveal object is her crew roller bag being unzipped at the checkpoint, with her
heels left beside it.

## The story in order

1. **Who she was.** A JetBlue flight attendant from New York (R1). The sources say little else that holds up in two
   independent places (see "Not narrated").
2. **The deal.** Her supplier paid her to carry cocaine and cash through crew checkpoints at LAX and JFK, where crew get
   much lighter screening (R3, R10).
3. **The checkpoint.** At LAX on 18 March 2016 she showed her crew badge at the crew checkpoint and was picked at random
   for extra screening (R2, R4).
4. **The run.** She left her bags, kicked off her heels and ran barefoot down an up escalator (R5). The bags held 11
   packages, nearly 60 lb of cocaine (R6, R7).
5. **The flight home.** The next day she flew to New York (R8). Five days after LAX she turned herself in at JFK (R9).
6. **The outcome.** She pleaded guilty in December 2016 (R11) and testified against her supplier, who was convicted in
   February 2018 (R12). She got time served in April 2018 (R13). He got about 14 years in July 2022 (R14).

## Naming decision

**Named: Marsha Gay Reynolds.** She pleaded guilty and was sentenced. She is named by the US Attorney's Office, AP, NBC,
CBS, ABC7 and the Jamaica Gleaner. She was an adult (about 31 at the arrest). She is drawn **only as a dark rim-lit
silhouette tagged NOT A LIKENESS**. **The supplier (Gaston Brown) is not named** in the reel ("her supplier"). He was
convicted, but he is not the subject and the name adds nothing. No family member is mentioned. No line puts her name next
to an accusing word without "pleaded guilty" in the same line.

## Sensitive handling

- The joke is on the scheme (a crew lane built for trust, a sprint in bare feet, a bag left behind), never on her as a
  woman or on her looks. No beauty-queen angle and no "glamour" framing.
- No one is hurt. The TSA and airport officers are generic figures with no badge, patch, agency insignia or weapon.
- Airline and programme names appear as plain text only ("JetBlue", "crew lane"). No livery, logo, uniform design or real
  badge is copied. The record card is our own graphic.
- Drugs: no method beyond what was reported as found (packages in a bag). No glamorising.
- The supplier is a convicted person and is not named. The end-card vote is about a sentence, not about guilt.
