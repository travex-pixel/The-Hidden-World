## How this research was done (read first)
- Research used the live web-search tool in this environment. **Direct page fetching was blocked by the environment's network policy** (WebFetch DNS failures and proxy 403s on abc.net.au, nma.gov.au, localcourt.nt.gov.au, wikipedia.org and others). Every claim therefore rests on **search-result extracts of the cited URLs**, cross-checked across independent outlets. No full primary document (coroner's findings PDF, Morling report) was read end to end.
- Wikipedia was used only as a pointer, never as sole support.
- Key on-screen claims were re-checked independently by the producer (not only the research agent): the sound-deadener finding (NMA, ABC, Canberra Times/ACM syndication), the jacket found on 2 Feb 1986 near a dingo lair during the search for a fallen climber (ADB, Macau Daily Times, NMA), release on 7 Feb 1986 (ADB), convictions quashed 15 Sep 1988 (EBSCO, Famous Trials), and the 2012 finding "attacked and taken by a dingo" (Al Jazeera, CSM/AP, Alice Springs News, IOL).
- **Before publishing**, a person with normal internet access should open the 2012 findings PDF (https://localcourt.nt.gov.au/sites/default/files/decisions/2012NTMC020.pdf) and confirm the quoted phrase. Every outlet quotes it identically.

## Ethical and legal review (Phase 3)
- **Legal status:** Lindy and Michael Chamberlain's convictions were quashed (1988), and the 2012 coroner found a dingo responsible. The video never suggests otherwise. It does **not** claim the Royal Commission "declared them innocent", because it did not (NT Attorney-General, Hansard, June 1987).
- **Avoided wording:** the pop-culture misquote "a dingo ate my baby", the unverified claim that lab results were "faked", the name of the climber (a private individual), and the disputed compensation details.
- **No depiction of** the baby, of violence, or of blood. The dashboard speckle is drawn as dark compound, never red.
- Every illustrated object or place carries an **ILLUSTRATION** tag, the jacket and clothing say **NOT THE ACTUAL JACKET / NOT ACTUAL ITEMS**, and the court and coroner cards are our own typed graphics, not replicas of records.
- Lindy Chamberlain-Creighton is alive, so the tone is factual and respectful. There are no "dingo jokes", and the ending is a memorial line for Azaria.

## 2. People and roles

- **Azaria Chantel Loren Chamberlain:** the victim, nine weeks old.
- **Lindy Chamberlain (later Chamberlain-Creighton):** Azaria's mother. Convicted of murder in 1982, released in 1986, convictions quashed in 1988.
- **Michael Chamberlain (1944–2017):** Azaria's father and a Seventh-day Adventist pastor. Convicted as an accessory, then exonerated.
- **Denis Barritt:** coroner at the first inquest (1981 dingo finding).
- **Gerry Galvin:** coroner at the second inquest (1982 committal).
- **Justice Trevor Morling:** Royal Commissioner (1986–87).
- **Marshall Perron:** NT Attorney-General who announced the remission of Lindy's sentence in 1986.
- **Asche CJ, Nader J, Kearney J:** the NT Court of Criminal Appeal bench that quashed the convictions in 1988.
- **John Lowndes:** coroner at the third inquest (1995 open finding).
- **Elizabeth Morris:** NT Coroner at the fourth inquest (2012 dingo finding).
- **Murphy J and Deane J:** High Court dissenters (1984).
- **Dr Joy Kuhl:** prosecution forensic biologist whose "foetal haemoglobin" test results were central at trial. Her name came from search extracts in this research, and her role is well documented in the trial and Royal Commission literature.
- **Prof. Barry Boettcher:** defence and inquiry scientist. He testified that the anti-foetal-haemoglobin tests gave so many false positives they were effectively useless.
- **David Brett:** English tourist who fell from Uluru in Jan 1986. The search for his remains led to the jacket.
- **Stuart Tipple:** the family's lawyer, active up to 2017.

## 3. Established facts (official findings)

1. **Convictions.** On 29 Oct 1982 Lindy was convicted of murder and given a mandatory life sentence with hard labour. Michael was convicted as an accessory after the fact and given an 18-month suspended sentence.
2. **Appeals failed.** The 1983 Federal Court appeal failed, and the High Court dismissed the appeal 3–2 on 22 Feb 1984.
3. **Release.** The matinee jacket was found on 2 Feb 1986 near dingo lairs. Lindy was released on 7 Feb 1986 by executive remission.
4. **Royal Commission.** The Morling Royal Commission (report signed 22 May 1987, tabled 2 Jun 1987) found serious doubts about guilt. It found that the trial judge would have had to direct an acquittal had its evidence been before the jury.
5. **Convictions quashed.** All convictions were quashed on 15 Sep 1988.
6. **Final cause of death.** On 12 Jun 2012 the coroner found Azaria died at Ayers Rock on 17 Aug 1980, and that the cause of death was being "**attacked and taken by a dingo**". She applied the coronial balance-of-probabilities standard, scaled to the seriousness of the finding (*Briginshaw*). Reports quote her as satisfied the evidence was "adequate, clear, cogent and exact" and "excludes all other reasonable possibilities".

## 5. Allegations, disputes and contradictions

- **Morling's report was not a declaration of innocence.** The NT Attorney-General stressed in 1987 that it "makes no declaration of innocence". Full official closure came only in 2012.
- **The 1995 inquest was "open".** It found the cause unknown. That is what allowed doubters to persist until 2012.
- **Compensation is disputed.** Sources disagree on whether the payment was *ex gratia* or court-ordered, and whether the NT or "Australian" government paid. Say only "about $1.3 million in 1992".
- **Several dates conflict between sources:**
  - first inquest quashed: 18 vs 20 Nov 1981
  - trial opening: 13 vs 3 Sep 1982
  - jacket found: 2 vs 3 Feb 1986
  - third inquest: Nov vs 13 Dec 1995
  - Morling report: 22 May (signed) vs 2 Jun 1987 (tabled)

  Safer on-screen wording: "February 1986", "1987", "1995".
- **"Jacket" vs "cardigan".** Some popular sources call the garment a "cardigan". The official and NMA term is "matinee jacket".
- **The famous phrase.** "A dingo ate my baby" is the pop-culture misquote. The NFSA and film material render it as "The dingo's got my baby!" Avoid presenting either as Lindy's verbatim words unless checked against trial or witness transcripts.
- **Exact condolence wording in 2012 varies.** The NMA version reads: "Please accept my sincere sympathies on the death of your special and loved daughter and sister Azaria." Media versions are shorter.

## 6. Strongest storytelling angle

**"The jacket nobody believed in."** Lindy always said Azaria wore a matinee jacket over her jumpsuit. It was never found, and she went to prison for life.

More than five years later, a stranger's fatal fall from Uluru sent searchers into dingo-lair country at the base of the rock. There, half-buried, was the jacket. Five days later she was free.

Science then finished the reversal. The "baby's blood" sprayed under the car's dashboard turned out to be factory sound-deadener. This is a chain of evidence with no gore.

## 7. Most compelling verified revelation

The "foetal blood spray" under the Torana's dashboard, a pillar of the prosecution, was **sound-deadening compound applied at the factory**. Morling found no blood in it.

Add to that the matinee jacket, found on 2 Feb 1986 only because of a search for a fallen climber, and the coroner's 2012 words "attacked and taken by a dingo". Together they make the cleanest "the evidence was wrong" arc.

## 8. Suitability for a Short

- **High.** The arc is clear and fits roughly 45–60 seconds: disappearance, then conviction, then a chance discovery, then the science overturned, then a 32-year final ruling.
- **No graphic content is needed.** The story turns on objects (tent, jacket, car, a lab result).
- **Legal and ethical risk is low.** Everyone has been formally exonerated. Lindy Chamberlain-Creighton is alive, so treat her with respect and avoid "dingo jokes" (The Conversation, 2012: https://theconversation.com/azaria-chamberlain-inquest-forget-the-dingo-jokes-and-recognise-lindys-trauma-7590).
- **Be careful with:**
  - do not say Morling "declared them innocent"
  - do not use the "faked" lab claim
  - do not quote "a dingo ate my baby" as her words
  - keep the compensation wording simple
