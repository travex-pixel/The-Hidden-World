- **Research date:** 2026-10-12.

## Method and limits

- **Only search-result extracts were used.** WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment. Every claim comes from WebSearch result summaries of the URLs cited, or from the dossier, which used the same method. No full page was read, and quotes are as the extracts gave them.
- **Searches:** 9 WebSearch calls in standard mode, under the 12 allowed for this assignment and the brief's cap of 15.
- **Where a detail came from:** sometimes a search summary did not say which URL a detail came from. In that case the claim cites the group of URLs it appeared with, and this ledger says so.
- **Labels:**
  - VERIFIED means at least two independent reputable sources: FBI, court filings, museum, major outlets or university archives.
  - Wikipedia, content farms and aggregators (for example the distractify/phpfox mirror that came up) are pointers only and are never counted as a source.
- **Scope:** I did not re-check anything about 2024–2026 media or anniversaries. The case is closed, so nothing in this episode depends on recent events.

## Fun fact (one card, no narration; scene 9)

`M_car_museum`: the Mercury Marquis McVeigh was driving when he was arrested was added to the Oklahoma City National Memorial & Museum's collection. The 2013 announcement said it would be shown with video of the traffic stop, and a later report describes it as a key exhibit.

- Sources: Public Radio Tulsa (AP), 21 April 2013; News On 6 (2013); KRMG (2013); KGOU (exhibits preview before the museum reopened).
- The card says "later went on display" and gives no year, because the extracts do not give the date it first went on show.

## Conflicting details and how they were handled

- **"90 minutes":**
  - The FBI says "just 90 minutes".
  - famous-trials.com gives a stop time of 10:20 a.m., which is about 78 minutes after 9:02.
  - NLEOMF says "seventy minutes".
  - Handling: the narration says "within about 90 minutes" and the label says "Per the FBI".
- **How close he came to release:**
  - History.com and The Globe and Mail say shortly before or just before he was due to be released.
  - The FBI 2005 story says the hold kept him from being released.
  - constitution.org (a secondary compilation) says "a few hours"; Wikipedia says "minutes" (a pointer only).
  - Handling: the narration says "about to be released" and gives no number of hours or minutes.
- **Distance north of Oklahoma City:** given as about 60, 62, 75 or 80 miles. Not used. The label says "north of Oklahoma City".
- **When the axle was found:** the day of the bombing (AP 1997), 20 April (FBI) or the day after. Not used.
- **Rental date:** 17 April in most sources, 15 April in one (possibly the reservation date). Not used on screen.
- **Who found and recorded the axle:**
  - Oklahoma City police technician Mike McPherson (Memorial Museum).
  - FBI material associates Special Agent Barry Black with it.
  - Handling: neither is named. The narration says the number "led the FBI to Kansas".
- **Car colour:** "yellow", "rusting yellow" (Tampa Bay Times) and "pale yellow" (defense filing). Drawn as a faded pale yellow with rust spots.

## Myths and claims NOT used

- **"The trooper knew he had caught the bomber."** A myth. He was held on misdemeanors, and the link was made two days later. The script never implies the trooper knew.
- **"The plate alone identified him."** Not said. The axle number, the rental shop and the sketch identified him; the stop held him. The callback says the plate "held him".
- **John Doe No. 2 as an open mystery.** Not mentioned. The FBI's position is that it was a misidentification.
- **"Bob Kling" (alias) and the Dreamland Motel name.** Not used, to save words. The card says "matched at a motel".
- **The exact time before release ("hours" or "minutes").** Not used; the sources conflict.
- **McVeigh's statements, motive or ideology.** Deliberately excluded.

## Ethics decisions

- **The toll is said once, plainly:** "His truck bomb killed 168 people, including 19 children." It appears on a quiet typed card in a task-force room, with the score stepped back. The video closes with "And we remember the 168."
- **What is never depicted:** the explosion, the Murrah Building, damage, victims, rescue scenes, injuries and famous photographs (including the firefighter photograph).
- **McVeigh:**
  - He appears only as an anonymous rim-lit silhouette (`characters/figures/mc22_driver.json`, `real_person: true`) or as a dark head shape through a rear window.
  - He has no face or likeness, and his booking photo is never recreated.
  - He is never posed heroically and never shown with a weapon.
  - Every shot he appears in is tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS".
- **The trooper:**
  - Two sources agree he was Trooper Charles "Charlie" Hanger of the Oklahoma Highway Patrol: NLEOMF, the Tampa Bay Times and the Blackwell Journal-Tribune (via the dossier).
  - He is a living person, so he is shown only as a generic figure (`mc22_trooper`) and is not named on screen or in the narration; he appears only in his role.
- **Weapons:** none are drawn. The gun is mentioned only as part of the charges ("gun and traffic charges").
- **No brand:** "Ryder" never appears on screen. The rental counter, the box trucks through the window and the patrol car carry no name, colours, crest or logo.
- **No real material is imitated:**
  - The axle's number is shown only as masked blocks.
  - The composite sketch is never shown.
  - The jail, highway, lab and court are generic.
  - Every card is the series' own typed graphic.
- **Families:** no victim or family member is named or shown.
  - The dossier notes that families were divided on the execution. That is not addressed in 40 seconds, and nothing in the script celebrates the execution: it is stated in four words.
