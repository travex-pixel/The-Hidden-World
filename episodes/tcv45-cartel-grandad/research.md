## Corrections to the brief
- **Home state:** the brief said Sharp was "from Michigan". He lived and grew daylilies in **Michigan City, Indiana** (AP headlines: "Ind. man, 87"; ABC7 Chicago: "Michigan City, Indiana"). He was *stopped* in Michigan. The map labels both; the narration says "Back home" and the label says "MICHIGAN CITY, INDIANA".
- **Who stopped him:** Michigan State Police troopers made the traffic stop (with a police dog); Wikipedia and the Daily Mirror add that the DEA had been tracking him (DEA Special Agent Jeff Moore). The narration says "Michigan troopers pulled him over"; the DEA is not named on screen (only one class of source for the DEA detail in extracts).
- **"104 kg":** reports give **104 brick-shaped packages, about 228 lb** (one says 229 lb); Motor City Muckraker gives 104 kilograms. 228 lb = 103.4 kg. The narration says "104 bricks"; the chip and label say "104 KG" / "ABOUT 104 KG".
- **Release:** "released early on health grounds" holds: released in 2015 after about a year, for humanitarian reasons / declining health (ABC7 Chicago epitaph; Wikipedia). Died 12 December 2016, aged 92.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
Spoken cue: "Offered an easy delivery job? Save these three warning signs." (the plain "three signs" version was misheard as "Often ... science" by the recogniser)
Rows: "A 'JOB' FORWARDING PARCELS" / "MOVING CASH FOR STRANGERS" / "TOLD TO RUSH OR STAY QUIET"; credit row "FTC · FBI · Boston Police Dept."
- Row 1: FTC consumer alert, "Can you unbox the signs of a reshipping scam?" (Dec 2025) https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/12/can-you-unbox-signs-reshipping-scam (a job receiving goods and mailing them on is a reshipping scam; "reshipping is never a real job"); FBI red flags as reported by NBC Chicago https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/local/victims-used-as-money-mules-officials-warn/2710/ ("receiving packages and reshipping them to another location").
- Row 2: FBI red flags (NBC Chicago: picking up money and sending it elsewhere; opening or sharing a bank account); FDIC OIG public service alert https://www.fdicoig.gov/public-service-alert-money-mules ; Boston Police (do not receive or transfer money at the instruction of someone met online or by phone).
- Row 3: Boston Police Department community alert, 27 Sept 2025 https://police.boston.gov/2025/09/27/bpd-community-alert-scams-targeting-older-adults-involving-money-mules (pressure to act quickly, secrecy about the reason); Journal of Accountancy (Sept 2019) https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2019/sep/money-mule-schemes.html .
- Caption extra (not on screen): FTC advice to talk it over with someone you trust; report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov; people 60+ can call the National Elder Fraud Hotline, 1-833-FRAUD-11 (Boston Police alert).
The lesson is framed as protecting an older relative; it does not suggest Sharp was scammed (he pleaded guilty, and prosecutors said he was paid).
