Researched 2026-10-15. **Every source below was read through search-result snippets only**: this sandbox cannot open web
pages (DNS lookups for nps.gov and wikipedia.org fail). An editor must check each claim against the full source before

## Concept and why
The brief: something cool and obscure, with places that are instantly recognisable. The hero is the most recognisable
statue on Earth, and the obscure hook is real and checkable: her right heel is raised, so she is mid-step. The route
gives one famous place per scene: Liberty Island and the Manhattan skyline, 1884 Paris, New York Harbor with the Brooklyn
Bridge and the Staten Island Ferry, the Titanic, the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Mont-Saint-Michel, the Arc de Triomphe, the
Eiffel Tower and the Île aux Cygnes replica. The closing line-up adds Christ the Redeemer, the Sphinx, the moai, Big Ben
and the Leaning Tower of Pisa.

## Worked numbers
**W5, walking speed.** Animals of very different sizes prefer to walk at about the same Froude number,
Fr = v²/(gL) ≈ 0.25, where L is leg length (S7, S8; it is a rule of thumb, and one large study found larger animals walk
at lower Fr). Her heel-to-head height is 33.86 m (S2); a human's leg is about 0.53 of standing height, so L ≈ 18 m.
v = √(0.25 × 9.81 × 18) = √44.1 ≈ 6.6 m/s ≈ 24 km/h. New York to Paris is ≈5,837 km great-circle (S9):
5,837 / 23.9 ≈ 244 h ≈ 10.2 days. Ignored: water drag (she walks most of the way on the sea floor), the detour, and
whether the Froude rule holds at 46 m. On screen: "Our sum … ≈ 24 km/h → ~10 days", labelled ESTIMATE.

**W8, pressure.** NOAA: pressure rises about 1 atmosphere per 10 m of seawater (S14). 3,800 m / 10 m ≈ 380 atm
(381 absolute). The statue is a hollow copper shell on an open iron frame; flooded, the water inside is at the same
pressure as outside, so there is no net crushing load on the skin. This is our reasoning, not a tested result: the
narration says "she's hollow, and full of seawater, so it pushes back from inside just as hard". Labelled ESTIMATE.

**Comparisons used on screen.** Arc 50 m vs statue 46 m (W11, W2): she is 4 m shorter. Eiffel first floor 57 m vs 46 m
(W12): she does not reach it. "Eighty-nine years": 1937 to 2026. Replica 11.5 m vs 46 m: a quarter (W14).

## Not used, and why
* "Nobody has been allowed up to the torch since 1916" (the Black Tom explosion): sourced, but it did not fit the route.
* "Black smokers" on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge: the vents are drawn but not claimed in narration, because I did not source
  a specific vent field.
* Exact year she turned green: sources conflict, so the script says only that it took decades.
* "The replica originally faced east": sources disagree, so the script says only "since 1937, she has faced west".
* No quotations are used anywhere in the script.

## Places drawn, and what makes each recognisable
* **Liberty Island:** the Fort Wood star walls, Hunt's granite pedestal with its loggia band, the statue with seven
  crown rays, raised torch and tablet (JULY IV MDCCLXXVI).
* **Manhattan:** One World Trade Center's tapered, chamfered shaft and spire; the Empire State Building's setbacks and
  mast; the Chrysler Building's terraced arches; the Brooklyn Bridge's twin pointed arches and cable web.
* **New York Harbor:** the orange Staten Island Ferry, a tug, harbour seals and gulls.
* **Paris 1884:** zinc mansard roofs and chimney pots, the copper-coloured statue in scaffolding (no Eiffel Tower yet).
* **The Titanic:** a plumb stem, the forecastle rail converging on the bow, portholes, rusticles and the fallen
  foremast.
* **Mont-Saint-Michel:** the conical rock, ramparts, the village, La Merveille and the spire with its gilded archangel.
* **Paris:** the Arc de Triomphe with the tricolour in the arch; the Eiffel Tower's splayed legs, great arch, three
  platforms and lattice; the Seine and the Pont d'Iéna; the replica on the Île aux Cygnes with the tower behind.
* **Line-up:** Christ the Redeemer, the Sphinx and a pyramid, three moai, Big Ben's clock face and the Pisa tower.
