## Method and limits

## Corrections to the brief
- **Sentence date:** the brief said "sentenced in 2002". He pleaded guilty in 2002 but was **sentenced in January 2003** (AP via AccessWDUN, Jacksonville, 9 Jan 2003; Yahoo/AP copy): 37 months. The card says "JAN 2003 · 37 MONTHS IN PRISON".
- **$24 million:** this total comes from later reporting (Daily Beast 2018, Cox/AJC/WSB, Business Insider). The contemporaneous record is the September 2001 indictment: "more than $20 million worth" of winning pieces (UPI, Tampa Bay Times, Washington Times); the August 2001 arrest announcement said "more than $13 million". Narration therefore says **"Over 20 million dollars in prizes."**
- **"About 12 years (1989-2001)":** reported by the Daily Beast, Axios and Cox (2018); the indictment says "as far back as the late 1980s"; Jacobson himself testified at sentencing that he took most of the high-value pieces **from 1995 to 2001**; the FBI in 2001 said "as early as 1995". The narration says "For twelve years, nobody stopped him" (the 2018 reporting, consistent with "late 1980s" to 2001); the YEAR chip shows 1989 only as the reported start.
- **"Former police officer":** confirmed only by 2018 reporting (Daily Beast; Newsweek): about a year with the Hollywood, Florida, police, ending after an injury around 1980. Not in the 2001-2003 wire copy. Narration: "a former cop"; label "EX-POLICE OFFICER · Hollywood, Florida, as reported".
- **"The big prizes almost never went to ordinary customers":** no source gives a count of honest top-prize winners, so this is not narrated as a fact. The narration says what is sourced: he stole the top winning pieces (his own testimony: most high-value pieces 1995-2001).

## Fun fact (card)
"THE FBI POSED AS A / McDONALD'S FILM CREW." Daily Beast (2018) and Newsweek (2018) both report that two undercover agents posed as a McDonald's film crew at a "winner's" home. It passes SUCCESs (simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, a story). Plain text only; no logo is drawn.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
Spoken cue: "Got a prize message? Save these three rules."
Rows: "REAL PRIZES ARE FREE" / "NO GIFT CARDS OR WIRES" / "NEVER GIVE BANK DETAILS"; credit row "Source: FTC, consumer.ftc.gov".
FTC consumer advice "Fake Prize, Sweepstakes, and Lottery Scams" https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fake-prize-sweepstakes-lottery-scams ("If you have to pay to get your prize, it's a scam. Real prizes are free"; scammers ask for payment by wire, gift card, cash or cryptocurrency; there is no reason to give a bank account or card number or Social Security number to claim a prize); FTC consumer alert (April 2023) and the San Joaquin County DA's republication carry the same advice.
