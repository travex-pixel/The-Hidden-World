## Method and limits
- WebSearch result extracts only (15 searches); WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so no article page was read in full. Every claim below comes from at least two independent outlets' extracts, except where marked (and then it is not used on screen).
- The Fresno coverage all traces back to the same May 2013 reporting (AP, ABC News, KXTV/KGPE), republished by FindLaw, Police Magazine, APCO, The Christian Post and EMS1; the extracts agree on every fact used. No court outcome was found for the Fresno men or the Walnut Creek man, so both cases are ACCUSED/CHARGED only (never named, silhouettes tagged NOT A LIKENESS, "police say", AS OF OCT 2026). The Silver Spring man admitted seven break-ins and was sentenced (UPI, NBC Washington), so his outcome is stated; he is still a silhouette and never named on screen.
- No pocket-dial burglary case from 2024–2026 was found in the extracts (the newest hits were Houston 2015, Daytona Beach, Roswell, Shelby County OH and a New Jersey indictment, all older), so the Fresno case leads.

## Corrections to the brief
- **Date of the Fresno case:** the brief said October 2013. Every extract puts the arrests on **9 May 2013** (Police Magazine's report is dated 20 May 2013; ABC News/AP May 2013). Corrected to MAY 2013 on screen and in the caption.
- **Outcome of the Fresno case:** the brief asked for charges and any plea/conviction. Charges: arrested for investigation of burglary, conspiracy to commit a crime and possession of stolen property (AP: Sgt. Jaime Rios). No plea, trial or sentence was found in any extract, so the reel says "charged" and labels "no verdict found in reports".
- **Walnut Creek date:** KTVU and CBS San Francisco agree on every fact, but neither extract carries a date (searched three times, including an extended search). The on-screen label therefore says "the reports give no date", and the caption does not date the case. Flagged as an open risk.
- **"Nobody hung up":** the men did not notice the call; the dispatcher stayed on the line (AP: "the operator didn't hang up"). The line is literally accurate for both ends of the call.
