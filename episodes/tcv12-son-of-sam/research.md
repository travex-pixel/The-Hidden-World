## Method and limits

- Research date 2026-10-11. 12 web searches ran in "standard" mode; 3 further calls were refused by the search tool
  before running (blocked domains), which used up the 15-call budget. WebFetch and curl are blocked from the build
  environment, so every source below is a **search-result extract** (title, URL, summary text). No full article was
  read. Dates come from the URL or the extract; otherwise "date not in extract" is stated in `episode.json`.
- The search tool sometimes summarises a whole result set without saying which URL carried a detail. Where that
  happened, the claim cites the group and this ledger says so.
- Wikipedia, fan wikis and content farms (studentbridge, ticketlog, bollywoodshaadis, simplyforensic, fandom) appeared
  in the results. They are used only as pointers, never as one of the two sources.
- No quote, date, document or dialogue was invented. The letters are never quoted. No words of Berkowitz are quoted on
  screen or in the narration.
- "Not found" means the searches returned nothing. It does not prove a thing did not happen.

  sentenced to 25 years to life for each murder, consecutively (reported as the maximum then allowed).

  (One extract says he also pleaded guilty to attempted-murder counts; another describes extra assault/attempted-murder

## Fun facts with sources (two or more each)

1. **FUN FACT 1 (S_demon): the "demon dog" story was a hoax, by his own later account.** After his arrest he claimed that
   "Sam" was his neighbour, whose dog relayed a demon's orders to kill. He later retracted it: a 20 March 1979 letter to
   his psychiatrist, David Abrahamsen, called it "a silly hoax" (wording from the Wikipedia/Biography result group), and
   in the 1980 interview tapes used by Netflix's 2025 series he said he made the dog story up. Sources: Biography.com
   (biography facts); Netflix Tudum; Fox News (unearthed tapes); TIME (2025); Oxygen. Narration: "He later claimed Sam
   was a neighbour whose dog relayed a demon's orders. Then he admitted it was a hoax." Pop-up: "He later admitted the
   'demon dog' story was a hoax." Sparkle anchored to "admitted". The neighbour is not named beyond the first name
   already in the alias; the dog is not shown. Placed right after the letters (it explains the name), framed as "later".
2. **FUN FACT 2 (S_law): the "Son of Sam law".** New York passed it in 1977, after reports that Berkowitz might sell his
   story; it sent a criminal's earnings from telling the story of the crime to the victims. In 1991 (Simon & Schuster v.
   Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, a case over the Henry Hill book *Wiseguy*) the US Supreme Court held
   that version unconstitutional under the First Amendment; New York later broadened and revised the law. Sources: Free
   Speech Center at MTSU ("Son of Sam laws"); The Christian Science Monitor, 16 Dec 1991; Reporters Committee for Freedom
   of the Press (2002); CBS News (via the dossier). Narration: "New York passed a Son of Sam law against criminals
   profiting from their stories. In 1991, the Supreme Court struck it down." (The narration's "it" is the law New York
   passed; the card adds "A REVISED LAW FOLLOWED".) Sparkle anchored to "passed".

Cut for length (verified, not used): some women dyed their dark hair blonde in summer 1977 (CBS New York "Back Stories";
HISTORY.com; Jezebel on 1977 TV footage). Not used as a fun fact: "a 200-officer task force" (WNYC/AP only; the "Omega"
name only in the HISTORY.com extract).

## Ethics decisions

- **Survivor-first framing.** The six people killed and the seven wounded are counted, never shown, and not named. The
  last scene opens with "IN MEMORY OF THE SIX PEOPLE KILLED · and for the seven who were wounded" before the next-case
  card.
- **No violence on screen.** No shooting, no gun, no victim, no injured person, no crime scene. The Brooklyn street is a
  generic illustration tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT THE REAL STREET", and the label says "Generic street, not the scene";
  the ticketed car is a generic 1970s sedan.
- **Berkowitz is alive:** he appears only as an anonymous rim-lit silhouette (`characters/figures/ss12_berk.json`,
  `real_person: true`, "not a likeness"), only walking to his car, being arrested and standing in court, and every such
  shot carries "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS". His religious message and his own words are not platformed. The killer is
  never called anything admiring; the "demon" claim is presented only as a story he later admitted was a hoax.
- **The letters** are mentioned in one scene, never quoted, never shown; the envelopes on screen are plain and blank.
- **The witness** is a generic, unnamed silhouette with a dog. **Police** are generic 1970s figures with no badges,
  patches or logos. The newspaper press shows a blank generic paper web: no masthead, no real headline.
- **The hero ticket** is our own design: "PARKING TICKET / VIOLATION: FIRE HYDRANT / BROOKLYN · 31 JULY 1977", with no
  agency name, seal, amount, plate or real form layout.
- **No imitation** of real tickets, police forms, court papers or newspapers: all cards are the series' own typed cards.
- **Timing:** the 50th anniversaries of the 1976-77 attacks fall in Oct 2026 to Mar 2027. The episode is not tied to any
  attack date; the caption suggests no date hook.
