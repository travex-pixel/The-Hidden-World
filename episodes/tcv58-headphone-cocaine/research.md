# Case File 58: Cocaine in 18 headphone boxes (tcv58-headphone-cocaine)

Research ledger, 2026-10-16 (all accesses on this date). Legal status verified on 2026-10-16: Cassandra "Cassie" Sainsbury pleaded guilty under a plea deal and was sentenced in Bogotá on 2 November 2017; she was freed on parole in April 2020. The case is closed.

## Method and limits
- 10 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**; no full page was read. The BBC and Guardian reports were not returned by search and are not cited. - Nature column: **fact** = reported consistently by independent outlets or the court outcome; **police account** = what Colombian police said, attributed; **her claim / family claim** = her own or her family's account, attributed on screen and in narration.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Sentence date:** the brief said July 2018. **Wrong.** A Bogotá judge accepted the plea deal and sentenced her on **2 November 2017** (SBS, InDaily/AAP, The New Daily/AAP, AP via Inquirer). Six years: confirmed. Fine: US$90,000 (AAP); AP says "nearly $100,000". The fine is not narrated.
- **Release / return:** the brief said she was "released and returned to Australia in 2020". **Partly wrong.** She was freed on **parole** from El Buen Pastor women's prison on 17 April 2020 under Colombia's COVID-19 humanitarian release, after about three years, and had to stay in Colombia for a further 27 months (Nine News via AAP). Later reports place her back in Adelaide, but no extract gives the date she flew home, so the screen says only "paroled April 2020".
- **"18 sets of headphones":** police described **18 headphone boxes / 18 packages** (5.8 kg); her family said 15 pairs of headphones. The script says "18 headphone boxes" and never "18 pairs".
- **"She first said she thought she was carrying documents":** **not found** in any extract. Not used. What is reported: she told police she had bought the headphones to bring back to Australia for her wedding (AP); her family said they were gifts for her bridal party and that she was set up by a man she met in Colombia; later she told the court she agreed to carry the drugs only after her family was threatened (AAP, July 2017), which put the first plea deal in doubt; at sentencing the judge weighed that claim (AAP).
- **"Flew home":** she was arrested as she was about to board a flight **to London** (AP), so the script says "tried to fly out of Bogotá", not "flew home".
- **Arrest date:** 11 or 12 April 2017 (reports differ: Colombian police said 12 April; others say 11 April). The screen says only "April 2017".
- **"Personal trainer":** widely reported (BuzzFeed, PEDESTRIAN.TV, AAP: "former personal trainer"). A 60 Minutes claim about other work is not used (irrelevant and not about the offence).
- **Street value:** Colombian police put about US$1 million on it in one report only; no second source, so no money figure is used. The statistic is the weight.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
Spoken cue: "Someone asks you to carry a package abroad? Save this."
Rows: "CARRY NOTHING FOR OTHERS" / "PACK YOUR OWN BAG" / "FREE TRIP + EASY CASH = NO"; credit row "Smartraveller (Australia) · UK FCDO".
- Rows 1-2: Smartraveller (Australian Government): be aware of what is in your bags, do not transport anything on behalf of another person, do not leave bags with strangers or use bags that are not yours (https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/following-law/carrying-or-using-drugs ; https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/before-you-go/laws/drugs ). FCDO / NCA Travel Aware toolkit: always pack your own luggage and do not carry anything through customs for someone else (https://www.abtot.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/FCDO-Drug-Smuggling-Asset-Toolkit-Travel-Aware.pdf ).
- Row 3: GOV.UK (FCDO): criminal networks recruit young people with promises of free travel, easy money or "low-risk opportunities"; recruits are often told the luggage holds clothes, gold or cash (https://www.gov.uk/government/news/young-brits-recruited-as-drug-mules-as-overseas-drug-smuggling-arrests-quadruple-in-three-years ).
