# Fact-check: "The Pub Fight That Broke a Village" (H03)

**Setting:** an English village at harvest, 1351, three years after the Black Death.
**Accessed:** 2026-10-12.

## Method and limitations (read first)

* **Pages were not opened.** WebFetch failed (DNS errors), and the egress proxy refused direct requests (403). This
  included Fordham, Avalon, legislation.gov.uk, British History Online, The National Archives and Wikipedia.
* **Every source below was seen only as a search-result snippet.** No URL was invented: each one appeared in a
  search result.
* **Check before broadcast.** Compare anything shown on screen against the full text, especially quotations from
  statutes, which should be checked against *Statutes of the Realm*.
* **What is fiction.** The village, Jacko, Simmo, Maud, the coroner and this particular killing are fictional. The
  narration and dialogue use modern Australian slang on purpose (an editorial choice, labelled in the description).
  The map is not to scale.

Tags: **established** (documented) · **estimate** · **disputed** · **plausible** (reconstruction or weaker
attribution) · **illustrative** (fiction).

### Dramatised in the script (not claimed as fact)
* **"Lord over the hill's paying double":** the wage is fictional. The chronicler Knighton claimed that in 1349 reapers
  could not be hired for under 8d with food; that is a claim, not a measured wage, and is not used.
* **"No police":** a modern framing. Policing was communal: the hue and cry, the tithing and the constable.
* **Jacko could hang:** homicide was a felony, punishable by hanging if he was convicted. He avoids trial by
  abjuring.

## Not used (unconfirmed or wrong)
* **Death toll:** no single precise figure, and not "70%".
* **Stocks:** "three days in the stocks for demanding higher wages" is a loose paraphrase. In the statute, the stocks
  are for refusing or breaking the oath.
* **The F brand:** not shown as carried out, and the F is not "for fugitive".
* **Penalties:** the 40-day or quarter-year prison terms and the double or triple penalties.
* **Prosecution numbers:** for example, "7,556 fined".
* **Abjuration myths:**
  * "Barefoot, bareheaded, ungirt" as fact (weak sources only).
  * Wading into the sea every day.
  * Beheading if he strayed.
* **Ale:** prices for the 1350s, and "a gallon a day" for harvesters.
* **Neck verse:** Psalm 51 presented as the rule in 1351.
* **Hammer's homicide rate:** the per-100,000 figure for Oxford.
