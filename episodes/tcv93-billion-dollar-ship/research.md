## Corrections to the brief
- **Tonnage:** the brief said "about 20 tonnes (reported 17.5 t at first)". CBP's final figure is 39,525 lb, which is
  nearly 20 US (short) tons = about 17.9 METRIC tonnes; the first figure was about 16.5 US tons (33,000 lb), not 17.5 t.
  The reel avoids tonnes for this case: the counter shows 39,525 LB and the narration says "$1 billion".
- **Value:** CBP put the street value at about $1.3 billion; DOJ releases say "worth more than $1 billion" / "$1 billion
  worth". The narration says "$1 billion" (true on both).
- **How it was found:** no source in the extracts says exactly what tipped the inspection (FreightWaves infers a tip from
  the size of the boarding party; Bloomberg says the ship was tracked and agents acted on intelligence). The gotcha is
  therefore the documented catch: Coast Guard swabs found cocaine residue on the second mate's hands and arms (arrest
  affidavit, reported by the Inquirer and FreightWaves). The X-ray detail (anomalies in seven containers, X-ray next day)
  appears in a single extract and is NOT used.
- **The ship seizure:** CBP seized the vessel on 4 July 2019; it was later released on bond (gCaptain; the amount differs
  between its headline and body, so no amount is used). The callback says only "Then customs seized the ship itself." (true
  as an event).
- **Ecuador:** 22 tonnes is the army's count ("cerca de 22"); Reuters reports 21.5 tonnes at the destruction. The
  narration says "nearly 22 tonnes". The drugs were in underground stashes beneath a pig shed on a farm (Reuters: "storage
  space built under a pig farm"; Primicias, Vistazo: "bajo una chanchera"). One outlet says a drain in the pigsty was the
  entrance; it is single-sourced and NOT used. Primicias also called the farm a banana hacienda; the reel says "pig farm"
  only where Reuters does.
