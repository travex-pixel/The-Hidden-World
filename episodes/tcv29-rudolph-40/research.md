# Case File 29: Eric Rudolph, the Olympic Park bomber, and a grocery-store dumpster (40-second cut) — research ledger

Date: 2026-10-12. Episode id `tcv29-rudolph-40`, prefix `er29`. Closed case (guilty pleas April 2005; life without parole;
held at ADX Florence, Colorado). Framing: a five-year manhunt that ended when a rookie on a routine patrol found a man
digging through a grocery store's dumpster for food.

## Method and limits
- 10 WebSearch calls in "standard" mode. WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so only **search-result
  extracts** were read, never full pages. Every source record in `episode.json` says so in `accessed`.
- No fbi.gov page surfaced in search; FBI material is represented by the Justice Department (2003 Attorney General statement
  on the arrest; 1998 charging release; 2005 plea release), the ATF history page and an FBI 20th-anniversary account
  syndicated on Yahoo News. Contemporary press: AP (via Suffolk News-Herald, Selma Times-Journal, NBC News), CBS News, ABC
  News, CNN transcripts (31 May 2003; 21 March 2002), AccessWDUN, WSB-TV, Carolina Public Press, ABA Journal, History.com,
  Al Jazeera, JURIST, Atlanta News First. Wikipedia is listed only as a cross-check, never as a sole source.
- Narration uses only what at least two sources agree on, worded no more specifically than they are.

## Established facts (used in narration; claim ids in episode.json)
- **R_hid** — Rudolph, the Centennial Olympic Park bomber, was a fugitive from 1998 until 31 May 2003 (about five years),
  hiding in the mountains of western North Carolina (CBS 2003; DOJ 2003; AP; Wikipedia). Narration: "The Olympic Park
  bomber hid from the FBI for five years."
- **R_dumpster** — he was found early on 31 May 2003 behind a grocery store (a Save-A-Lot) in Murphy, N.C., going through the
  dumpster for food. Wording in the sources: AP "scavenging for food behind a grocery store"; WSB-TV "rummaging for food in a
  dumpster behind a grocery store"; ABA Journal "foraging in the dumpster"; FBI account via Yahoo "rummaging through a trash
  bin behind a rural grocery store" (headline: "until he dug in trash"); ABC/CBS "sifting through a garbage bin". Narration:
  "It ended at a grocery store dumpster." / "A man was digging through the dumpster for food." Headline: "A GROCERY STORE
  DUMPSTER / HELPED CATCH THE OLYMPIC BOMBER" ("helped catch", not "caught": the officer made the arrest; the dumpster is
  why he was there and why he was seen).
- **R_toll** (stakes) — the bomb in Centennial Olympic Park, Atlanta, early on 27 July 1996 killed one woman and injured more
  than 100 people (111 in most accounts); a second man died of a heart attack (History.com; Atlanta News First 2026;
  Wikipedia). Narration: "His bomb at the 1996 Olympics killed a woman and injured over 100." The victims are not named.
- **R_more** — three more bombings: an abortion clinic in Sandy Springs, Ga. (January 1997), a lesbian nightclub in Atlanta
  (February 1997), an abortion clinic in Birmingham, Ala. (January 1998; an off-duty police officer was killed and a nurse
  critically injured) (WSB-TV; DOJ 1998; DOJ 2005; History.com). Narration: "After three more bombings…"; the card says
  "TWO CLINICS · A NIGHTCLUB".
- **R_list** — added to the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list on 5 May 1998; a reward of up to $1 million (DOJ 2003; CBS 2003; DOJ
  1998; Wikipedia). Narration: "…he made the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list." Reward on the card only.
- **R_hunt** — more than 200 federal and state agents searched the Nantahala mountains in 1998 (CNN; AccessWDUN: "more than
  200 people searched at one time", called the largest manhunt before 9/11); helicopters with infrared scopes and logs rigged
  with motion detectors (AP 2003); later scaled back (CBS). Narration: "More than 200 agents searched the North Carolina
  mountains." / callback "Five years, hundreds of agents."
- **R_rookie** — the arresting officer, Jeff Postell, was a 21-year-old Murphy police rookie (on the force less than a year)
  on routine patrol; he drove behind the store and thought he had a burglar / someone breaking and entering or prowling
  (AP; CBS; Carolina Public Press; ABA Journal; Wikipedia). He is not named on screen (generic, unnamed officer). Narration:
  "Then a 21-year-old rookie on patrol drove behind a grocery store." / "Then one rookie."
- **R_name** — the man first gave a false name (AP: "Jerry Wilson"; one CNN transcript has "Gary Wilson", so the card says
  "Reported as"); officers at the jail recognised him and he admitted he was Eric Robert Rudolph; the FBI confirmed it by
  fingerprints (AP; FBI via Yahoo; CNN; DOJ 2003). Narration: "The man gave a fake name." / "At the jail, he was recognised:
  Eric Rudolph."
- **R_plea** — April 2005: guilty pleas to the Atlanta and Birmingham bombings under a deal that spared him the death penalty
  (he also revealed buried dynamite); sentenced 18 July 2005 (Birmingham, two life terms) and 22 August 2005 (Atlanta, four
  consecutive life terms plus 120 years); held at ADX Florence (History.com; NBC/AP; Al Jazeera; DOJ 2005; JURIST).
  Narration: "He pleaded guilty to avoid the death penalty." / "Life without parole." (The count of life terms differs by
  how the two sentencings are added up, so narration does not give a number.)

## Conflicts noted (not narrated)
- Time of the arrest: 3:27, 3:30, about 4 and 4:30 a.m. in different accounts. The card says "ABOUT 4 A.M. · Times vary by
  account".
- The store: "Save-A-Lot" in most accounts, "Valley Village shopping center"/"strip mall" in others. Not named on screen.
- The officer's surname appears once as "Postdale" (AP variant); Postell is the dominant spelling. He is not named anyway.
- Olympic Park toll: one killed directly, a second death by heart attack; injuries 111 (some say "over 100"). Narration says
  "a woman" and "over 100".

## Myths and claims NOT used
- That Rudolph was "caught by the dumpster" or that the FBI caught him: the arrest was by local police; the FBI confirmed
  identity.
- That he hid in a particular cave or camp at the arrest site, or speculation about local sympathisers who may have helped
  him: unproven, left out.
- His motives, statements and beliefs: never quoted or summarised.
- The milk crates he reportedly hid behind (Postell quote): drawn only as generic set dressing; not narrated.

## Ethics decisions
- No bomb, backpack, explosion, park, victim or injury is drawn anywhere. The toll is a typed card in a quiet task-force room.
- Victims (including the woman killed in the park and the officer killed in Birmingham) are not named in narration; the
  memorial "We remember his victims." is the last spoken line.
- Rudolph appears only as an anonymous rim-lit silhouette, tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS"; never his booking photo;
  never posed heroically.
- The arresting officer is a generic, unnamed figure (not a likeness); no badge, patch or force insignia.
- The security guard who found the device in 1996 and was wrongly investigated (then cleared by the FBI) is left out: one
  line would not do him justice at this pace, and he does not need to be named.
- Store, dumpster, patrol car, jail and mountains are generic, with no name, sign or logo; the jail's wanted-style notice is
  our own blank design (no seal, no face, no words).
- End question (burned in): "WAS IT JUST LUCK? / YES OR NO?" — a one-tap opinion question about the arrest, not about the
  victims.
