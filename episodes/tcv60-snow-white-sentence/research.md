# Case File 60: The Snow White sentence (tcv60-snow-white-sentence)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Legal status verified on 2026-10-17: Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63, was decided on 5 March 2003; the sentence was upheld. Andrade's later custody status is **unconfirmed** (see below) and is not claimed.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Where the tapes went:** the brief said "into a coat pocket". The opinion says that at the Montclair Kmart he put four videotapes **in the rear waistband of his pants**. The narration says "He hid them in his waistband"; frame 1 shows a tape sliding under a jacket hem into the waistband.
- **Which tape where:** "Snow White" was in the FIRST theft (five tapes, Kmart in Ontario, California, 4 November 1995; CSM, Balls and Strikes); "Free Willy 2" in the SECOND (four tapes, Kmart in Montclair, California, 18 November 1995; CSM). Both stores are in San Bernardino County (NPR; San Bernardino County District Attorney named in commentary).
- **Value:** $84.70 + $68.84 = **$153.54** (opinion); press "about $150" / "$153". Narration "About 150 dollars"; counter "$153".
- **Sentence:** two consecutive 25-years-to-life terms (50 years to life): confirmed (opinion; NPR; AP via Lawrence Journal-World; AFP via Mail & Guardian).
- **Vote and date:** 5-4, decided 5 March 2003 (argued 5 November 2002): confirmed. Majority by Justice O'Connor (with Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas); Justice Souter dissented, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.
- **Souter's wording:** extracts give "the rare sentence of demonstrable gross disproportionality" (Cornell LII dissent page, and a search summary of the same text). Only ONE independent source (the opinion itself), so **no direct quote is used**; the "critics" line paraphrases (the punishment did not fit the crime).
- **Later outcome (release / resentencing):** NOT confirmed. Wikipedia says he petitioned under Proposition 36 (2012) and "was released shortly after Prop 36's passage"; a commentary extract (date unclear) says he "remains in prison today, with no possibility of parole until he's eighty-seven". No AP/NYT/LA Times/NPR report of a release was returned by four targeted searches. **Not narrated.** The last line states the sentence as imposed ("first shot at parole at 87"), with an on-screen "AS SENTENCED" label.
- **Prior record:** "prior burglary convictions": confirmed (three counts of first-degree residential burglary, 1983; opinion; Wikipedia). Narrated as "old burglary convictions".

## Lesson (save card, for the viewer)
**W_lesson.** If you are arrested or questioned: (1) you have the right to remain silent: say clearly that you are using it; (2) ask for a lawyer (a public defender if you cannot afford one) and do not talk, sign or decide anything without one; (3) say clearly "I do not consent to a search", and do not physically resist if officers search anyway. Rows: SAY YOU WILL STAY SILENT / ASK FOR A LAWYER / DO NOT CONSENT TO SEARCHES.
