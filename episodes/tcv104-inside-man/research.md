# Case File 104: The Inside Man (ONE SMUGGLER, format v10)

Research ledger built 26 October 2026. Lane: customs (an airport insider). Episode brief: a customs, border or port official,
or an airport/port worker with access, CONVICTED of letting drugs through or smuggling them himself.

## Verdict, first

**Chosen case (verified, concluded, widely named): Junaed Dar, a Heathrow Airport security manager from Slough.** On
Saturday 14 December 2019 he arrived at Heathrow about three hours before his shift, in his security uniform, took an
official airport vehicle to Terminal 2B and met two couriers off a flight from Bogotá, Michael Williams and Jessica
Waldron of Dudley. He escorted them to the toilets, took possession of their holdalls and was arrested by waiting
National Crime Agency officers as he was about to drive away. The bags had held about 22 kg of cocaine, but Colombian
police, working with the NCA, had searched them at Bogotá airport before departure and **replaced the cocaine with blocks
of wood**. A jury at Kingston Crown Court found him guilty of attempting to import Class A drugs; he was sentenced to
**16 and a half years** on Monday 4 August 2025. The couriers had pleaded guilty and got six years and eight months each
(Isleworth Crown Court, October 2022); two organisers, Ruford Davis and David Farquharson, got 14 and a half years each.

Why it qualifies: one person; a concluded outcome (jury conviction and sentence); the "how he was caught" line is concrete
and carried by two independent sources (the NCA's 2025 statement and PA Media's 2022 court report: the wood swap, the
toilets); the half-reveal object is a holdall being unzipped with the inside hidden, and the inside is the twist (wood,
not cocaine); and the irony is the brief's: the man paid to keep contraband out met it at the gate.

## The story in order

1. **Who he was.** A security manager at Heathrow, from Slough (41 at arrest, 47 at sentence). Nothing else about him is
   reported, so the script says nothing else.
2. **The plot.** Two organisers (Davis, Farquharson) arranged couriers from Dudley to fly 22 kg of cocaine from Bogotá in
   holdalls, with an insider to meet them at Heathrow and take the bags past the checks. They used encrypted messages to
   tell one courier where to meet Dar (detail single-sourced; not narrated).
3. **The interception.** Colombian police, working with the NCA, searched the bags at Bogotá airport before departure,
   took the cocaine out, put blocks of wood in its place and let the bags fly.
4. **The day.** 14 Dec 2019: Dar came in about three hours before his shift, in uniform, took an airport vehicle to
   Terminal 2B, met the couriers, took the bags in the toilets and put them in his vehicle.
5. **The arrest.** NCA officers were waiting; he was arrested as he was about to drive off. Border Force arrested the
   couriers.
6. **The outcome.** Couriers: guilty pleas, 6 y 8 m each (Oct 2022). Organisers: 14.5 y each. Dar: convicted by a jury,
   16.5 years (Kingston Crown Court, 4 Aug 2025).

## The question (end card)

**"WHO DESERVES MORE TIME?" / "THE INSIDE MAN OR THE MULES?"** Grounded: he got 16.5 years after a trial; the couriers
got 6 y 8 m each after guilty pleas (a plea earns a discount; the judge also weighed his breach of trust, per the NCA's
"gross betrayal of trust"). Both sides are arguable without questioning anyone's guilt. Pinned vote: 1 = the inside man,
2 = the mules, 3 = the same.
