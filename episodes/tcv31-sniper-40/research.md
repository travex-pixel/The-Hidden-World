# Case File 31: the DC sniper attacks and a highway rest stop (40-second cut) — research ledger

Date: 2026-10-13. Episode id `tcv31-sniper-40`, prefix `ds31`. Closed case (Muhammad convicted 2003, executed 2009;
Malvo serving life; a Maryland resentencing ordered in 2022 is stalled; appeal refused August 2026). Framing: for weeks
police hunted a white box truck; the break came from the killers' own call to the tip line about an earlier crime in
Alabama, a fingerprint there, a car registration, and a truck driver who saw the car at a rest stop.

## Method and limits
- 12 WebSearch calls in "standard" mode. WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so only **search-result
  extracts** were read, never full pages. Every source record in `episode.json` says so in `accessed`. apnews.com,
  reuters.com and washingtonpost.com did not surface as reachable results, so AP reporting is cited where it is
  syndicated (Fox News, Indiana Daily Student, NBC News, ABC7) and the Post's reporting is not cited directly.
- Official material: the FBI's own case story "The Beltway Snipers, Pt. 2" (fbi.gov, 2007). Contemporary press: CNN
  transcripts and reports of 24 and 27 October 2002, Fox News/AP, Nextgov, CBS News, Truck News, Mail & Guardian. Later
  summaries: Britannica, CBC (archived), Travel Channel, NBC Washington (2022), Northern Virginia Magazine timeline
  (2019), WTOP (2026). Execution: ABC News, NBC News/AP, CBS News, ABC7. Malvo's legal status: CNN Fast Facts, NBC News
  (2017), NPR (2022), WUSA9 (2022), Maryland Matters and The Daily Record (August 2026).
- Narration uses only what at least two sources agree on, worded no more specifically than they are.

## Established facts (used in narration; claim ids in episode.json)
- **D_hook** — over about three weeks in October 2002 (2–22 October; the FBI: "23 days of random attacks") two snipers
  shot people at random in the Washington area; the attacks ended with the arrests at the rest stop on 24 October (FBI;
  Britannica; CBC; WTOP). Narration: "The DC snipers shot people at random for three weeks." / "Then a rest stop ended
  it." Headline: "A REST STOP / ENDED THE DC SNIPER ATTACKS" (the arrests at the rest stop ended the attacks; the trucker
  called 911 and police made the arrests).
- **D_toll** (stakes by 0:10) — ten people were killed and three wounded in Maryland, Virginia and DC (Travel Channel;
  CBC; Britannica "killed 10 people and injured 3"; NBC Washington). Narration: "Ten were killed and three wounded,
  across Maryland, Virginia and DC." Card: "10 KILLED · 3 WOUNDED".
- **D_truck** — the early lookout was for a white box truck with one or two suspects (DC police chief Charles Ramsey,
  CNN 27 Oct 2002); Britannica: conflicting reports of a white van, a white box truck and a dark Caprice; NBC Washington
  (2022). Narration: "For weeks, police hunted a white box truck." Card: "LOOKOUT: A WHITE BOX TRUCK".
- **D_call** (irony) — on 17 October 2002 a caller to the task-force tip line, claiming to be the sniper, told
  investigators to look into a shooting at a liquor store in Montgomery, Alabama (21 September 2002; one woman killed,
  one wounded; first treated as a robbery) (FBI: the break came "ironically, from the snipers themselves"; Fox/AP;
  IDS/AP; Mail & Guardian). Narration: "Then a tip-line caller claiming to be the sniper / pointed to a liquor store
  shooting in Montgomery, Alabama." No words of the call are quoted. The Alabama victims are not named.
- **D_print** — a fingerprint on a magazine dropped at the Montgomery scene went to the FBI Laboratory and was matched
  in the FBI's fingerprint database to Lee Boyd Malvo, 17, whose prints were on file from an immigration arrest in
  Washington State (FBI; Nextgov; Fox "How the cops cracked the case"; IDS/AP). Narration: "A fingerprint from that
  scene matched seventeen-year-old Lee Boyd Malvo." Card: "A FINGERPRINT · FBI DATABASE MATCH".
- **D_car** — Malvo's record led to John Allen Muhammad (an earlier tip had named him); on 22 October an FBI records
  search found a blue 1990 Chevrolet Caprice registered to Muhammad in New Jersey (FBI; CNN 24 Oct; NoVA timeline).
  Narration: "Malvo led to John Allen Muhammad, and a blue 1990 Chevrolet Caprice."
- **D_plate** — a lookout for the Caprice and its New Jersey plate went out on the night of Wednesday 23 October and
  was carried on radio and television (CNN: "put out a lookout ... Wednesday night"; NoVA timeline: BOLO 23 Oct; WTOP:
  the plate "leaked by law enforcement"; CBS: the trucker had heard the news conference on the radio). Narration:
  "Police broadcast the plate." The plate number is never shown or spoken.
- **D_rest** (the HOW) — at about 1 a.m. on 24 October a truck driver (Ron Lantz, not named on screen) saw the Caprice at
  a rest stop off Interstate 70 near Myersville, Maryland, and called 911; trucks were pulled across the exit; police
  found two men asleep in the car and arrested Muhammad and Malvo without incident, in custody at about 3:19–3:30 a.m.
  (CNN transcript, FBI's Gary Bald: a 911 call "at approximately 1:00 a.m.", "two males sleeping inside", "without
  incident"; WTOP; NoVA timeline; CBS; Fox/AP; Truck News; Officer.com; NBC/AP court report). Narration: "Hours later a
  trucker saw the car at a rest stop." / "Both men were asleep inside." Card: "MYERSVILLE, MARYLAND · ABOUT 1 A.M.".
- **D_trunk** (card only) — a hole had been cut in the car's body above the rear plate so a rifle could be fired from
  inside the trunk (WTOP 2026; NBC Washington 2022; CNN 24 Oct 2002). Card: "THE TRUNK HAD BEEN MODIFIED". No weapon
  is shown or named on screen.
- **D_muhammad** — Muhammad was convicted in Virginia in November 2003 of the murder of Dean Harold Meyers, sentenced to
  death in March 2004 and executed by lethal injection at Greensville Correctional Center on 10 November 2009 (ABC; NBC/AP;
  CBS; ABC7/AP). Narration: "Muhammad was executed in 2009."
- **D_malvo** — Malvo, 17 at the time, was sentenced to life without parole in Virginia (March 2004) and Maryland (2006).
  After the Supreme Court's juvenile-sentencing rulings a federal judge ordered a Virginia resentencing (2017) and
  Maryland's highest court ordered a Maryland resentencing (August 2022); that resentencing has stalled because Virginia
  will not transfer him, and on 25 August 2026 the Maryland Supreme Court refused to hear his appeal (CNN Fast Facts;
  NBC 2017; NPR 2022; WUSA9 2022; Maryland Matters and The Daily Record, August 2026). Narration: "Malvo was given
  life." Card: "MALVO, 17 · LIFE · RESENTENCING FIGHTS CONTINUE (2026)".
- **D_memorial** (card only) — "IN MEMORY OF THE TEN WHO DIED · Maryland · Virginia · Washington, DC · October 2002".
  Last spoken line: "We remember the ten who died."

## Legal status (verified 2026-10-13, extracts only)
- Muhammad: executed 10 November 2009. Malvo: serving life in Virginia (Red Onion State Prison); Virginia's 2020 law
  made him parole-eligible there; the Maryland resentencing ordered in 2022 has not been held; the Maryland Supreme
  Court declined his appeal on 25 August 2026. No later ruling found. Stated as "given life" plus the card.

## Conflicts noted (not narrated)
- Who spotted the car: most sources say a truck driver (Ron Lantz); an early CNN transcript credited "a motorist" and a
  rest-stop attendant, and the FBI's Bald said "a motorist called 911". Narration says "a trucker"; he is not named.
- Time of the 911 call: "about 1 a.m." (FBI), 12:47 / 12:54 a.m. (NoVA timeline), 1:54 a.m. (one trucker profile).
  Card says "about 1 a.m.".
- Time of custody: 3:19 a.m. (CNN) vs 3:30 a.m. (NoVA timeline). Not stated.
- The item with the fingerprint: "a magazine" (FBI), "a gun magazine" (Nextgov), a gun "catalogue" (Mail & Guardian);
  a CNN transcript noted it was unclear whether it was a paper magazine or a rifle magazine. Narration: "a fingerprint
  from that scene".
- The car's colour: blue (most sources) or "blue or burgundy" (CNN lookout). Narration says blue; the drawing is a
  generic dark blue sedan with a blank plate.
- Wider toll: including earlier shootings in other states, sources give 16 or 17 deaths; narration counts only the
  October 2002 DC-area attacks (10 killed, 3 wounded), as the FBI and Britannica do.
- Muhammad's age: 41 or 42. Not stated.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- Any wording of the snipers' calls, notes or demands (the "Call me God" note, the ransom demand): never quoted.
- That police stopped the Caprice on 3 October and let it go: true per Chief Ramsey but left out for length.
- That the trucker "caught" or "captured" the snipers: he saw the car and called 911; police made the arrests.
- The reward, the prayer-meeting story and the trucker's later life: left out.
- The rifle's make, the trunk's exact set-up and how shots were fired: not shown or described beyond "modified".
- The names of the Alabama victims, the DC-area victims and the truck driver: not on screen.

## Ethics decisions
- No shooting, weapon, victim, injury or crime scene is drawn anywhere. The toll is a typed card at a briefing; the
  Alabama beat is a storefront exterior at dusk with an evidence marker and an officer with a torch.
- Victims are not named; the memorial card says "the ten who died", and "We remember the ten who died." is the last
  spoken line.
- Muhammad and Malvo are only anonymous rim-lit silhouettes in court (tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS") and dark
  shapes inside the car; never a photo recreation; never posed heroically; nothing they said is quoted.
- The truck driver is a generic, unnamed figure (not a likeness); the officers are generic, with no insignia and no
  weapons. The car is a generic dark blue 1990s sedan with a blank plate; the rest stop, road, store, offices and court
  are generic drawings with no real name, number, sign or logo; every card is our own typed graphic. No dialogue is
  invented.
