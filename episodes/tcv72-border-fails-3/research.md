## Myths and claims NOT used
- COW SHOES (the brief's item 1): every source found is Prohibition-era (a 1922 Florida report and a 1924 Treasury photo of
  wooden "cow shoes" seized from moonshiners); no modern border seizure could be verified, so it was replaced by the fake carrots.
- "8 inches in diameter" (one outlet's description of the opening) vs AP's "large enough for people to walk through": conflicting,
  so neither is narrated; the drawing shows a modest floor opening.
- Total drug weight (168 kg / "near 400 lb" / "261 lb") and street value ($1M / $1.2M / $1.36M): outlets differ, not used.
- The owner's purchase of the building (April 2018, $390,000) and a cartel link (one outlet, attributed to HSI): not used.
- The "Border Patron" misspelled clone truck (CBS only): one source, not used.
- The narco-drone's destination (the U.S.?) and the "test run" theory: speculation, not used.

## Ethics decisions
- The owner is a real, charged (not convicted, outcome unreported) person: not named, drawn only as a dark silhouette tagged
  NOT A LIKENESS. Agents, officers and the bystander are generic figures with no badge, patch, flag or agency name. No weapon
  is drawn or pointed; agents and officers hold only flashlights.
- KFC is named as plain text only ("a KFC", "an old KFC"): no logo, no mascot, no Colonel, no bucket or brand artwork; the
  building is a generic closed restaurant with plain red-and-cream roof panels and a blank sign. Nothing implies the company
  had any part in the crime (the building was a former franchise site, vacant at the time).
- Drugs appear only as plain taped packets; marijuana as plain carrot-shaped orange packages.
