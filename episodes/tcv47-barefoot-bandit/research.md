# Case File 47: The Barefoot Bandit (tcv47-barefoot-bandit)

Research ledger, 2026-10-15 (all accesses on this date).

## Method and limits
- 15 standard web searches. WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment, so every source below is cited from **search-result extracts only**. No full page was read, including the court records, the DOJ press release and the Council on Criminal Justice PDF.
- Where the extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording that all of them support. Each disagreement is recorded below.
- No paid APIs were used. Nature column:
  - **fact**: reported consistently by independent outlets or an official record.
  - **reported**: attributed in the narration ("Reportedly").
  - **alleged**: not used.
- **Subject status:** Colton Harris-Moore is **alive**. He pleaded guilty, served his sentence in custody, moved to work release in 2016 and was later released from it under supervision. The episode says nothing about his life now. Undated pages that claim to describe his life "in 2023" look like templated content with no named source, so they were not used.

## Corrections to the brief
- **"Stole planes at 18" is corrected to 17.** The first plane he is believed to have stolen was taken in November 2008 from Orcas Island Airport (AOPA). With a birth date of 22 March 1991 (Wikipedia) he was 17 then. This is consistent with AVweb calling him 18 in October 2009 and AP calling him 19 in July 2010.
- **"100+ crimes" is not used.** Totals vary by outlet: about 70 crimes in nine states and British Columbia (Fox/AP); more than 80 in nine states; "over 100" in eight states and a province (FindLaw). No count is stated in the episode.
- **The number of states is not used.** Outlets give eight, nine or "at least five", so there is no STATES counter. The chip counts AGE instead.
- **The plane count is "at least five"** (CBS: "five private planes, crash-landing three of them"; AOPA: "at least five"). The narration says "at least five planes. Three crashed."
- **Cars are not mentioned.** No extract that I could see documented the car thefts separately.
- **The capture place is given as "off Eleuthera".** Reports name Harbour Island, northern Eleuthera, or "off Eleuthera". The narration names no island for the capture.

## Lesson (checklist card with SAVE ribbon, captions off)
- **Spoken cue:** "Plane, boat or cabin? Save these three."
- **Rows:** "NEVER HIDE A SPARE KEY" / "ADD A SECOND LOCK" / "REPORT ANYONE LURKING".
- **Sources for the rows:**
  - AOPA, "Lock 'em up" (2008): https://www.aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2008/june/pilot/answers-for-pilots-lock-em-up . Do not leave keys inside or hidden around the aircraft or hangar; use an auxiliary lock (throttle, prop or prop-control lock).
  - AOPA Airport Watch: https://aopa.org/advocacy/airports-and-airspace/security-and-borders/aopas-airport-watch . "Lock up, look out", developed with TSA; report suspicious activity.
  - Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department tips (Beverly Press, 2012): https://beverlypress.com/2012/07/lasd-offers-tips-to-ward-off-burglars/ . Give a spare key to a trusted neighbour rather than hiding it outside; use dead-bolt locks.
  - Ramsey County Sheriff's Office, Burglary Prevention: https://www.ramseycounty.us/sites/default/files/Sheriff/CPT-Burglary%20Prevention%202017.pdf . Call 911 about people lurking who do not belong.

## Ethics decisions
- He is alive and served his sentence. He appears only as a faceless rim-lit silhouette tagged "ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS". The sentence and the 2016 release are stated plainly, with no speculation about his life now.
- His difficult childhood is not used for drama or mocked. The tone is remarkable and ironic, and the episode does not glamorise theft: the lesson card is about protecting property, and the statistic shows how far teen burglary arrests have fallen to the adult level.
- No weapon is drawn and no one is hurt on screen. The crashed plane is empty. The chase is shown as lights on the water, and his boat's motor is not shown being shot.
- Shop owner, deputies, Bahamian officers and the plane owner are generic figures. Bahamian officers are drawn as local people in plain uniforms, with no crest or flag. Aircraft carry no registration or logo, and the shop has no name.
- The end question is an opinion about the person ("GENIUS OR CRIMINAL?"), not about guilt, which is settled. The SEND line is "SEND TO THE FLIGHT-SIM MATE".
