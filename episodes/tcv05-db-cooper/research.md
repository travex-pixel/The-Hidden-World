# Research ledger: D.B. Cooper, the hijacking of Northwest Orient Flight 305 (Case File 05)

**Research date: 2026-10-11.** The case is unsolved. No one has been charged or identified. This episode names no suspect and does not suggest that anyone is Cooper.

## Method and limits
- **Starting point.** The existing dossier `series/true-crime/research_other_candidates/dbcooper.md` (2026-10-11), which carries the FBI-sourced timeline, plus 11 new web searches (standard mode) for this episode.
- **Search extracts only.** WebFetch and curl are blocked in this environment. Every claim rests on search-result extracts of the URLs listed, not on full-page reads. Each source record in `episode.json` says so in its `accessed` field.
- **Source ranking.**
  1. FBI pages: the Famous Cases page, the 2016 Seattle press release and the 2009 archived story.
  2. Established news and reference outlets: CBS News, NPR, The Seattle Times, the Los Angeles Times, Columbia Journalism Review, HistoryLink.org (the Washington State history encyclopedia) and KIRO 7.
  3. Specialist outlets: Coin World, Simple Flying, HISTORY (A&E) and AeroCorner.
  4. Wikipedia, used only as a pointer.
- **Search budget.** Searches were limited (a shared limit of 12). One domain-restricted query was refused because two of the requested domains are blocked, then re-run without them.
- **Not re-opened.** The FBI Famous Cases wording was not re-opened verbatim (no page fetch). The script therefore paraphrases it and quotes nothing.

Wording choices:
- "Stopped actively investigating", not "closed". The FBI says it "redirected resources", and some headlines that say "closed" overstate it.
- The narration says the boy "uncovered" the bundles. The on-screen label "clearing sand for a campfire" follows HistoryLink's "smoothing sand for a campfire". The loop line "a kid went digging" reflects the common description of digging a campfire pit (dossier, FBI 2009).
- "He lowered the rear stairs": the FBI says he jumped from the back of the plane. HistoryLink and the Wikipedia "Cooper vane" extract (pointer) say he deployed the aft airstair.
- "Rotting" bills follows "deteriorating" (HistoryLink, KIRO 7) and "decaying" (dossier, FBI).
- "Thanksgiving eve" is a calendar fact (Thursday 25 Nov 1971).

## Unverified or popular myths NOT used
- "The only unsolved hijacking in US commercial aviation history." It is widely repeated (one 2011 wire extract says it) but was not confirmed by a second reputable source this session. Not used.
- **The FBI's view that he probably did not survive.** It is attributed to the 2007 FBI story and to individual officials, and one extract frames it as one official's belief. It was dropped from the script to avoid presenting an assessment as fact.
- **The ticket price.** $18.52 (CJR) and $20 (Wikipedia) conflict. Not used.
- **The bourbon and soda, the cigarettes, the clip-on tie, the "wires and red sticks" glimpse, and the attendant pocketing the note as a phone number.** These details are true or plausible, but were not re-verified in two sources within the budget. Not narrated; the briefcase on screen is closed.
- **Weather.** The FBI cites "inclement weather" that night (CBS 2011 extract). Rain appears only as atmosphere in the illustration and is never narrated.
- **The landing in Reno** and its time (10:15 pm vs 11:02 pm). Not narrated.
- **Suspect counts** ("over 800" or "over 1,000"). Not used.
- **Named suspects:** Richard McCoy, Kenneth Christiansen, Duane Weber, Lynn Doyle Cooper, Robert Rackstraw and others. Not named, shown or hinted at. Neither are the 2017 tie-particle theories nor the 2022–2025 McCoy parachute claims (the FBI has published no conclusion).
- **The 1978 airstair placard.** Not used. This is why the script says the manhunt "found no trace of him", not "nothing was found".
- **How the money got to Tena Bar** (river flow, dredging or burial). Not used; the episode says "no one knows for sure".
- **"America's most wanted man" (Bulger).** He was on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list (FBI press release title: "Most Wanted Fugitive"). Whether he ranked number one at the time was not verified, so the tease says "a most-wanted fugitive".
- **"A stray cat gave him away."** Softened to "**helped** give him away". The tipster knew the couple through a stray cat Catherine Greig fed; the publicity campaign produced the tip.

## Fun facts with sources
1. **"D.B." was never his name.** He bought the ticket as "Dan Cooper"; "D.B." came from a media mix-up.
   - FBI Famous Cases page: a media mistake, "a myth created by the press".
   - Los Angeles Times, 28 Jul 2016: former reporter James Long, "I got the name wrong".
   - Columbia Journalism Review (2016): traces it to wire reporting by UPI's Clyde Jabin, and says the exact cause will never be known.

   Because the sources disagree on *which* reporter, the narration says "a media mix-up", not "a reporter". Claim **K_name**. Sparkle sound effect on "Fun".
2. **The "Cooper vane".** After Cooper's jump and copycat hijackings in 1972, the FAA required Boeing 727s to carry a spring-loaded aerodynamic paddle. Airflow in flight swings it to block the rear airstair, so the stairs cannot be lowered. It is named after the hijacker.
   - Simple Flying ("The Problem With The Boeing 727's Rear Door"): the FAA mandated the component in 1972; it was named after the hijacker.
   - HISTORY (A&E) ("Who Was the Mysterious Hijacker D.B. Cooper?"): returned by a domain-restricted search for the Cooper vane.
   - Wikipedia "Cooper vane" (pointer; cites Federal Register Vol. 37 No. 231, 30 Nov 1972).
   - AeroCorner: rule signed a year after the jump; airlines had until 28 Aug 1973.

   Sources differ on the exact dates, which the narration does not use. Claim **K_vane**. Sparkle sound effect on "Fun".
   **Residual risk:** the HISTORY attribution comes from a merged search extract, not a full read. Simple Flying is the clearest single extract.

## Ethics decisions
- **Cooper** is an illustrated representation, **not a likeness**. The figure `characters/figures/db5_cooper.json` uses only the widely published generic description: dark business suit, white shirt, black tie, dark sunglasses, short dark hair. It is never based on the FBI sketches, a suspect or a photograph. Every shot he appears in is tagged `ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS`; all other shots are tagged `ILLUSTRATION · NOT A PHOTOGRAPH`.
- **No suspect is named, shown or implied.** The end card says only "no one knows who he was".
- **The boy who found the money** (a living person) is a small anonymous silhouette at a distance. He is not named on screen or in the narration. `db5_boy` has `real_person: true`.
- **Flight attendants, agents, the reporter and the investigators are generic.** The flight attendants on Flight 305 were real people; the generic attendant (`db5_attendant`) wears an invented uniform and is not any of them.
- **No imitation of real material.**
  - No real FBI document, seal or logo.
  - No airline livery or name on the plane.
  - No ticket replica: the name card is a typed label.
  - No real newspaper.
  - The bank notes are plain green rectangles, with no portrait, seal or real serial numbers; serials are shown as illegible bars.
  - The 727 is a generic illustrated airliner tail with a T-tail and rear stairs.
- **The bomb claim** is presented as what the note said. No bomb, wires or explosives are shown; the briefcase stays closed.
- **The jump** shows the figure dropping into darkness. No parachute is shown opening, because whether it did is unknown.
- **The 2016 status** is worded as the FBI states it ("stopped actively investigating"), not "closed".
- **No dialogue or quotes are invented.** Only the narrator speaks.
