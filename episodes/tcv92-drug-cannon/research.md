## Method and limits
- WebSearch result extracts only (13 searches); WebFetch and curl are blocked in the build environment, so no article page was read in full. Every narrated claim below rests on at least two independent outlets' extracts, except where marked; a single-source detail is used only on a label with its source named, or not at all.
- The 2017 Agua Prieta coverage traces back to one release from Mexico's Attorney General's Office (PGR, 15 November 2017), reported independently by NPR (with El Universal), the Tucson Sentinel, the Arizona Daily Star and KTAR. The 2016 coverage traces back to the Mexican Federal Police report (Quadratin) and AP (ABC15, KRWG, 10News), with KGUN, the Arizona Daily Star and Fox News.
- Nobody was reported arrested for either Agua Prieta van or for the 2012 San Luis cannon, so nobody in those cases is identified, named or shown as arrested. The 2008 ultralight pilot pleaded guilty and was sentenced (Arizona Daily Star; AP): convicted, outcome stated on a label, drawn as a silhouette tagged NOT A LIKENESS and never named.

## Corrections to the brief
- **"February 2017, Agua Prieta: federal police found a van fitted with a compressed-air cannon."** No February 2017 cannon was found. February 2017 in Douglas was the catapult on top of the fence (already used, not in this reel). There are two Agua Prieta cannon vans: **September 2016** (Mexican Federal Police found a van reported stolen in Hermosillo parked on a street, a roughly 3 m / 10 ft launch tube through a hole cut in its roof, an air compressor, a gasoline engine and an air tank) and **November 2017** (the military and federal police seized a van whose roof could be raised and lowered to fire an air cannon from inside, with a compressor, a gasoline engine, an air tank and about 1,800 lb of marijuana). The reel's headline is the **November 2017** van (bigger seizure, the "roof that opens" picture); the 2016 van is the callback ("the town's second cannon van").
- **"December 2013, near Yuma: Mexican police seized a cannon/air-compressor launcher on a trailer and 33 cans of marijuana (about 85 lb)."** The 33 cans (about 85 lb) are **December 2012**, near **San Luis, Arizona** (Yuma Sector), and it was **US Border Patrol** that found them, in a field, with the CO2 tank by the fence; the cannon itself was not recovered and nobody was arrested. No 2013 trailer-mounted cannon seized near Yuma was found (a Mexicali cannon of February 2013, made from a plastic pipe and an old car engine, is a different case; not used).
- **How the 2017 van was found:** the extracts do not say (no stop, no tip-off, no arrest is described). The headline gotcha is therefore the seizure and its haul as reported ("Soldiers seized it, with 1,800 pounds of weed.").
- **Ultralight case chosen:** Marana, Arizona, October 2008 (a human caught, two outlets plus CBP's own release). Rejected: the 2008–2011 crashes (one pilot killed, one paralysed: nobody is hurt in this series); the October 2019 Kino Springs ultralight (pilot found hiding in a wash, but no drugs mentioned in the extracts, held for immigration violations: out of lane); the undated meth/fentanyl ultralight (pilot escaped, no arrest).

## Statistic and fun fact
- Statistic (counter): about **1,800 lb** of marijuana seized with the 2017 van (PGR via NPR, Tucson Sentinel, Arizona Daily Star).
- Fun fact (SUCCESs): **in the US you need no pilot's licence to fly an ultralight** (FAA Part 103; AOPA; Pilot Institute). Simple, unexpected, concrete, and it explains why smugglers chose them.

## Ethics decisions
- The headline is a seizure (a settled fact), so the first spoken words state it agent-first ("Smugglers built…"); the people come later, and only the convicted pilot is shown as caught, as a silhouette, never named.
- The van operator in frames 1 and last is an illustrated reconstruction of how the device was meant to work: an anonymous silhouette, tag ILLUSTRATION · GENERIC FIGURES; no one is identified.
- Nobody is hurt in any case used (ultralight crashes with a death or an injury were rejected).
- Soldiers, police, agents and crew are generic figures with no insignia and no weapons; vehicles and aircraft carry no logos, liveries, plates or registrations; places are generic illustrations.
