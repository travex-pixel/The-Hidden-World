# Case File 95 — Cocaine Bricks With Haaland's Face (CARTEL BRANDS · 1)

Research ledger, built 23 October 2026. Engine v8 (making-verb hook), lane: bust. New series in the proven lane:
cocaine seizures whose bricks carried a famous face or a brand. Every case is a seizure by a named police force, so each
is a settled fact (the drugs exist and were found). Nobody accused is named or drawn as a likeness.

## Method and limits

- Web search (standard mode), 12 queries, 23 Oct 2026. Full pages could not be opened from the build environment
  (WebFetch returned DNS errors for abcnews.com and lanacion.com.ar), so every claim below rests on **search-result
  extracts**, cross-checked between at least two outlets. Access date for every URL: 2026-10-23.
- The Haaland story is overwhelmingly one Associated Press wire story republished by many outlets (ABC News, La Nación,
  Santa Maria Times, Lethbridge Herald, Gray stations). It is counted once; each claim below also has a non-AP outlet
  (Fox News/OutKick, LBC, IBTimes, Daily Sabah, 1News, Earlygame, zamin.uz, Blueprint, Ynet).
- Breitbart (named in the brief) did not surface in any search; it is not cited.

## Corrections to the brief and claims NOT used

- **Weight:** 469 kg (AP and three others) against 369 kg (LBC, MyJoyOnline; probably a typo). The narration says only
  "over 1,000 pounds" (AP, OutKick); the counter shows the AP 469 kg.
- **Detainee:** AP says the driver; LBC names a Colombian woman (initials only). The reel says "1 arrested · not named".
- **"Stamped":** the reports say stickers, not stamps. The hook uses "branded" (= marked), which fits stickers, and the
  callout says STICKERS. The YouTube and Godfather bricks were stamped (VICE; AFP), so "stamp" appears only there.
- **Package count:** "370 packages" appears only in the AP text; not used.
- **Value** ($842,000 in Ecuador / $11 million in the US / $19.6 million in Europe, AP and Ynet): not used; Ynet's
  headline and body disagree.
- **The Godfather case:** a May 2018 Portuguese report (Sábado, citing Colombian prosecutors) gives 2.3 t and 11
  detained; the reel follows AFP and AP (about 2.5 t, ten arrested). The number of vehicles (one car per AP; "four
  vehicles" in an AFP extract) is not narrated.
- **The YouTube case:** VICE gives $7 million (dosed on the Uruguayan market), La Patilla over $3.5 million; not
  used. La Patilla reports the 20-year-old was later sentenced (16 years); not narrated, nobody is named.
- Candidates considered and not used: Tesla/Prada-logo bricks (Mexico 2022), Rolex-logo brick (Ohio traffic stop),
  the swastika bricks (Peru 2023, not a fit for the tone), Vacheron Constantin bricks (Spain, Sept 2026; used only as a
  source for the "label" explanation).

## Ethics decisions

- Erling Haaland is named because the stickers carried his face and it is the search term; the reel states twice (a
  label from the first seconds of the gotcha shot, and the closing line) that he has no part in it. He is never drawn:
  the sticker is a plain green rectangle with a generic footballer silhouette and a "9", no likeness, club badge or kit.
- Messi appears only as typed words. Marlon Brando is not drawn. The Godfather and YouTube appear only as plain typed
  words on the bricks, never as logo artwork; no Rolls-Royce or Mini marks are drawn.
- Nobody accused is named. The Ecuador arrest, the ten in Spain/Portugal and the three in Uruguay are typed labels; the
  man by the pickup in Uruguay is an anonymous silhouette, an illustrated reconstruction of the pickup in the reports.
- Officers are generic figures in plain work clothes with no badge, patch, flag, agency name or insignia; they hold
  torches only; no weapon is shown or mentioned; no force is shown.
- Vehicles and the plane carry no logo, livery, plate or registration. Places are illustrations, not replicas.
- Every card is our own typed graphic; no police video, photograph or document is imitated.
