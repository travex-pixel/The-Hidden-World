# Case File 13: the Hatton Garden safe deposit burglary (London, Easter 2015). Research ledger

Episode: `tcv13-hatton-garden` · "Prosecutors Called It England's Largest Burglary. The Ringleader Was 76."
Ledger date: **2026-10-11** (`legal_status_verified` = 2026-10-11).
Base dossier: `series/true-crime/research_next_slate/heists_and_mysteries.md`, section 3 (verified 2026-10-11; sources [HG1]-[HG31]).

## Method and limits

- 15 web searches on 2026-10-11 ("standard" mode), on top of the dossier. WebFetch and curl are blocked from the build
  environment, so every source is a **search-result extract** (title, URL, summary text). No full article, judgment or
  court record was read. Dates come from the URL or the extract; otherwise "n.d." in `episode.json`.
- Wikipedia, BuzzFeed-style explainers and jeweller blogs were used only as pointers or corroboration, never as one of
  the two required sources on their own (BuzzFeed News is paired with a newspaper in every claim it supports).
- No quote, date, figure or source was invented. The only quoted phrases on screen are the prosecution's "largest
  burglary in English legal history" (Fox News/AP, VOA, ITV) and the nickname 'Basil'.
- Legal status (2026-10-11): all convictions are final. Four ringleaders pleaded guilty (2015); Wood, Lincoln and Doyle
  were convicted at trial on 14 Jan 2016 (one defendant acquitted); sentences March 2016; Michael Seed ("Basil")
  convicted March 2019. Confiscation orders followed (2018, 2020). Brian Reader died September 2023 (reported April 2024);
  Terry Perkins died in 2018. Nothing found that changes the episode.

## Attributed claims (prosecution case, as reported at trial)

- **HG_buspass:** prosecutors said Reader travelled to Hatton Garden by bus on a free older person's travel pass (a
  Freedom Pass). Fox News (AP) says the pass was in another man's name (T. McCarthy); ITV calls it "his Oyster Freedom
  Pass"; the Camden New Journal (book report) says "a borrowed Freedom Pass". Because the sources disagree on whose pass
  it was, the narration says only "a pensioner's free bus pass", attributed to prosecutors.
- **HG_quit:** prosecutors said Reader quit after the first night and did not come back (ITV 2015-11-24, "'Mastermind'...
  'quit on first night'"; NPR via WGBH: Reader "backed out after the gang ran into difficulties").

## Fun facts (each with two or more reputable sources)

1. **FUN FACT 1 (HG_buspass): the 76-year-old ringleader took the bus to the job on a pensioner's free pass, prosecutors
   said.** Fox News (AP) "Prosecutors: London jewel theft suspect used free senior citizens bus pass to get to heist";
   ITV "Dad's Army masterminds" (used an Oyster Freedom Pass to travel by public transport from Dartford); Camden New
   Journal (two buses, borrowed Freedom Pass). The Malay Mail wire report (dossier [HG7]) adds that his Oyster card
   showed his journeys. On screen: "Prosecutors said the 76-year-old ringleader took the bus to the job, on a free
   pensioner's travel pass."
2. **FUN FACT 2 (HG_basil): "Basil", the man who got them inside, stayed free for about three years.** Seed evaded capture
   until police raided his Islington flat in March 2018; he was convicted in March 2019 and jailed for 10 years. Police
   said he was responsible for accessing the premises and disabling security. Sources: Fox News 2019; Westminster
   Extra; The Daily Telegraph 2019-03-16 ("Unmasked, Basil the mystery Hatton Garden raider"); QEB Hollis Whiteman.
   (Older "Basil is still unknown" lines are outdated and were not used.)
3. **Closing beat, not styled as a fun fact (HG_unrecovered): much of the loot was never recovered.** Kent Online (only
   about £4m recovered); BuzzFeed News trial report (about £9m "still unaccounted for"); Police Professional and Benchpeg
   (2018: a large amount of cash, loose stones and bullion still outstanding); NPR 2015-05-19 per the dossier (£4.3m
   recovered). The current total is not verified, so the narration says "much of the loot was never recovered" with no
   figure. Shown as a sober card rather than a FUN FACT pop-up, because these were people's savings and stock.

## Ethics decisions

- **Real people.** Reader is named once (closed case, convicted, deceased) and appears as an illustrated generic older man
  (`hg13_reader`, `real_person: true`), tagged 'ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIKENESS'. The other gang members appear as generic
  older men in hi-vis or plain clothes (`hg13_crew_a`, `hg13_crew_b`, `hg13_driver`, all `real_person: true` with "not a
  likeness" depictions) and are never named on screen. "Basil" is an anonymous silhouette (`hg13_basil`). No one is
  caricatured; nobody is shown laughing at victims.
- **The acquitted defendant** is never named, shown or alluded to beyond the count of convictions.
- **Non-violent, not glamorised.** The break-in is shown as a plain reconstruction; the episode states the losses ("much
  of the loot was never recovered") without a fun-fact flourish, and ends on the convictions and the ringleader's death.
- **No real branding or documents.** The jewellers' fronts carry generic words only; the bus, van and Mercedes carry no
  logos or badges; the CCTV grid, call log and court cards are the series' own typed graphics; no real CCTV still is
  imitated. The pub is a generic London pub, not a replica of the Castle.
- **Pronunciation.** "CCTV" is spoken as "C C T V"; the haul is written as words ("14 million pounds") so Piper cannot
  read a currency symbol. Years are spoken by the engine's year rule.
