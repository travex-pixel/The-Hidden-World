# THE QUIET GAME #1 — The Rumour: research and fact check

**Status of the video's content:** fiction. Nothing in the episode claims to be a real event, and the video makes no statistical or scientific claims on screen. The only "lesson" is the closing line, *"Nobody lied. Everyone just filled in the gaps."*, which describes the story, not a study.

## What the story dramatises, and how strong the evidence is

| Story beat | Idea | Strength | Source(s) actually consulted |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Careful with the new guy" → "something happened at his last job" → "he got fired for stealing" | Retold information loses detail and is reshaped toward listeners' expectations (levelling / sharpening / assimilation). | **Plausible tendency.** Classic lab finding from serial-reproduction studies (Allport & Postman, 1947). Critics note the lab chain differs from real rumour spreading; Caplow (1947) reported military rumours that became *more* accurate as they spread. Do not present it as a law. | S1, S2 (secondary summaries; the 1947 book itself was not consulted) |
| Pen taken from a shared pot, wallet opened to find a name → read as theft | Ambiguous behaviour is interpreted to fit an existing belief (confirmation bias). | **Established**, broad review literature. | S3: Nickerson (1998), DOI 10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175 (DOI confirmed via search results; doi.org was not reachable from the build environment) |
| Theo's frustration at being shunned is taken as proof | Expectations can draw out the behaviour they predict (behavioural confirmation / self-fulfilling prophecy). | **Established** as a lab phenomenon (Snyder, Tanke & Berscheid, 1977, telephone-conversation study). The story's office version is an illustration, not a replication. | S4: catalogue record confirmed via search. Citation details: JPSP 35(9); page range shown as 655–666 or 656–666 depending on index. Not cited by DOI because the DOI could not be verified from this environment. |

## Deliberately avoided
- No diagnosing characters (nobody is called a narcissist, manipulator, gaslighter, etc.). Rosa made an honest mistake; Ben and Kemi guessed. That is the point.
- No invented statistics ("70% of rumours…"), quotes or study names on screen.
- No claim that the story "proves" anything.

## Suggested video description
> By lunch, everyone hated him. Nobody had actually spoken to him.
> A fictional story about how a vague comment becomes "everyone knows".
> Ideas behind it: rumour transmission (Allport & Postman, 1947, with later criticism), confirmation bias (Nickerson, 1998), behavioural confirmation (Snyder, Tanke & Berscheid, 1977).
