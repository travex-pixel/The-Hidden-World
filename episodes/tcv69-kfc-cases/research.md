# Case File 69: KFC's strangest cases (tcv69-kfc-cases)

Research ledger, 2026-10-17 (all accesses on this date). Three closed, historical stories; nobody in them was charged with
a crime, so there are no allegations against a person. KFC is the subject of every story; only facts reported by the
sources are stated about the company, and KFC's own statements are attributed to KFC.

## Method and limits
- 15 standard web searches (the full budget). WebFetch was not used; every source below is cited from **search-result
  extracts only**; no full page was read.
- Independent newsrooms per item: shortage = AP, Reuters, AFP (Arab News), PA, Fortune, CNBC, NPR, The Week, East London
  Lines; fried rat = ABC (ABC7 Los Angeles), Fox (FOX 2, FOX6), CNN wire (WTVR), theGrio, Sinclair (KATU);
  recipe = AP (via NBC News), Nation's Restaurant News, KFC's own release (Franchising.com), SecurityInfoWatch.
- Where extracts disagree, the narration uses the narrowest wording all of them support and the detail goes on a card.

## Corrections and checks against the brief
- **Closure count: DISPUTED, so not spoken as a number.** Counts quoted at different times in February 2018: 562 closed
  (Fortune, Monday snapshot), "more than 600" (PA), "more than 700" (KFC via AFP/Arab News); AP: "most of its 900
  outlets"; Reuters: "the majority of its 900 outlets". The counter shows the verified base (900 UK restaurants) and the
  narration says "Most of its 900 UK restaurants shut."
- **"People called the police": verified.** Tower Hamlets police (Met, London) tweeted on 20 Feb 2018 asking people to
  stop contacting them; the Met called them nuisance calls (PA, The Week, East London Lines, Business Insider, NPR);
  Greater Manchester Police released audio of a man's 999 call about a closed branch (Newshub, Express Tribune, The Week).
  The brief also mentioned "several UK police forces": two are verified (Met/Tower Hamlets, Greater Manchester).
- **The fried rat:** the brief's facts check out (June 2015, California, KFC said an independent lab's DNA test showed
  chicken). The customer is a private person: he is **not named** on screen or in narration, and is drawn only as a
  silhouette. KFC's result is attributed ("KFC said"); his own account (a manager confirmed it was a rat, which KFC
  disputed) is noted on a card.
- **The recipe move (2008):** verified. The courier (Bo Dietl) is a FORMER New York City police detective hired as a
  security consultant, so the narration says "a former cop"; he is not named. The escort is reported differently (KFC's
  release: Louisville Metro Police and Brink's; AP: off-duty police officers and private guards), so the narration says
  only "in an armored car".
- **Not used:** the South African "fake KFC food inspector who ate free for a year" (KFC South Africa called the viral
  student-arrest story fake; its 2022 "private investigator" hunt was an Ogilvy advertising campaign, run as sponsored
  content); the 2009 Oprah free-coupon rush (verified, but no police element found); the 2016 Chicago Tribune "possible
  copy of the recipe" story (not needed for three items; not re-verified).

## Fun fact (SUCCESs)
KFC's apology for running out of chicken was a full-page ad of its own empty bucket with the letters rearranged to "FCK"
(CNBC, Marketing Week, Newshub, Fortune). Simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, funny: a pub fact. Shown as plain text
only; the ad itself is not reproduced.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "Fake food inspector ate free KFC for a year / two years" (South Africa): KFC South Africa called the viral arrest
  story fake (Complex; Norfolk and Tillsonburg News); the 2022 investigation was an Ogilvy campaign (Ogilvy case page;
  TimesLive and News24 labelled it paid/sponsored).
- Police at the 2009 Oprah coupon rush: not found in sources.
- "Several police forces": only two are verified, and the narration says only "people called the police".

## Ethics decisions
- No logos, mascot or Colonel likeness; the bucket is a generic red-and-white striped tub with no lettering; the "FCK" ad
  is described in text only.
- The fried-rat customer and the former detective are real people: unnamed, dark silhouettes tagged NOT A LIKENESS.
- KFC's lab result is attributed to KFC; nothing implies wrongdoing by KFC or by the customer beyond what the sources
  report.
- The tweet is shown only as reported wording on our own card, never as a replica of a social-media post.
