# Case File 36 — the 300 million yen robbery (Fuchu, Tokyo, 10 December 1968)
Research ledger, verified 2026-10-13. Episode id `tcv36-yen-heist` (short cut, 44–52 s, "Danger Zones" series).

## Method and limits
- Web search extracts only (12 searches, "standard" mode). WebFetch/curl are blocked, so no article was read in full; every
  claim below rests on at least two search-result extracts. Japanese-language extracts (Bunshun, Gendai) were used to
  cross-check details the English sources leave out.
- Japan Times, NHK and Kyodo pieces did not surface in the search results; the reputable English sources found were the
  South China Morning Post (two pieces, 2012 and 2018, the 2012 one carrying AFP copy) and Wikipedia, plus Unseen Japan
  (a Japan-focused publication), Japanese Nostalgic Car, and Shukan Bunshun (bunshun.jp) in Japanese.

## Fun fact (one card)
- **The Toshiba workers still got their bonuses: the money was insured.** Unseen Japan ("Toshiba's losses were covered by
  insurance, and its employees received the end-of-year bonuses they were promised"); Shukan Bunshun (bunshun.jp/articles/-/87093,
  round-table: the bonuses were paid again the day after the robbery; the money was insured). "The next day" rests on
  Bunshun alone, so the card says only that they still got their bonuses.

## Lesson (one card)
- **A uniform isn't proof: ask for ID, or call the agency on a number you look up yourself.** Hawaii Police Department
  (via Spectrum News, Jan 2025 / Nov 2024: legitimate officers will readily give ID details; contact the agency using
  publicly available contact information); New Westminster Police (never call back on a number the caller gives).

## Allegations / inquiry findings
- None used. A 19-year-old son of a serving officer was a suspect, died by suicide, and was ruled out; later "suspects"
  and confessions in books are unproven. **No suspect is named or hinted at.** The robber is shown only as a helmeted
  figure with the visor down, in silhouette.

## Myths and claims NOT used
- "In three minutes": not found in any extract (English or Japanese). Not said.
- Inflation-adjusted values (sources disagree: ¥2–3 bn vs ¥30 bn). Not used.
- "Exactly 300 million": the true figure is ¥294,307,500; narration says "two hundred ninety-four million", the
  headline says "NEARLY ¥300 MILLION", the counter shows ¥294,307,500.
- Theories about the robber's identity, the 1985 "case closed" line in one extract (the limitation date is 1975).
- Rain on the morning (Bunshun only): used as atmosphere in the picture, not narrated.

## Ethics decisions
- Nobody was hurt; the flare is shown as pink smoke with a small flicker, no explosion, no injury, no weapon.
- The robber is never identified: helmet, visor down, silhouette. Bank staff and detectives are generic Japanese 1960s
  figures (not likenesses). The motorcycle, car, bank and road are generic drawings with no insignia, logos, plates or
  real police markings. The Toshiba name is spoken and typed only; no logo.
- No stereotyping: the "danger" angle is a contrast ("one of the safest countries on Earth" vs one brazen trick).
