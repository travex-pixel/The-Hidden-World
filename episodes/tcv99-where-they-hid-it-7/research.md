# Case File 99 — A Horse Saddle Stuffed With Cocaine (WHERE THEY HID IT · 8)

Research ledger, built 24 October 2026. Engine v9, lane: smuggling. Series rule for this one: everyday things that were
cut open with drugs inside. Every case is a seizure by a named agency, so each is a settled fact (the drugs exist and
were found); nobody arrested is named, and the people drawn at all are silhouettes.

## Method and limits

- Web search only (standard and extended modes), about 40 queries in English and Spanish, 24 Oct 2026. Full pages could
  not be opened from the build environment (WebFetch: DNS failure; the egress proxy refuses direct fetches), so every
  claim below rests on **search-result extracts**, cross-checked between at least two independent outlets.
- Access date for every URL: 2026-10-24.
- Where outlets disagree on a number (the saddle's weight, the Montreal weight), the reel uses only what two of them
  agree on, or rounds down ("over 5 kilos", "about 2 kg").

## Ethics decisions

- The hook states a seizure (a fact), agent-first with a making verb: "Smugglers stuffed cocaine in a horse saddle."
  "Smugglers" refers to the scheme the Guardia Civil seized, not to a named person.
- Everyone arrested is "arrested" or "charged", never guilty; never named; never given a face. The three arrested in
  Spain are not drawn at all; the Montreal traveller and the two Auckland men are dark rim-lit silhouettes (NOT A
  LIKENESS). "Police say" carries the allegation that one was told the parcel was not theirs.
- Officers are generic figures without insignia; no weapons; no force (the airport arrest is an officer stepping into
  the path).
- No logos, airlines, plates or real interfaces: the X-ray image is our own false-colour drawing; the departures board
  shows only HAWAII and dashes; the parcel label is blank lines.
- The joke is on the hiding places (a saddle in the post, pumpkins, a jewelled horse head), never on a person.
