## Which wall we are hitting

Not the scroll-stop: every reel reaches 1,000–4,000 strangers from the Reels tab, 70 % are still there at 4 s and
the 28-second cuts hold people for 57 % of their length. Not the stay, either: 30–40 % reach the end on the recent
reels, and 98 shows 1.58 views per viewer (replays).

**The wall is the share.** Instagram spreads a Reel on sends and saves, and this account produces almost none: 0 shares on
24 of 29 reels, 0 saves on 18 of 30, 0 follows. The plateau at 2,000–4,500 is exactly what a reel gets when skip rate
alone carries it through the first two distribution tiers and nothing carries it further.

The two exceptions say what a shareable one looks like. **45** (an 87-year-old war hero caught hauling cartel cocaine:
13 shares, 6 saves, 4 comments, like rate 1.7 %, 2,846 views at 5 h on a steep curve) and **37** (a plane full of cocaine
crashed in the Papua New Guinea jungle: 11 shares, the only follow). Both are **one person, one story, told start to
finish**, with a human reason to send it ("look at this grandad", "this is unbelievable"). Every reel since 63 has been a
three-case list about objects (tunnels, melons, jackets); the lists win the scroll-stop and lose the send.

## Ranking: what worked and what did not

**Top three by views:** 73 fake Border Patrol truck (4,054), 92 drug cannon van (2,980), 63 Jeep on the fence (2,920).
**Top two by sends:** 45 and 37. **Bottom three:** 87 Uber getaway (157), 84 Instagram burglar (184), 85 sleeping burglar
(195), all posted in the same hour as 86 (1,950) and 88 (2,214), so posting time is not the difference.

| | Top (73, 92, 63, 45, 37) | Bottom (84, 85, 87) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook, first spoken words | an agent doing something, stated as fact: "Smugglers made/built/drove…", "At 87, he…" | "Police say he/a burglar…": a news attribution before the story |
| Subject | smuggling and cartels (border devices, cocaine in motion); an unlikely person | ordinary burglars and getaways |
| Length | 27–39 s (37 at 49 s is the outlier, carried by sends) | 31 s: length is not it |
| Frame 0 / cover | the object mid-act (truck behind bars, cannon through the roof, Jeep on the fence, a plane nosed into grass) | an ordinary picture whose wrongness is only in the text (man on a sofa, sleeper in a bed, taxi outside a bank) |
| Posting | same afternoon slot (~16:00 AEDT) as the losers | same slot |
| Caption | not measured by Insights; comments 2–4 whatever the caption or seed line | 2–3 |

Three rules survive every batch: the first spoken words name the doer and the deed; the lane is smuggling; frame 0 is
the object mid-act with the inside still hidden (97: 17 % skip, 65 % at 10 s). What no batch has fixed: **the send**.

## What is not in the data (and matters)

- **Who watches.** No Audience screen with countries, cities, age or gender was ever captured (two were cut off). The
  posting slot has been ~16:00 Sydney, which is 01:00 New York and 06:00 London; the audience's time zone is unknown.
- **The voice.** The owner reports the narration sounds robotic (Kokoro `am_adam`); Insights cannot see this, but
  nothing has been sent, saved or followed since the voice change either way, so it is untested, not exonerated.
- Reels 89–91 (reported flops), 46, 48–58 except 50, 64–71 and 80–82 have no screenshot; 99 and 100 are unposted.

## Honest summary

The engine reliably makes a 28-second reel that 1,000–3,000 strangers stop for and half of them watch to the end. It has
never made one that people send to each other, and that is the only thing between 2,500 and 10,000 views. The two reels
that were shared were stories about a person; the last 35 reels have been lists about objects.
