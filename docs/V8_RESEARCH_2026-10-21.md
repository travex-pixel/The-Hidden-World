# What gets a reel past the hour-1 seed: the 18-reel table (21 October 2026)

Nine of the last twelve reels failed Instagram's hour-1 seed test (150–250 viewers, then a decision on skip rate).
Engine v6 fixed the middle of the video (kept-at-10-s rose from 42–52 % to 45–55 % on the reels that were pushed),
and v7 fixed the headline's first line, but neither touched what decides the seed. This note takes every reel we have
a skip rate for, plus the three v7 flops, and asks what the pushed ones share.

## What does not separate them

- **Frame-0 brightness and colour.** The best reel ever (63) has a dark, colourless frame; 87 and 85 are bright and
  colourful and flopped. The v7 "impossible picture" rule was applied to 89–91 and all three flopped anyway.
- **A number in the hook.** Present in winners and losers alike.
- **Posting time or batching.** 86, 87 and 88 went up within minutes of each other and split 21 % / 40 % / 24 %.
- **Length.** The 28-second cut (88) is the best reel at 7 h, but 89–91 were also 28 s and flopped.

## What does

1. **The first spoken words.** Hooks that open with an agent doing something, stated as fact ("Smugglers built…",
   "Thieves tunnelled…", "Customs checked…", "A fake ambulance. Full of cocaine."): **10 of 10 pushed**. Hooks that
   open with "Police say…" or a question: **2 of 8 pushed**, and the two exceptions had a viral-famous image (the
   watermelon heads) or a universal mistake (butt-dialing 911). v7 moved "POLICE SAY" off the headline's first line but
   explicitly allowed it as the first spoken words; sound-on viewers heard a news bulletin.
2. **The lane.** Smuggling, border and drug busts: **8 of 9 pushed** (the miss, 89, also opened on "Police say" and a
   white cast on a grey bench). Wine heists: 2 of 2, borderline. Generic burglar and getaway stories: **2 of 7**. The
   seed audience is the audience the account trained on; trade reporting on the 2026 ranking agrees that an account
   that pivots between categories dilutes its classification, and the last nine reels pivoted four times (Dumbest
   Getaways, Caught By Their Own Phone, They Stole What?, Disguise Fails).

The two rules overlap almost perfectly because an accused-person story forces a "police say" hook, and the smuggling
lane is built on seizures, which are facts no matter who is convicted. That is also why v8 keeps the fairness rule
intact: the hook states the seizure, the people come later as "arrested" or "convicted".

## External reporting (secondary sources, used only where they agree with our data)

- Ranking signals Mosseri named in January 2025: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach, with sends weighted more
  for reaching non-followers ([dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers),
  [highstyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026), [clixie](https://www.clixie.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm)).
  Our sends are 0 on every reel; nothing in the seed test depends on them, but nothing past the seed will scale
  without them.
- The seed-then-expand model: a reel goes to a test pool of a few hundred accounts and expands on early behaviour,
  measured within about an hour ([socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm),
  [kometmedia](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/instagram-reels-algorithm-and-reach-explained)); creators who pivot
  between categories dilute their classification ([macetric](https://macetric.com/how-instagram-ranks-reels-creators/)).
  This matches the flat-from-hour-1 curves on 84, 85, 87.
- Trial Reels (shown to non-followers only, then promoted if they do well) would be the right tool to test hooks, but
  reports put the floor at 200–1,000 followers ([socialchamp](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels/),
  [metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-trial-reels/)); the account has about 20, so not yet.
- Posting frequency: a Buffer figure reported second-hand says 5+ reels a week got up to 2.1× the reach of under 2
  ([creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm/)); no study isolates several-in-one-day. One a day in
  the slot that pushed 86 and 88 stays the rule.

## Engine v8 (enforced in pipeline/engage_qa.py for engagement_version 8)

- `hook_agent_first`: the first spoken line does not open with "police/prosecutors/… say", a pronoun, "this" or a
  year, and its first sentence is not a question.
- `hook_in_lane`: the first spoken line contains a lane word (smugglers, cocaine, border, customs, tunnel, heist…).
- `lane_locked`: the episode declares `lane` from {smuggling, border, customs, heist, bust}.
- Unchanged from v6/v7: payoff by 9 s, a gotcha per case, callback, loop, 28-second cut, object-first headline, the
  cover-the-text check on frame 0 (kept as a floor, no longer the theory).
- Content rule: the headline case is a seizure or a conviction, so the hook is a fact; accused people appear only in
  later lines, as "arrested" with "police say".

## What would prove or refute it

Three v8 reels in the proven lane, one per day in the same slot. If all three clear 30 % skip at 6 h, the lane and
hook rules are confirmed and the next step is sends (the one signal still at zero). If one fails, its hour-1 numbers
and frame are the next thing to read.
