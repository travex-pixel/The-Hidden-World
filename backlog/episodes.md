Candidate subjects, not approved scripts. Each needs a research record (`episodes/<id>/research.md`) and claim-level fact check before production.

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each; a guide for choosing, not a prediction of virality)

| Criterion | What 5 looks like |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Hook | The opening question stops a scrolling thumb in under 3 seconds |
| Evidence | Primary sources (standards, investigation reports, papers) exist and are accessible |
| Visual | The mechanism can be *drawn*: pipes, networks, scales, cascades |
| Clarity | Explainable accurately in ~60 seconds without hand-waving |
| Reveal | There is a genuine "oh — *that's* why" moment |
| Misread risk (inverse) | Low chance viewers walk away with a wrong simplification |
| Production | Fits the existing scene kit; few new rigs needed |
| Distinct | Not a re-tread of an existing episode |

Priority = sum. Ties broken by evidence quality.

## Lessons from episodes 001–002
- Episode 001 felt slow: an abstract concept (accounting) with a fictional company has low stakes. Episode 002 uses a **real event with a vanishing act, a culprit and a twist**. Prefer topics where something real happened, went wrong or surprised experts.
- Lead with the event, not the concept. Name the stakes in the first sentence.
